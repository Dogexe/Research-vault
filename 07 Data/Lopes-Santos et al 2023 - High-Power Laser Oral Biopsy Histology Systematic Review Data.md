## Whether the review distinguishes preset/set from measured/delivered power

- FACT: The review's own Data collection process (Methods) lists "protocol used for the high-power lasers (power, wavelength, wave mode, and optical fibers)" as an extracted data item, with no separate item for independently measured or calibrated output. Every power value reported in Table 1/Table 2 and the narrative text is a single number per study/arm ("strength = X W"), with no indication of whether it is a console/panel setting or an independently verified value, and no mention anywhere in the review of a power meter, calibration protocol, or measured-vs-set distinction.
- FACT: This absence is not flagged by the review itself as a limitation — set/measured power distinction is not raised in the review's own Discussion, Limitations, or Conclusion.

## Whether incision speed was reported

- FACT: Incision speed (or any handpiece-movement-rate value) is not mentioned anywhere in the review's Methods, Results, Table 1/Table 2, Discussion, or Limitations, for any of the 7 included studies (diode or otherwise).

## Whether tip initiation, fiber diameter, CW/PW, pulse duration, duty cycle, contact mode were reported

- Tip initiation status: not mentioned anywhere in the review, for any included study.
- Fiber diameter: reported for 2 of 4 diode studies (Amaral 600 μm; Monteiro 300 μm); UNKNOWN for Bhatsange and Gill.
- CW/PW: reported for 3 of 4 diode studies (Amaral CW; Bhatsange CW; Gill 808 nm arm CW, 980 nm arm pulsed). The review's own text explicitly flags the fourth (Monteiro) as not reporting wave mode: "One article did not report the wave mode used [3]."
- Pulse duration: not reported as a discrete value for any diode study; Gill's 980 nm arm has only the ambiguous "pulsed mode at 50 ms intervals" descriptor, which the review does not itself classify as pulse width, period, or duty-cycle-equivalent.
- Duty cycle: not reported for any diode study. (The review does report Hz for the two Nd:YAG arms in Vescovi et al. 2010 — 60 Hz and 30 Hz — showing the review is capable of extracting frequency where the source study reports it; it simply was not available to extract for the diode studies.)
- Contact mode: not mentioned anywhere in the review, for any included study of any laser type.

## How thermal artifact / margin quality / diagnostic quality were defined

- FACT: **Thermal artifact** is operationalized two ways in this review: (1) a binary "thermal damage: Yes/No" per study in Table 2, and (2) for two studies only (Vescovi's Nd:YAG arms and Gill's two diode arms), a quantified mean linear measurement in μm. The review's own summary statement is that thermal artifacts were "present in 80–100% of cases, measuring 17.9–72.6 μm" across all 7 studies — this range is driven by Gill's diode values (17.92, 3.80 μm — note 3.80 μm falls below the review's own stated 17.9 μm floor, an unresolved internal arithmetic inconsistency in the review's own summary sentence) and other lasers' higher values.
- FACT: **Margin/incision-quality** is operationalized as the "incision damage morphology" score, 0–4, where the review's own Methods state 0 = worst quality and 4 = highest quality. This score was reported for only 3 of 7 studies (Vescovi, Monteiro, Bhatsange — the last "not defined" despite being marked "Yes"); it was not reported for Amaral, Tuncer, Suter, or Gill.
- FACT: **Diagnostic quality** is operationalized as a binary "histopathological diagnosis affected: Yes/No/N/A" per study — a qualitative judgment of whether laser use changed the pathologist's ability to render a diagnosis, not a quantified diagnostic-yield percentage or unreadable-specimen rate. Of the 4 diode studies, only Amaral and Monteiro address this comparison at all (both "No"); Bhatsange and Gill do not report this comparison ("N/A").
- INTERPRETATION: None of the review's three outcome operationalizations (thermal damage Yes/No or μm; incision score 0–4; diagnosis affected Yes/No) is a quantified diagnostic-yield percentage of the kind already established as this vault's strongest biopsy-quality metric (≥4–5 mm specimen-size threshold in [[06 Synthesis/Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Quality]]). The review does not report specimen size or margin distance as a variable at all.

## Limitations identified by the review (verbatim / closely paraphrased)

- Verbatim (Discussion): "This systematic review has limitations, including the methodological heterogeneity of the selected studies, the diversity of the high-power laser devices, and the many possibilities of different clinical protocols used in various types of oral lesions. Another important limitation was the restriction of our eligibility criteria: The necessity of using cold scalpel surgeries as a control group drastically restricted our sample."
- Verbatim (Abstract): "Due to the heterogeneity of the data, a meta-analysis was not performed."
- Verbatim (Results/Discussion): "The certainty of the evidence was classified as low due to the risk of bias and imprecision of the articles evaluated in this systematic review."
- Verbatim (Discussion, on cross-study comparability specifically): "It was extremely difficult to compare the wavelength and power of different lasers in this review, mainly because the various studies discussed different types of high-power laser devices."

## Future research recommendations

- FACT: **The review does not contain a dedicated "future research directions" section.** No heading or paragraph is framed as a research agenda. The closest statements are clinical-practice recommendations embedded in the Discussion and Conclusion, plus one general methodological observation:
  - Verbatim (Discussion): "An important consideration is that the variety of high-power laser devices and clinical protocols now available may comprise a major limitation to establishing the best standard protocols for different oral soft tissue lesions."
  - Verbatim (Discussion, attributed to a cited external source, Gundlapalle et al. — not the review's own independent finding): "It is recommended that a device be used in pulsed wave mode accompanied by air suction and water coolants to reduce damage to oral tissue."
  - Verbatim (Conclusion): "The eligibility criteria and adequate indications for high-power lasers in different oral soft tissue lesion treatments must be respected to avoid tissue artifacts that impair precise histopathological diagnosis. Additionally, the prior knowledge, by pathologist, of high-power laser devices use in oral biopsies and the possible cytological artifacts or tissue architecture damage that could result from their use is essential to prevent histopathological misdiagnosis and, consequently, inadequate patient's treatment."
- INTERPRETATION: These are calls for clinician awareness/practice and for "standard protocols" in general terms, not a specific, itemized future-research agenda (e.g., no explicit call for standardized power-meter verification, incision-speed reporting, or tip-initiation reporting).

## Explicit statement(s) about inconsistent parameter reporting / lack of standardization

- FACT (clearest, most specific instance): "One article did not report the wave mode used [3]" — the review explicitly flags one included diode-adjacent study (Monteiro et al. 2019) for missing a specific operating parameter. This is the review's only instance of naming a *specific* missing parameter in a *specific* study, rather than describing heterogeneity in general terms.
- FACT (general framing): "It was extremely difficult to compare the wavelength and power of different lasers in this review, mainly because the various studies discussed different types of high-power laser devices" and "the variety of high-power laser devices and clinical protocols now available may comprise a major limitation to establishing the best standard protocols for different oral soft tissue lesions" (both quoted in full above).
- INTERPRETATION: The review frames this issue as **device/protocol diversity impairing cross-study comparison**, not as a **measurement-verification or reporting-completeness** problem. It never raises — as a limitation, in its Discussion, or anywhere else — the absence of power-meter verification, incision-speed reporting, tip-initiation reporting, or duty-cycle reporting as gaps in the underlying primary-study literature. Its "standardization" concern is about clinical protocol choice (which device/wavelength/power/mode to use for which lesion), not about how completely or verifiably operating parameters are documented in publications. This distinction matters for comparing the review's gaps against this vault's own — see [[06 Synthesis/Novelty Check - Prior Systematic Review]].

## Notes

- FACT: Risk-of-bias domains for the diode-containing studies specifically: Amaral et al. (randomization low risk, allocation concealment low risk, detection bias low risk); Bhatsange et al. (random sequence generation high risk — not randomized; allocation concealment high risk — method not explained; detection bias unclear); Monteiro et al. (randomization unclear — not specified as randomized; allocation concealment unclear; detection bias low risk); Gill et al. (detection bias unclear; randomization and allocation concealment not individually reported in the retrieved text for this study).
- FACT: The review's own reviewer-agreement (kappa) was 0.97 (title/abstract) and 1.0 (full-text) — high agreement on study selection, not on data extraction accuracy per se.
- FACT (cross-reference to this vault): The review's Discussion cites, as reference [36], a study on pulsed-mode reducing tissue damage: "which was confirmed by Gundlapalle et al. [36]." This is **[[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]]**, already processed in this vault. Gundlapalle et al. 2022 was **not** one of the review's own 7 included/analyzed studies (it postdates the review's July 2022 search cutoff) — it is cited only as external Discussion support, not as data contributing to the review's own synthesis or Table 2. The review's within-review diode evidence for CW vs. PW is limited to Gill et al.'s two arms, which confound wavelength (808 vs. 980 nm) and power (2.5 vs. 5 W) with mode simultaneously — the same type of confound this vault has already identified and excluded as unusable CW-vs-PW evidence in Romeo et al. 2014 (diode-CW vs. KTP-PW, confounded by wavelength/laser type) — see [[06 Synthesis/Contradictions in Diode Laser Biopsy Evidence]].
- FACT: Two internal numeric inconsistencies within the review's own text/table are preserved above rather than resolved, per this vault's standing convention of not silently correcting source-level arithmetic conflicts (as already done for Zegaib et al. 2011 and Romanos et al. 2022 in this vault): (1) Gill et al.'s epithelial-cleft percentage stated as "75.4%" in narrative text vs. "71.4%" in the review's own Table 2 (75/105 = 71.4%, arithmetically consistent with the table, not the narrative sentence); (2) Gill et al.'s diode wavelength labeled "880 nm" in Table 2's thermal-damage row vs. "808 nm" everywhere else in the review (Methods, Table 1, narrative Results) for the same study arm.
- HYPOTHESIS: None proposed independently by this extraction; the review itself proposes no testable hypothesis beyond its narrative conclusions.
- Classification for this vault: **background/context source** — a general oral-lesion (multi-laser-type) systematic review, not a diode-specific or power-parameter-focused source. It contributes no new primary diode-biopsy data beyond what four of its cited studies (Amaral, Bhatsange, Monteiro, Gill) already report, none of which overlaps with sources already in this vault's core biopsy evidence. Its chief value to this vault is as an independent check on this vault's own scope and gap analysis — see [[06 Synthesis/Novelty Check - Prior Systematic Review]].
