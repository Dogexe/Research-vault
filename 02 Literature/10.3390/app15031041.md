## Paper Summary

### Citation

Liu, Z.; Lu, Y.; Lange, B.; Meister, J. "Technical Characterization of a High-Power Diode Laser at 445 nm for Medical Applications: From Continuous Wave Down to Pulse Durations in the µs-Range." *Applied Sciences* 2025, 15(3), 1041. DOI: [10.3390/app15031041](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15031041).

### Study type

Ex vivo experimental study (technical/engineering characterization, with a tissue-cutting validation component).

### Research question / purpose

To characterize the achievable output-power, pulse-duration, and duty-cycle envelope of a 445 nm high-power diode laser system (CW down to µs-range pulses) and to relate a subset of those settings to cutting depth and thermal-damage-zone width in ex vivo porcine tissue.

### Study design

Bench characterization of laser output (LabMax Top + PM10 power meter, Coherent) across CW and pulsed modes (duty cycles 1–90%, pulse durations 1 µs–900 ms), followed by 380 standardized cuts (average power 2–8 W, pulse durations 10 µs/100 µs/1 ms plus CW) on **porcine ear tissue** — not oral tissue; the tissue model was chosen for the paper's stated otorhinolaryngology (ENT) application context, using the same senior author's (J. Meister) histological thermal-damage-zone evaluation protocol as [[07 Data/Hanke et al 2021 - Eight-Wavelength Diode Cut Efficiency Data|Hanke et al. 2021]] (cited directly as ref. [20]). Cuts were made in contact mode with a 375/405 µm fiber, mounted on a computer-controlled 3D micropositioner (VT-80, Micos — the same device family used by Hanke 2021/Strakas 2023), at a fixed cut speed of 3 mm/s. See [[07 Data/Liu et al 2025 - High-Power 445nm Diode Technical Characterization Data]] for the full extraction.

### Main findings

- Measured maximum CW output was 12.7 W, against a manufacturer/system nominal rating of 13.5 W (~6% below spec) — within the acceptable ±20% tolerance of IEC 60601-2-22, cited by the authors themselves.
- Peak pulse powers up to 34.5 W were achieved for 5–10 µs pulses (roughly 3× the average power).
- Cutting depth increased approximately linearly with average power, reaching up to 1 mm for 100 µs pulses (various duty cycles) and CW mode; pulse-duration/duty-cycle regime did not produce a statistically distinguishable cutting depth from CW at the sample sizes tested.
- Thermal-damage-zone width was 200–350 µm across all tested settings, with a non-significant trend toward a steeper (deeper) increase for CW at average power > 4 W.
- Cut speed (3 mm/s) and contact mode were held fixed across every condition — speed was a standardized experimental control, not a tested variable.

### What this paper supports

A device-level, independently power-meter-verified characterization of a 445 nm diode laser's achievable power/pulse-duration/duty-cycle combinations, and a demonstrated (if modest) gap between measured maximum CW output and the system's nominal/manufacturer-stated rating, using the same measurement lineage (LabMax Top + PM10, Coherent) as this vault's Hanke 2021/Strakas 2023 prior art. It also supports a general power-density-to-thermal-damage-zone relationship in ex vivo tissue at a fixed cut speed.

### What this paper does NOT support

- **Not oral-tissue evidence.** The tissue model is porcine **ear**, explicitly chosen for an ENT/otorhinolaryngology application context — not oral mucosa, gingiva, or tongue. It should not be cited as oral soft-tissue evidence for this vault's biopsy project without this caveat.
- **Not a speed-effect experiment.** Cut speed (3 mm/s) was fixed and mechanized across every single condition — it was never varied as an independent variable. Do not cite this paper as evidence that speed/dwell affects thermal damage; it cannot speak to that question by design.
- **Not preset evidence.** No manufacturer-named clinical preset is tested, referenced, or implied anywhere in the paper — the tested power/pulse/duty-cycle combinations are researcher-selected bench parameters for system characterization, not a recalled device preset. The measured-vs-nominal gap (12.7 W vs. 13.5 W) concerns a system's rated maximum output capability, not a set-power-vs-measured-output comparison for a clinical parameter setting.
- **Not a biopsy or specimen-quality outcome.** The only histologic outcomes are cutting depth and thermal-damage-zone width (a technical/mechanistic pair), not margin readability, tissue-architecture preservation, or any diagnostic-adequacy judgment.
- Not human tissue (porcine).

### Key limitations

Limited sample size per parameter combination (five repeats per condition) is explicitly acknowledged by the authors as insufficient to confirm the CW-vs-pulsed thermal-damage-zone trend they flag as needing further investigation. Several results/figure sections of the source PDF extracted with column-reflow/duplication artifacts in automated text extraction; numeric values used here were cross-checked against the abstract and prose statements, but a human should visually re-confirm Tables 1–3 and Figures 6–13 against the original PDF.

### Relevance to current project

Contributes to the **console/manufacturer-rating → actual delivered power** link in general (a measured-vs-nominal-spec gap, using the vault's established measurement lineage) and to the **actual delivered power → tissue interaction** link (power/pulse-duration/duty-cycle → cutting depth and thermal-damage-zone width) — but at a fixed, non-oral, non-clinical-preset setting. Does not touch the **incision speed/movement** link (speed fixed, not varied) or the **histopathologic specimen quality** link (no biopsy-oriented or margin/architecture outcome).

### Role in the project

- Methodological support (shares equipment/protocol lineage with Hanke 2021/Strakas 2023)
- Delivered-power evidence (device-rating-vs-measured-output angle only)
- Contextual evidence only (non-oral tissue, non-preset)

### One-line takeaway

Liu et al. 2025 rigorously characterizes a 445 nm diode laser's power/pulse/duty-cycle envelope and its relation to cutting depth and thermal-damage-zone width — using the same measurement lineage as this vault's Hanke/Strakas prior art — but on non-oral (porcine ear) tissue, at a fixed (not varied) cut speed, and without any manufacturer preset or biopsy-oriented outcome.

### สรุปภาษาไทย

พบว่ากำลังขาออกสูงสุดที่วัดได้จริงในโหมด CW คือ 12.7 W ต่ำกว่าค่าที่ระบุของระบบ (13.5 W) ประมาณ 6% ซึ่งยังอยู่ในเกณฑ์ยอมรับได้ตามมาตรฐาน IEC งานนี้ไม่เกี่ยวข้องกับค่าพรีเซ็ตทางคลินิกใดๆ ไม่ได้ทดสอบผลของความเร็ว/ระยะเวลาสัมผัส และไม่มีการประเมินคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแบบ biopsy จึงไม่ควรใช้เป็นหลักฐานสนับสนุนประเด็นความเร็ว-ผลต่อเนื้อเยื่อ หรือความน่าเชื่อถือของพรีเซ็ตของโครงการนี้โดยตรง
