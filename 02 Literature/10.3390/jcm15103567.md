## Paper Summary

### Citation

Mazzoni, A.C.; Vieira, A.M.; Mandetta, A.R.H.; Mesquita-Ferrari, R.A.; Navarro, R.S.; Sobral, A.P.T.; Labat Marcos, R.; Bussadori, S.K.; Motta, L.J. "The Thermal and Histological Evaluation of Lingual Frenulum Incision Using Diode Lasers in an Experimental Model." *Journal of Clinical Medicine* 2026, 15(10), 3567. DOI: [10.3390/jcm15103567](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15103567).

### Study type

Two-phase experimental, controlled, comparative study: Phase 1 ex vivo (porcine tongue, thermal-only screening); Phase 2 in vivo (Wistar rat, thermal + histological outcome).

### Research question / purpose

Whether lingual frenulum incision with 450 nm (blue) and 980 nm (infrared) high-power diode lasers produces different intraoperative thermal behavior and subsequent histological tissue-repair effects than a surgical-scissors control.

### Study design

**Phase 1 (ex vivo, screening only):** 60 porcine tongues, 12 instrument/parameter groups (Iris scissors, electrocautery, electric scalpel, an Er:YAG comparator, and several diode power/mode combinations at 450 nm and 980 nm), incisions in the lingual-frenulum region, temperature recorded by infrared thermography (FLIR C5). This phase is **thermal-only — no histology was performed in Phase 1**, and its purpose was solely to select the instruments/parameters carried into Phase 2.

**Phase 2 (in vivo, primary outcome):** 30 prepubescent Wistar rats, 3 groups (n=10 each): Iris scissors (control), 980 nm diode (1.3 W, 20 Hz, pulsed), 450 nm diode (1.5 W, 20 Hz, pulsed). Standardized ~1 mm × 1 mm incisions in the lingual frenulum under general + local anesthesia. Thermography during incision; histology (H&E for inflammatory infiltrate, Masson's trichrome for collagen organization) on tissue collected immediately (day 0, n=5/group, euthanized) and at day 7 (n=5/group, euthanized). No incision speed, movement rate, or dwell time is reported anywhere in either phase — incisions are described only by their final dimensions, not by a rate of movement. No power-meter or other independent verification of the diode output is described; the power/frequency values used are the devices' set/console values. See [[07 Data/Mazzoni et al 2026 - Diode Laser Lingual Frenulum Thermal Histology Data]] for the full extraction.

### Main findings

- In the ex vivo screening phase, no instrument/setting raised local tissue temperature above 40–42 °C — the threshold the authors cite as the point where protein denaturation/irreversible coagulation begins — under the tested conditions; scissors showed the lowest median warming (5.9 °C), diode lasers 8.4–14.0 °C.
- In vivo, both 450 nm and 980 nm diode lasers produced significantly greater temperature increases than scissors; the 450 nm laser showed a higher initial peak but faster cooling than the 980 nm laser.
- Histologically, at day 0 the 450 nm group showed a larger immediate zone of thermal coagulation than the 980 nm group; by day 7, the 450 nm group showed **less** collagen-fiber disorganization and more organized granulation tissue than the 980 nm group, with a healing pattern the authors describe as closer to the scissors control.
- The scissors group had the least thermal effect but the most intraoperative bleeding (excessive bleeding in 4/10 animals) and no hemostatic effect.

### What this paper supports

A direct, wavelength-controlled (450 nm vs. 980 nm) comparison of intraoperative thermal behavior and downstream (day 0 and day 7) histologic wound-healing/repair effect for diode-laser incision of oral (lingual frenulum) tissue in vivo, plus a stated safety-threshold framing (40–42 °C) against which the tested settings did not exceed injury-level heating in the ex vivo screening phase.

### What this paper does NOT support

- **Not incision-speed or dwell-time evidence.** No numeric speed, movement rate, or dwell time is reported anywhere in either phase — not even as a fixed/controlled value. This paper cannot be cited for or against a speed/dwell → thermal-damage relationship; it simply does not address the variable at all.
- **Not independently measured power.** The Methods fully describe the device settings used (set/console W and Hz values) without describing any power-meter verification step — per this vault's schema convention, a complete Methods section that never mentions verification is recorded as `measured_power: false`, not `null`.
- **Not biopsy-specimen-quality evidence in this vault's operational sense.** The histologic outcome is a wound-healing/tissue-repair judgment (inflammatory infiltrate, collagen organization, fibrosis, tracked over 7 days) — not a margin-readability, tissue-architecture-preservation-for-diagnosis, or specimen-interpretability score. **Do not describe this paper's thermal-damage or histology findings as "specimen quality"** — it measures therapeutic healing outcome, not diagnostic specimen adequacy.
- **Not human tissue.** Ex vivo phase is porcine; in vivo phase is Wistar rat — an animal ankyloglossia-surgery model, not human tissue or a human clinical trial.
- **Not a manufacturer-preset test.** The diode power/frequency settings (1.3 W/20 Hz; 1.5 W/20 Hz) are researcher-selected treatment parameters chosen from a wider ex vivo screening set — the paper does not name, cite, or test a manufacturer-labeled clinical preset.
- **Not a biopsy-oriented or diagnostic design.** No lesion is excised and no diagnosis is rendered; this is a therapeutic incision (ankyloglossia release) and healing-outcome study.

### Key limitations

No a priori sample-size calculation; exploratory design; short (7-day) follow-up; animal model (rat) biological variability may not generalize to human infant frenulum tissue; ex vivo Phase 1 Table 1/Table 2 device-parameter tables extracted from the source PDF with column-reflow artifacts in automated text extraction, cross-checked against prose where possible.

### Relevance to current project

Adds oral-tissue (lingual frenulum), wavelength- and power-linked thermal and histologic outcome data to the **actual delivered power → tissue interaction → thermal/structural alteration** part of the project's logic chain — but the outcome is a therapeutic wound-healing endpoint, not the project's biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome (margin readability, tissue-architecture preservation for diagnostic purposes), and it contributes nothing to the **incision speed/movement** link, which this paper does not address at all. It also does not touch the **preset** link, since no named manufacturer preset is tested.

### Role in the project

### One-line takeaway

Mazzoni et al. 2026 gives an oral-tissue, wavelength-controlled diode thermal-and-healing comparison (450 nm favorable over 980 nm at day 7) with an explicit safety-threshold framing, but it does not report incision speed at all, does not independently measure delivered power, and measures wound-healing quality rather than this vault's biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome.
