## Paper Summary

### Citation

Monteiro et al. 2019 — "A histological evaluation of the surgical margins from human oral fibrous-epithelial lesions excised with CO2 laser, Diode laser, Er:YAG laser, Nd:YAG laser, electrosurgical scalpel and cold scalpel." DOI: [10.4317/medoral.22819](https://doi.org/10.4317/medoral.22819). Full author list, journal name, volume/issue/pages: UNKNOWN (not recorded anywhere in this vault's notes — not invented here).

### Study type

Cross-sectional / retrospective observational study (real human specimens, single-center, instrument assigned per patient without a described randomization protocol).

### Research question / purpose

Whether six different excision instruments (CO2, diode, Er:YAG, Nd:YAG lasers, electrosurgical scalpel, cold scalpel) produce different margin thermal damage, tissue architecture alteration, and diagnostic adequacy when excising benign oral fibro-epithelial lesions.

### Study design

Retrospective, single-center (CESPU, Portugal, 2010–2018), n=130 real excised benign fibro-epithelial hyperplasias (of 142 originally, 12 excluded for incomplete data), one blinded pathologist, six instrument arms. Histologic evaluation criteria explicitly stated as adopted from Vescovi et al. 2010. See [[07 Data/Monteiro et al 2019 - Multi-Instrument Oral Fibro-Epithelial Margin Histology Data]] for the full six-arm parameter and outcome tables (diode arm: 980 nm, 3.5 W set power, 300 μm fiber, contact mode).

### Main findings

- The diode arm had the highest epithelial alteration score (2.95±0.22) and the worst (least regular) incision score (1.86±0.73) of all six instruments — the least favorable instrument on two of the three ordinal constructs tested.
- Diode's epithelial tissue-damage-extension (TDE) was the second-highest of six instruments (913.73 μm); connective TDE was mid-range (284.81 μm, 3rd of six).
- Lower incision-regularity scores correlated with greater TDE across the whole cohort (epithelial r=−0.438, connective r=−0.467, both p<0.001).
- No histological diagnosis was limited in any of the 130 cases, regardless of instrument used — a whole-cohort statement, not a per-case score.
- No correlation was found between specimen size and TDE or any categorical alteration variable in this study (contrasting with Angiero et al. 2011 and Vescovi et al. 2010, which found strong size-dependent effects) — the authors attribute this partly to all their specimens exceeding 4 mm.

### What this paper supports

- That, in this real six-instrument human comparison, the diode arm showed relatively poor thermal-damage and incision-regularity outcomes compared to most other instruments tested, while diagnosis was unimpaired in every case regardless of instrument.
- A genuine diagnostic-biopsy pathway (real diagnosis rendered on every specimen) linking a stated diode console setting to histologically scored margin/architecture outcomes.

### What this paper does NOT support

- No power was independently measured for any instrument (all values are manufacturer-recommended device settings, not power-meter-verified) — this is NOT delivered-power evidence.
- No incision speed is reported for any instrument — this does not close the speed/movement evidence gap.
- Instrument assignment is described as "random" without a described randomization protocol — weaker methodological control than a true RCT (contrast with Suter et al. 2017).
- "No limitation of diagnosis" is a whole-cohort statement, not an individually adjudicated per-case variable for every histologic finding — thermal damage and incision-regularity scores should not be read as equivalent to a diagnostic-outcome judgment.
- Margin condition (TDE, ordinal scores) is a distinct construct from whole-specimen diagnostic readability; this paper reports both but they should not be merged into one claim.

### Key limitations

- Retrospective design; instrument assignment method not fully described.
- Single blinded pathologist, no multi-rater reliability statistic.
- No independently measured power or incision speed for any arm.

### Relevance to current project

Contributes to the **console set power → tissue interaction → histopathologic specimen quality** links of the conceptual chain, specifically as a real multi-instrument (diode-inclusive) diagnostic-biopsy comparison. Does not reach the **actual delivered power** or **incision speed/movement** links (neither measured for any instrument).

### Role in the project

- Histopathologic outcome evidence
- Biopsy / specimen-quality evidence

### One-line takeaway

The vault's only real six-instrument human diagnostic-biopsy comparison including diode, in which diode showed comparatively poor thermal-damage and incision-regularity outcomes while diagnosis remained unimpaired in every case.
