# Literature Note

## Source

- Citation: Azevedo AS, Monteiro LS, Ferreira F, Delgado ML, Garcês F, Carreira S, Martins M, Suarez-Quintanilla J. "Histological evaluation of the surgical margins made by different laser wavelengths in tongue tissues." *J Clin Exp Dent.* 2016;8(4):e388-e396. DOI: [10.4317/jced.52830](https://doi.org/10.4317/jced.52830). PMID: 27703606. PMCID: PMC5045685.
- Source type: Original ex vivo comparative research article (porcine tongue model)
- Source link: https://doi.org/10.4317/jced.52830
- Source locator: Full text (Introduction; Material and Methods; Results; Discussion; Tables 1–4; Figures 1–2), retrieved via PubMed Central (According to PubMed).

## Study

- Objective: To determine the macroscopic and microscopic morphological changes at surgical margins in tongue tissue induced by different surgical instruments, including several laser types.
- Design: Ex vivo comparative experimental study; coded samples, double-blind histologic scoring by two pathologists reaching consensus.
- Sample or material: 10 pig cadaver tongues (24 h post-slaughter); 120 total incisions across six instrument/parameter groups (Er:YAG, CO2, diode, Nd:YAG, electroscalpel, cold scalpel); diode n=20 (16.7% of total).
- Clinical or laboratory context: Ex vivo bench model, not a human diagnostic-biopsy pathway — no pathologist rendered a diagnosis; the outcome measured is thermal/histologic tissue damage, not diagnostic yield.

## Device and operating parameters

- Device: Diode laser, LITEMEDICS®, 980 nm, contact mode, tested at two settings (3.5 W PW and 3.5 W Boost PW).
- Operating-parameter extraction: [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]

## Findings

- FACT: The diode laser at 3.5 W PW produced an Extent of Thermal Tissue Damage (ETTD) of 456.15 μm; at 3.5 W Boost PW, ETTD was 626.82 μm — the second-highest ETTD of all eight instrument/setting groups tested, after Nd:YAG (670.68 μm).
- FACT: A significant positive correlation was found between ETTD and macroscopic Degree of Carbonization across all instruments/settings pooled (r = 0.789, p = 0.01).
- FACT: A significant negative correlation was found between ETTD and Regularity of the Incision score, across all instruments/settings pooled (r = −0.299, p = 0.01).
- FACT: Significant differences in ETTD were found according to histologic tissue-change scores (nuclear, cytoplasmic, connective-tissue changes), each p < 0.001 — greater tissue-change scores were associated with higher ETTD.
- FACT: The diode laser was tested only in pulsed-wave (PW) mode in this study, at two power/intensity sub-settings ("PW" and "Boost PW"); no diode CW arm was included.
- INTERPRETATION (source's own): Carbonization degree may serve as a clinical indicator of the severity of thermal tissue damage; the diode's comparatively high ETTD is discussed alongside its wavelength's greater absorption by hemoglobin/melanin and consequent deeper tissue penetration relative to CO2 or Er:YAG.
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN — no formal hypothesis is stated beyond the study's comparative aim.

## Limitations

- Ex vivo porcine tongue, not human tissue and not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway; no diagnostic-yield outcome is reported.
- Diode power values are set/console values; no independent power-meter verification of delivered output is reported for this or any instrument in the study.
- The diode laser's own numeric carbonization-degree and incision-regularity scores were not reproduced in the retrieved full-text prose (Table 1/Table 4 cell values were not present in the extracted text) — marked UNKNOWN in the Data extraction rather than inferred from the pooled correlation statistics.
- Manufacturer city/country and fiber diameter for the diode arm specifically are not stated in the retrieved text (fiber diameter is given only for the Nd:YAG arm, 300 μm).
