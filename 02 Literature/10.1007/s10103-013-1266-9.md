## Paper Summary

### Citation

Seoane et al. 2013 — "Er,Cr:YSGG laser therapy for oral leukoplakia minimizes thermal artifacts on surgical margins: a pilot study." DOI: [10.1007/s10103-013-1266-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-013-1266-9). Full author list, journal name, volume/issue/pages: UNKNOWN (not recorded anywhere in this vault's notes — not invented here).

### Study type

Other (single-arm pilot case series; the source itself describes it as a "descriptive... cross-sectional analysis").

### Research question / purpose

Whether Er,Cr:YSGG laser excision of oral leukoplakia produces thermal artifacts at the surgical margin severe enough to be mistaken for genuine dysplastic changes, potentially interfering with histologic diagnosis.

### Study design

Single-arm pilot case series, n=4 human leukoplakia patients, complete surgical excision at the manufacturer's "3C preset" (recommended for soft tissue incisions, 2 W). Two pathologists, double-blinded, scored specimens until reaching consensus for each case — a stronger pathologist-involvement design than any diode-specific source in this vault. See [[07 Data/Seoane et al 2013 - Er,Cr YSGG Leukoplakia Pseudodysplastic Artifact Data]] for the full parameter table.

### Main findings

- Mean thermal damage width at the incision margin was 26.60±25.3 μm across the series (per-patient range 3.32–56.86 μm).
- "Pseudodysplastic" epithelial artifacts (cellular/nuclear polymorphism, hyperchromatism, loss of intercellular adherence) were present in 2 of 4 patients, described as low-intensity and confined to the basal/suprabasal epithelial layers.
- The paper's central interpretive conclusion is that this thermal-artifact profile "avoids diagnostic interferences with real dysplastic borders" — one of the four patients had a genuine histologic diagnosis of moderate dysplasia rendered alongside this assessment.
- No power was independently measured and no incision speed was reported.

### What this paper supports

- That a manufacturer-recommended device preset (the Waterlase "3C" soft-tissue-incision setting) was used in real leukoplakia excisions and paired with a blinded, two-pathologist-consensus histologic assessment of thermal artifact.
- The introduction and definition of the "pseudodysplastic artifact" construct as a named category of margin thermal artifact distinct from genuine dysplasia — new to this vault's terminology.

### What this paper does NOT support

- This is NOT diode-laser evidence — Er,Cr:YSGG is a distinct solid-state garnet laser system, kept in this vault as a non-diode methodological precedent only.
- No comparator arm exists (single-technique case series) — this paper alone cannot support any cross-laser or cross-instrument comparison.
- Very small sample (n=4, explicitly self-described as a "pilot study") sharply limits how far the "avoids diagnostic interferences" conclusion can be generalized.
- No independently measured delivered power (the manufacturer's stated output range is a device specification, not a power-meter verification) and no incision speed reported — this does not close this project's delivered-power or speed evidence gaps.
- "Avoids diagnostic interferences with real dysplastic borders" is the paper's own interpretive conclusion, not a per-case adjudicated diagnostic-outcome variable — specimen interpretability (artifact vs. genuine dysplasia) and a formally scored diagnostic-outcome judgment should not be conflated here.

### Key limitations

- n=4, single-arm, no comparator group.
- No independently measured power; no incision speed reported.
- No inter-rater reliability statistic reported for the two-pathologist consensus process (consensus reached, but not quantified).

### Relevance to current project

Contributes to the **manufacturer preset/recommendation → tissue interaction → histopathologic specimen quality** links of the conceptual chain, via a manufacturer-preset-attributed setting paired with a rigorous (blinded, consensus) histologic artifact assessment. Does not reach the **console set power → actual delivered power** or **incision speed/movement** links (neither measured).

### Role in the project

- Preset / manufacturer-guidance evidence
- Histopathologic outcome evidence
- Methodological support

### One-line takeaway

A non-diode pilot study introducing the "pseudodysplastic artifact" construct and the vault's strongest pathologist-blinding/consensus design, showing a manufacturer-preset setting did not appear to obscure genuine dysplasia diagnosis in a very small sample.
