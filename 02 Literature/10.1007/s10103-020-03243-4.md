## Paper Summary

### Citation

Hanke et al. 2021 — "Quantitative determination of cut efficiency during soft tissue surgery using diode lasers in the wavelength range between 400 and 1500 nm." DOI: [10.1007/s10103-020-03243-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-020-03243-4). Full author list, journal name, volume/issue/pages: UNKNOWN (not recorded anywhere in this vault's notes — not invented here).

### Study type

Ex vivo experimental study (controlled, mechanized cutting-efficiency characterization).

### Research question / purpose

Whether increasing diode laser output power produces a proportional increase in cutting efficiency and how this relationship varies across 8 wavelengths (405–1470 nm) in soft-tissue incision.

### Study design

Controlled ex vivo study on porcine gingiva using two diode devices (Fox®/Wolf®, A.R.C. Laser) across 8 wavelengths, CW mode, mechanized incisions at a fixed, computer-controlled speed. Output power was checked with a calibrated power meter before every individual cut (≥5% deviation triggered fiber-tip replacement). Outcome measures were histometric: cut depth, cut width, and total interaction zone (thermal damage) depth, combined into a cutting-efficiency factor γz = cut depth / total interaction zone depth. See [[07 Data/Hanke et al 2021 - Eight-Wavelength Diode Cut Efficiency Data]] for the full parameter table.

### Main findings

- Cutting efficiency (γz) did not increase linearly or uniformly with output power across wavelengths — power increases did not correlate proportionally with incision depth or thermal damage.
- Maximum cut depth reached 820 μm (445 nm) and 344 μm (810 nm), both above 2 W; cut width averaged 125 ± 24 μm across all wavelengths/powers.
- γz showed statistically significant (p < 0.0001) wavelength- and power-dependence.
- Output power was independently verified with a calibrated power meter before every single incision, a stricter QC protocol than most other sources in this vault use — but the actual measured wattage per incision is not tabulated separately from the nominal set value.
- No diagnostic outcome, specimen margin, or histopathologic diagnosis is reported; this is a pure cutting-behavior characterization, not a biopsy model.

### What this paper supports

- That diode cutting efficiency is wavelength- and power-dependent in a non-linear way, in an ex vivo porcine soft-tissue model.
- That per-incision, calibrated-power-meter output verification is methodologically achievable and was actually performed in a real study design (supports feasibility of the delivered-power-verification step in this project's own methodology).
- A precedent for combining verified power output with a controlled, mechanized incision speed in one ex vivo design.

### What this paper does NOT support

- It is NOT manufacturer-preset validation — the power levels tested were experimenter-selected (0.5–4 W steps), not a factory/manufacturer preset being validated.
- Set power ≠ delivered power here in the sense that matters for this project's own claims: the paper verifies delivered power stays within ≥5% of set power per-cut, but does not report the actual measured wattage values, so it cannot be cited as a source of measured-vs-set power deviation data.
- It is NOT a biopsy-oriented specimen-quality study — there is no margin quality, tissue architecture score, specimen interpretability judgment, or diagnostic outcome anywhere in the paper. Thermal damage (interaction zone depth) is measured, but this is a cut-efficiency metric, not a specimen-interpretability or diagnostic-outcome metric, and the two should not be conflated.
- Measured power + controlled speed + histology (thermal damage) here does not constitute preset validation of any specific clinical device/preset.

### Key limitations

- Ex vivo porcine gingiva, not human oral mucosa and not an in vivo biopsy context.
- Exact per-incision measured wattage values are not reported, only the pass/fail QC threshold (≥5% deviation).
- No lesion, no diagnosis, no specimen-interpretability outcome.

### Relevance to current project

Contributes primarily to the **console set power → actual delivered power → incision speed/movement** links of the project's conceptual chain, via a real precedent for per-incision power verification and controlled mechanized speed. Does not reach the **tissue interaction → histopathologic specimen quality** end of the chain, since its tissue-interaction outcome is cut-efficiency/thermal-damage-zone geometry, not specimen interpretability or diagnostic yield.

### Role in the project

- Core prior art
- Delivered-power evidence
- Speed / movement evidence

### One-line takeaway

The closest existing methodological precedent in this vault for combining verified delivered power with controlled incision speed, but it stops short of any biopsy-oriented specimen-quality or diagnostic outcome.

### สรุปภาษาไทย

งานวิจัยนี้เป็นการศึกษาทดลองแบบ ex vivo (นอกร่างกาย) บนเหงือกหมู เปรียบเทียบเลเซอร์ไดโอด 8 ความยาวคลื่น (405–1470 nm) ที่กำลังตั้งค่า 0.5–4 W ในโหมดต่อเนื่อง (CW) โดยควบคุมความเร็วการตัดด้วยเครื่องกลไกที่แม่นยำ และตรวจสอบกำลังขาออกจริงด้วยเพาเวอร์มิเตอร์ที่ผ่านการสอบเทียบก่อนการตัดทุกครั้ง

ผลสำคัญคือ ประสิทธิภาพการตัด (cutting efficiency) ไม่ได้เพิ่มขึ้นเป็นเส้นตรงตามกำลังที่เพิ่มขึ้น และขึ้นอยู่กับความยาวคลื่นอย่างมีนัยสำคัญ อย่างไรก็ตาม งานนี้**ไม่ใช่**การศึกษา biopsy หรือมีผลการวินิจฉัยทางพยาธิวิทยาใด ๆ — เป็นเพียงการศึกษาพฤติกรรมการตัดเนื้อเยื่อเชิงกลไกเท่านั้น

งานนี้มีความเกี่ยวข้องกับโครงการปัจจุบันในแง่ของการยืนยันกำลังขาออกจริงและการควบคุมความเร็วการตัด แต่**ไม่สามารถ**ใช้เป็นหลักฐานยืนยัน manufacturer preset หรือคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อสำหรับการวินิจฉัยได้ เนื่องจากไม่มีตัวแปรเหล่านี้ในงานวิจัยต้นฉบับ
