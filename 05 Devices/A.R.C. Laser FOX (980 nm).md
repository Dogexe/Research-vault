# A.R.C. Laser FOX (980 nm)

## Manufacturer

- A.R.C. Laser GmbH, Nuremberg, Germany — FACT ([[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]])

## Wavelength

- 980 nm — FACT

## Reported preset / set power

- 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 W (0.5 W increments), continuous wave — FACT (study-tested settings, not confirmed as manufacturer-recommended presets)

## Operating modes

- Continuous wave (CW) only tested — FACT

## Fiber / tip

- Bare fiber, 280 μm core / 300 μm diameter — FACT
- Non-initiated, curved-tip handpiece attachment, 30° inclination, 3 mm fiber projection, cleaned before each incision — FACT

## Measured output

- UNKNOWN (numeric value not published) — a fiber-tip power-meter measurement was performed before each incision as a quality-control step, but no numeric measured-output value was reported.

## Power loss / deviation

- UNKNOWN — measurement performed but not published numerically.

## Calibration / measurement method

- Power meter used to standardize fiber-tip output before each incision; brand/model and calibration protocol UNKNOWN.

## Biopsy / tissue outcomes

- Not a diagnostic-biopsy study. Ex vivo porcine mandible (periodontal pocket + oral mucosa) histological tissue-effect study across six wavelengths/systems and four CW power settings.
- No device-specific (980 nm) numeric ranking is reported beyond the pooled cross-wavelength finding that 810 nm and 1064 nm (FOX) showed the least damage and 1470 nm (WOLF) the greatest; 980 nm's relative position within that ranking is UNKNOWN from the extracted text.

## Studies using this device

- [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]] (only source in this vault)

## Known limitations

- Single ex vivo porcine study; no diagnostic/biopsy outcome measured.
- Fiber-tip power measurement performed but never published numerically.
- This device's specific position in the six-system thermal-damage ranking is not stated in the extracted text (only the best/worst systems are named).
