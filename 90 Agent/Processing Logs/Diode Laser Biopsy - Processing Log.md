## Research gap notes accumulated during processing

- CONFIRMED (inherited from [[06 Synthesis/Research Gaps in Dental Diode Laser Output]]): no source pairs controlled tip-initiation status with an independently measured, calibrated power-meter output value.
- CONFIRMED: no source in this vault pairs a calibrated, independently measured *dental-diode* power value with a histologically assessed biopsy specimen outcome (thermal damage zone, coagulation, carbonization, diagnostic yield) in the same study — confirmed across all core processed sources (Angiero et al. 2011, Romeo et al. 2014, Azevedo et al. 2016, Gambino et al. 2026, Gundlapalle et al. 2022, Pergolini et al. 2025, Palaia et al. 2021, Capodiferro et al. 2008/2009, Li et al. 2022), which all report set/console power only. Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 demonstrates the needed measured-power-plus-histology design is achievable in oral mucosa, but only for CO2, not diode, lasers.
- CONFIRMED: no source in this vault reports incision speed as an independently varied variable against specimen histologic quality. Two supporting, non-diagnostic sources now report a fixed (not varied) numeric diode incision speed — Goharkhay et al. 1999 (10 mm/s, ex vivo pig mandible) and Li et al. 2022 (1 mm/s, in vivo mouse tongue) — but neither is transferable to the other or to any diagnostic-biopsy source.
- CONFIRMED: specimen size/margin, not power, is the variable consistently associated with diagnostic yield across three independent sources — Angiero et al. 2011 (size-graded: 2mm 63.63% failure, 3mm 37.71%, ≥4mm 0%), Romeo et al. 2014 (5–10mm lesions, 0% failure), and Gambino et al. 2026 (standardized 5mm margin, 0% failure) all converge on a ≥4–5mm threshold, independently arrived at. Gundlapalle et al. 2022 independently recommends ≥5mm; Pergolini et al. 2025 (ex vivo, no diagnosis rendered) recommends only ≥2mm — an unresolved divergence. No source varies power while holding specimen size constant, or vice versa, to isolate the two variables — this remains open.
- PARTIALLY NARROWED, still unresolved: coagulation-zone evidence — Capodiferro et al. 2008 remains the only identified candidate that *scores* a coagulation zone for a dental diode laser; it is processed to an abstract-level data extraction ([[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data]]) but full text remains unavailable, so the numeric "high power density" threshold it claims is still unverifiable. Angiero et al. 2011 and Gambino et al. 2026 each describe coagulation-related histologic findings qualitatively; Gundlapalle et al. 2022, Pergolini et al. 2025, Capodiferro et al. 2009, and Li et al. 2022 do not improve this gap either — none scores or even mentions coagulation as a measured finding. See [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Coagulation Zone]].
- Capodiferro et al. 2009: this vault's first zero-thermal-artifact data point — a single (n=1) real human diode excisional biopsy (880 nm, 2.5 W CW) found no measurable margin thermal alteration at all. As n=1 this does not shift any CORE-tier threshold or range.
- Li et al. 2022: a diode-vs-Er:YAG-vs-cold-scalpel mouse oral-mucosa wound model corroborates, from an independent research group and tissue model, that diode lasers produce more carbonization and deeper thermal/apoptotic damage than a non-diode comparator — consistent in direction with Al-Ani et al. — but is not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway and does not touch specimen-quality or margin-readability evidence.
- Wilder-Smith et al. 1995: the vault has a worked example of the exact measurement design [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]] identifies as missing — calibrated power-meter-measured output paired with thermocouple and histologic thermal-damage data, in oral mucosa — but for three CO2 lasers, not a diode laser, so it cannot close the gap for this project. Retained as a methodological, not evidentiary, reference.
- RESOLVED: whether dental-diode-specific thermal-artifact and specimen-margin measurements exist at all — yes, confirmed by six independent processed sources. What remains unresolved is linking any of these to a *measured* (as opposed to set) power value.
- Gambino et al. 2026: a real untreated-control-group comparison shows laser-treated tissue is statistically significantly thicker than healthy tissue even when diagnosis is unimpaired — sharpens "thermal artifact does not equal diagnostic failure" from an assumption into a directly tested finding, but only at one fixed power/margin combination.
- Gundlapalle et al. 2022 and Pergolini et al. 2025: two dedicated CW-vs-PW comparisons disagree on direction — Gundlapalle et al. (real human biopsy, matched 3W) and Romanos et al. 2022 both found PW causes less thermal damage than CW, while Pergolini et al. (ex vivo, pooled across 2/3/4W) found a non-significant trend toward PW causing *more* damage. Pergolini et al. is also the vault's first source to formally test power level (2/3/4W) as an independent variable with a significance test, and found no significant power effect on thermal damage.
- Goharkhay et al. 1999: the CW-vs-PW conflict is three-way, not two-way — Goharkhay et al. (ex vivo pig mandible, 7 matched power levels 0.5–4.5 W) found no consistent direction of difference between CW and either of two pulsed settings at any power level, a third pattern distinct from both Gundlapalle et al. (PW<CW) and Pergolini et al. (PW>CW, ns). The same source also independently corroborates Pergolini et al.'s finding of no power-dependence of thermal-damage-zone width (0.5–4.5 W tested), while confirming a clean, strong power-to-incision-*depth* relationship — power affects how deep the cut goes, not clearly how much collateral damage results.
- Goharkhay et al. 1999: first source in this project's literature to report both a numeric incision speed (10 mm/s) and genuinely wattmeter-measured (not set/console) output power — but its device (Dentek LD 15, 810 nm) is not used anywhere else in this vault, so neither value can be transferred to any other source.
- Palaia et al. 2021: a fourth, large (n=42) real human in vivo 100%-diagnostic-yield source exists within the 1.6–2.7 W CW range (2.5 W, 445 nm), strengthening that range. Its 445 nm wavelength also shows a numerically larger epithelial thermal effect than the vault's 808/980 nm sources, and the opposite epithelium-vs-connective-tissue compartment pattern from Gambino et al. 2026 (980 nm) — suggestive of a wavelength effect on thermal-damage magnitude and distribution, but not confirmed by any controlled within-study wavelength comparison.
