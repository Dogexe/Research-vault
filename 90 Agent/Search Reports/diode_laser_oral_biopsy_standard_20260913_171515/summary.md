# Executive Summary: Diode Laser Oral Soft-Tissue Biopsy — Novelty Verification

This search targeted the specific niche of **oral soft-tissue biopsy performed with dental diode lasers**, with emphasis on **measured delivered power, incision/excision speed, operating parameters (wavelength, mode, duty cycle), and histopathologic outcomes** (thermal artifact, margin integrity, diagnostic quality). 220 candidate papers were retrieved and screened (OpenAlex + PubMed MeSH + one round of citation-network expansion from the top 3 seed papers); 36 were rated highly relevant (RCS ≥ 6), of which 24 are direct, on-topic hits (RCS 7-8).

**Field consensus.** There is a well-established and fairly mature body of literature (spanning ~2006-2026) directly on this topic. The consistent finding across in-vitro (porcine/rabbit tongue, gelatin phantom), ex-vivo, and in-vivo human studies is that diode lasers (commonly 810, 940, 980, and increasingly 445 nm "blue" wavelengths) produce a measurable, wavelength- and power-dependent zone of thermal coagulation/artifact at incision margins, which can compromise histopathologic interpretability if power is too high or contact time too long relative to tissue absorption. The dominant experimental paradigm is a **controlled comparison**: diode vs. scalpel, and/or diode vs. other laser types (CO2, Er:YAG, Er,Cr:YSGG, Nd:YAG, KTP), at specified/measured wattage and speed, followed by blinded histopathologic scoring of thermal damage width, margin clarity, and diagnostic adequacy.

**Key methods/frameworks.** Two recurring designs dominate: (1) **ex-vivo/in-vitro comparative incision studies** on animal or human tissue that explicitly report delivered power (W), mode (continuous wave vs. pulsed/super-pulsed, duty cycle), fiber/contact conditions, and incision/excision time, correlated with histomorphometric thermal damage zone width; and (2) **clinical excisional/incisional biopsy studies** in humans (leukoplakia, fibromas, mucoceles, hyperplastic lesions) comparing diode laser to cold-scalpel or other laser modalities on histopathologic diagnostic yield and margin quality. A smaller but important cluster uses **novel wavelengths** (445 nm blue diode, dual-wavelength diode systems) explicitly to reduce thermal artifact relative to traditional 810/980 nm diodes.

**Most influential / most directly relevant papers:**
1. **"The Impact of Laser Thermal Effect on Histological Evaluation of Oral Soft Tissue Biopsy: Systematic Review"** (*Dent J*, 2023, RCS 8) — the closest existing review to this exact novelty question; any new manuscript on this topic should be explicitly differentiated from this review's scope and conclusions.
2. **"Quantitative determination of cut efficiency during soft tissue surgery using diode lasers in the wavelength range between 400 and 1500 nm"** (*Lasers Med Sci*, 2021, RCS 8) — the strongest existing precedent for systematically varying wavelength/power and measuring cut efficiency with histology.
3. Multiple RCS-7 ex-vivo/in-vivo studies (Yammine 2018; Yildirim/945 & 976 nm studies 2020-2021; Palaia 2021; Pergolini 2025; Gambino 2026 OCT study) collectively establish that **wavelength, power, and mode are the primary independent variables already studied**, and thermal-artifact width / margin quality are the standard outcome measures.
4. Direct diode-vs-other-laser-type histology comparisons (Medoral 2010, 2020; JCED 2016) are the closest prior-art baseline for any manuscript claiming a novel diode protocol.

**Open questions / potential novelty gaps** (for the user to verify against their own manuscript's specific claim): the literature is dense on wavelength/power-vs-thermal-damage comparisons, but comparatively thinner on (a) real-time or closed-loop power/speed control during biopsy, (b) newer blue (445 nm) and dual-wavelength diode systems combined with quantitative histomorphometry in large human case series (most human dual-wavelength work is still ex-vivo or small case series), and (c) standardized/reported incision-speed metrics (many papers report power and wavelength but not measured speed) — this last point in particular appears to be a genuine reporting gap across the field and may be a legitimate point of novelty if the target manuscript measures and reports incision speed explicitly.

**Caveats on this search:** discovery-curve saturation estimation returned `fit_failed=true` (heuristic fallback estimate ~67%, 95% CI 58-78%), meaning the tool's own confidence in "we've found everything relevant" is moderate, not high — appropriate for a novelty pre-check but this should not be treated as an exhaustive systematic review. One citation-expansion hop was run (Standard tier, 1 hop) from the top 3 seed papers; a second hop or a dedicated Deep-tier search focused specifically on "incision speed" + "delivered power" as primary (not secondary) outcomes is recommended if the user needs publication-grade certainty on the novelty claim.
