## Study

- Objective: To establish whether diode-laser-induced physical (thermal) damage affects histopathologic diagnosis, and to evaluate damage to the resection margins.
- Design: Retrospective case series, 2005–2010, S. Gerardo Hospital, Milan; specimens examined separately by two pathologists.
- Sample or material: 608 cases of oral soft-tissue lesions (cheek, gingiva, buccal mucosa, tongue, lips).
- Clinical or laboratory context: Real human diagnostic-biopsy pathway; the largest sample size of any source currently processed in this vault for the biopsy-quality question.

## Findings

- FACT: For specimens larger than 3 mm excised with the diode laser, data were not significant in terms of stromal changes or vascular stasis.
- FACT: Epithelial and stromal changes were significantly more frequent in specimens with a mean size below 3 mm; diagnosis was "not achievable" in 46.15% of such small specimens.
- FACT: The authors conclude the diode laser is a valid instrument for excising oral lesions larger than 3 mm in diameter, but induces serious thermal effects in small lesions (mean size below 3 mm), and suggest that specimens have an in vivo diameter of at least 5 mm for a reliable histologic reading.
