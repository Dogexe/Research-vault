## Research question

How do diode laser preset/operating parameters and specimen size relate to thermal artifact, coagulation, carbonization, specimen margin quality, and diagnostic quality in oral soft-tissue biopsy?

## Current evidence

- [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]] — n=608, 808 nm diode, 1.6–2.7 W CW (average 2.5 W), 320 μm fiber, DMT Milan, retrospective 2005–2010. Reports size-graded diagnostic yield and thermal-damage width.
- [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]] — n=17, 808 nm diode 2 W CW (compared against 532 nm KTP 1.5 W PW), lesion size 0.5–1 cm. Reports per-lesion-type margin damage and diagnostic yield.
- [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]] — 980 nm diode, 3.5 W PW / 3.5 W Boost PW, ex vivo porcine tongue. Reports ETTD and carbonization score; no diagnosis rendered (not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway).
- [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]] — n=30, 980 nm diode 2 W CW, 300 μm fiber, contact mode, standardized 5 mm circumferential margin, Raffaello Laser DMT. Reports thermal damage, diagnostic yield, and a true untreated-tissue control comparison.
- Reused background, not itself a biopsy-diagnosis study: [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters|Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. 2020]] — in vitro porcine periodontal-pocket/mucosa model, six wavelengths (445–1470 nm) at 0.5–2.0 W set power; its own conclusion is stated "for oral soft-tissue biopsy" even though its tissue model is not itself a biopsy specimen.
- NEW: [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] — n=30 diode biopsies (15 CW, 15 PW, matched 3 W, 980 nm Zolar Plus), real human oral mucosal biopsy pathway (compared against 30 scalpel biopsies). Directly compares CW vs. PW thermal-damage width and pathologist-graded slide quality at one matched power; no quantified diagnostic-yield percentage reported.
- NEW: [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] — n=36, dual-wavelength (450/808 nm) diode, fully crossed 2/3/4 W × CW/PW design (6 groups), ex vivo porcine tongue. No diagnosis rendered (not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway); the vault's first source to statistically test power level and mode as separate independent variables against thermal damage.
- NEW (abstract-level only): [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data|Capodiferro et al. 2008]] — n=25 real human diode excisions (2–7 W), reports coagulation "only with high power density output" and a qualitative whole-cohort "no alterations were found" statement; no full text available, no quantified diagnostic-yield percentage.
- NEW (n=1 case report): [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2009 - Diode Laser Chondroid Lipoma Case Report Data|Capodiferro et al. 2009]] — real human diode excisional biopsy (880 nm, 2.5 W CW), 100% diagnosis (n=1) with *no* measurable margin thermal alteration on conventional or confocal microscopy. A separate paper from 2008, by the same author group; not a substitute for it and does not mention coagulation.
- NEW (SUPPORTING, not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway): [[07 Data/Li et al 2022 - Diode vs Er-YAG vs Cold Scalpel Mice Oral Mucosa Data|Li et al. 2022]] — mouse oral-mucosa wound model, diode 3.0 W pulsed; reports an explicit tip-movement/incision speed (1 mm/s) and qualitative carbonization/apoptosis findings, but no lesion and no diagnosis rendered.
- NEW (BACKGROUND, non-diode): [[07 Data/Wilder-Smith et al 1995 - CO2 Laser Incision and Thermal Damage Data|Wilder-Smith et al. 1995]] — three CO2 lasers, ex vivo pig mandible; independently measures delivered power with a calibrated power meter alongside thermal/histologic outcomes, demonstrating the needed measurement design but not for a diode laser.
- Suter et al. 2010 remains abstract-level, unprocessed to full text; see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]].

All seven contributing sources report **set/console power only**; none independently verifies delivered output with a calibrated power meter at the point of tissue contact.

## Parameter → histologic outcome relationships

- FACT: Wavelength/set-power combination is associated with tissue-effect magnitude in Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.'s in vitro model — lost-tissue area, thermal-damage area, and necrosis area were all higher at 2.0 W set power than at 0.5 W, regardless of wavelength; the source's own conclusion identifies 810 nm at 0.5–2 W as the combination least likely to cause thermal damage to peri-incisional margins. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- FACT: Within Azevedo et al.'s single diode device, the higher "Boost" pulsed setting (nominally still 3.5 W) produced a larger thermal-damage extent (ETTD 626.82 μm) than the standard PW setting at the same nominal power label (456.15 μm) — a within-device, set-power-level gradient. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- CONFLICTING WITHIN-SOURCE FINDING (preserved, not smoothed): in that same Azevedo et al. comparison, the setting with the *larger* ETTD (Boost, 626.82 μm) had a *lower* carbonization score (1.80) than the setting with the smaller ETTD (standard PW, 456.15 μm, score 2.50). The source's own pooled, cross-instrument ETTD–carbonization correlation (r=0.789, p=0.01) does not hold as a simple within-device pattern for the diode's own two settings. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: Diode incisions were scored "irregular" in 80% of cases at both Azevedo et al. settings (2/10 regular at each), the second-worst incision regularity of the six instruments tested (after Nd:YAG). [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: Angiero et al. measured thermal-damage width of 260.7–321.4 μm (mean 282.8 μm) across their 1.6–2.7 W CW series, but did not report width by individual power value within that range — the range itself is not resolved to a per-setting relationship. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- FACT: Romeo et al. found margin thermal damage varied by lesion type at one fixed diode setting (2 W CW): more inflamed/cellular lesions (oral lichen planus, 0.473 mm mean) showed greater margin damage than less-inflamed lesions (melanotic macula, 0.149 mm; mucocele, ~0.272 mm diode-only). Because power was held constant, this is a tissue-type effect, not a power effect. [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]
- FACT: Gambino et al., at one fixed set power (2 W CW) and standardized 5 mm margin, found laser-treated tissue statistically significantly thicker than untreated scalpel-control tissue for both epithelium (p=0.021) and lamina propria (p=0.034) — the clearest statistically tested tissue effect of a single set-power value in this vault's biopsy evidence, though power was not varied. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEW FACT: Pergolini et al. 2025, the vault's first fully crossed power (2/3/4 W) × mode (CW/PW) design, found raw thermal-damage minima/maxima increased with power (epithelium 0.62 mm at 2 W CW up to 3.12 mm at 4 W PW; connective tissue 0.53 mm at 4 W CW up to 3.19 mm at 4 W PW), but the pooled statistical test of power level alone (disregarding mode) found **no significant difference** in either epithelial (F(2,33)=0.271, p=0.765) or connective-tissue (F(2,33)=0.434, p=0.652) damage across 2/3/4 W. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEW FACT: This is the first time a vault source has formally tested power level as an independent variable against thermal-damage outcome with a significance test spanning more than two power values, and the result weakens rather than strengthens the "higher power → more damage" interpretation drawn informally from Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. and Azevedo et al. (neither of which ran a power-only significance test across ≥3 levels). [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- INTERPRETATION (revised): Taken together, the processed sources support only a weak, inconsistently-tested association between higher power/intensity setting and greater thermal-effect magnitude. Raw values trend upward with power in Gutiérrez-Corrales et al., Azevedo et al., and Pergolini et al., but the one source that formally tested power across three levels (Pergolini et al.) found no statistically significant effect, and the *carbonization* component does not track linearly with thermal-damage extent even within a single device (Azevedo et al.). No processed source varies power alone while holding tissue type, contact mode, and fiber diameter constant to isolate a clean power → histologic-outcome curve. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEW — pulsed vs. continuous mode, now directly evidenced: Gundlapalle et al. 2022, at matched 3 W set power in a real human biopsy pathway, found PW produced smaller mean thermal-damage width (208.766 μm) than CW (279.84 μm), plus better pathologist-graded slide quality at both periphery and center for PW. This agrees in direction with the vault's already-reused Romanos et al. 2022 finding (PW < CW thermal penetration depth at 2 W, ex vivo bovine gingiva). [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]]
- NEW — conflicting direction: Pergolini et al. 2025, pooling all three power levels, found the opposite trend — PW damage was numerically *greater* than CW for both epithelium (1.45 ± 0.64 mm vs. 1.22 ± 0.45 mm) and connective tissue (1.65 ± 0.73 mm vs. 1.49 ± 0.46 mm), though neither difference reached statistical significance (p=0.234, p=0.444). This directly conflicts with the CW<PW-damage direction reported by Gundlapalle et al. and Romanos et al., and is preserved as an unresolved conflict, not smoothed over. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: Whether protein coagulation specifically (as opposed to thermal-damage width generally) depends on power/power-density threshold, as Capodiferro et al.'s abstract claims ("coagulation of proteins were present only with high power density output," 2–7 W diode), cannot currently be confirmed or contested by this vault — the paper remains unprocessed and its power values appear to be set/nominal, not independently measured. See [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Coagulation Zone]].
- FACT: Coagulation-zone evidence remains unimproved by the two newly processed sources. Neither Gundlapalle et al. nor Pergolini et al. scores or measures a coagulation zone as its own isolated variable; Gundlapalle et al.'s Discussion describes coagulation only as a general laser-tissue physics mechanism (collagen shrinkage sealing vessels), not a measured finding in its own specimens, and Pergolini et al. does not mention coagulation as a distinct outcome at all. This is the same evidentiary gap already noted for Angiero et al. and Gambino et al. (qualitative descriptors only). [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]] [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- FACT: Carbonization is listed only as a qualitative artifact by both new sources (Gundlapalle et al.: figure caption only, not scored or split by CW/PW; Pergolini et al.: listed among observed artifacts, not scored numerically) — neither adds a quantified carbonization score comparable to Azevedo et al.'s 0–4 scale. [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]

## Specimen size and diagnostic quality

- FACT: Angiero et al. (n=608) found diagnostic failure sharply size-graded: 63.63% unreadable at 2 mm specimen size (29/46), 37.71% unreadable at 3 mm (31/84), and 0% unreadable (100% readable) at ≥4 mm; the authors recommend an in vivo specimen diameter of at least 5 mm to allow for post-excision tissue shrinkage. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- FACT: Romeo et al. (lesion size 0.5–1 cm, i.e., 5–10 mm) achieved certainty diagnosis in 17/17 specimens (100%). [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]
- FACT: Gambino et al. (standardized 5 mm circumferential margin) obtained definitive diagnosis in 30/30 specimens (100%), even though laser-treated tissue was statistically significantly thicker than control tissue in the same specimens. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- INTERPRETATION: All three diagnostic-yield sources converge, independently, on a ≥4–5 mm specimen size/margin threshold below which diagnostic failure becomes likely and above which it was not observed in any processed source. This is the single strongest and most consistent parameter-outcome relationship in the vault's biopsy evidence — stronger and more consistent than any power-related relationship identified above. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- HYPOTHESIS: Specimen size/margin relative to the margin thermal-damage zone (which itself measures roughly 150–630 μm / 0.15–0.63 mm across the processed sources) may be a more direct determinant of diagnostic yield than the power setting itself, provided the setting is otherwise compatible with clean tissue removal. This remains untested as a controlled independent variable — no processed source varies power while holding specimen size constant, or vice versa. [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]]
- FACT: Gambino et al.'s a priori 5 mm margin protocol matches Angiero et al.'s independently, empirically derived ≥5 mm recommendation — two different methods (a priori design vs. retrospective size-stratified failure analysis) converging on the same figure. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]] [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- NEW FACT: Gundlapalle et al. 2022 independently recommends a minimum 5 mm normal-tissue margin "if a diode laser with a power setting of 3W or lesser is used" — a third, independent convergence on the ≥5 mm figure, in a real human biopsy pathway. [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]]
- NEW FACT, diverging: Pergolini et al. 2025 recommends a smaller margin — at least 2 mm, "especially in excisional biopsies of lesions suspected of malignancy" — based on its ex vivo finding that 2 W CW produced the least thermal damage. This is a real divergence from the ≥5 mm figure independently reported by Angiero et al., Gambino et al., and Gundlapalle et al.; see Conflicting findings below. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEW: No diagnostic-yield percentage is reported by either new source — Gundlapalle et al. states only qualitatively that laser-biopsy artifacts "were negligible, thus allowing adequate histological examination and correct diagnosis" without a numeric figure, and Pergolini et al. is ex vivo with no diagnosis rendered at all (like Azevedo et al.). Neither source therefore adds a new diagnostic-yield data point to the ≥4–5 mm threshold established above. [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]

## Conflicting findings

- Not a confirmed cross-source conflict: Angiero et al.'s size-graded failure below 4 mm is consistent with, not contradicted by, Romeo et al.'s and Gambino et al.'s 100% success at larger sizes/margins — the three sources describe different points along what looks like the same size/margin-dependent curve, not opposing claims. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- CONFIRMED within-source conflict (preserved as-is): Azevedo et al.'s own diode data show ETTD and carbonization score moving in *opposite* directions between the two tested settings (higher ETTD at Boost, lower carbonization at Boost) — this contradicts the naive expectation that more thermal-damage extent implies more carbonization, and is not resolved or averaged away here. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- Rough, not validated, order-of-magnitude consistency: margin/thermal-damage figures across the four earlier-processed sources (Angiero et al. 260.7–321.4 μm; Romeo et al. 0.149–0.473 mm i.e. 149–473 μm; Azevedo et al. 456.15–626.82 μm; Gambino et al. 288.9–430.3 μm) fall in the same broad sub-millimeter range despite different species (human vs. porcine), wavelengths (808 vs. 980 nm), and power/mode — noted only as a cross-check, not a pooled or averaged comparison, since methods and tissue differ. Gundlapalle et al.'s 208.766–279.84 μm and Pergolini et al.'s 0.53–3.19 mm (530–3190 μm) figures are consistent with this range at their lower end but Pergolini et al.'s higher-power values (up to 3.19 mm) exceed it substantially — the widest damage range yet reported for a dental diode source in this vault, from an ex vivo model with no diagnosis rendered. [[04 Evidence/Surgical Margins]] [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEW CONFIRMED cross-source conflict — direction of CW vs. PW thermal-damage effect: Gundlapalle et al. (real human biopsy, matched 3 W) and the vault's reused Romanos et al. 2022 (ex vivo bovine, 2 W) both found PW produces *less* thermal damage than CW. Pergolini et al. (ex vivo porcine, pooled across 2/3/4 W) found the opposite trend — PW numerically greater than CW for both epithelium and connective tissue — though not statistically significant. This is a genuine, unresolved directional conflict across three sources, not smoothed into a single conclusion; possible explanations (untested by any single source) include species/tissue-model differences (human vs. bovine vs. porcine), power level (Gundlapalle et al. and Romanos et al. tested one fixed power each; Pergolini et al. pooled across three), and device/wavelength differences (single 980 nm vs. dual 445/808 nm vs. Romanos et al.'s own wavelength). [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]] [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]]
- NEW CONFLICT — recommended safety margin: Angiero et al., Gambino et al., and Gundlapalle et al. (all real human biopsy pathways, or a priori designed around one) independently converge on ≥5 mm, while Pergolini et al. (ex vivo, no diagnosis rendered) recommends ≥2 mm. This is preserved as a genuine divergence, not averaged into a single number — the difference plausibly tracks the ex vivo/no-diagnosis vs. in vivo/real-diagnosis distinction (Pergolini et al.'s own Discussion notes ex vivo thermal effects are likely *underestimated* relative to in vivo, due to reduced vascularization in deceased tissue), but this explanation is INTERPRETATION, not independently tested by any source. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]] [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- NEW — internal source inconsistency (Pergolini et al., preserved not resolved): the source's own narrative text reports "t = 1.94, p < 0.01" for the Group A (2 W CW) vs. Group B (2 W PW) epithelial-damage comparison, but the source's own Table 2 lists p = 0.084 for the identical comparison. NEEDS VERIFICATION — this vault does not correct or reconcile the discrepancy. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]

## Major limitations

- Every contributing source reports **set/console power only**; none independently verifies delivered output with a calibrated power meter at the point of tissue contact. Per vault rule, set power is never treated as delivered power anywhere in this synthesis.
- No processed source reports incision speed at all.
- Measurement methods for thermal damage/margin are inconsistent across sources: distance from specimen border to end of visible denaturation at 100× (Angiero et al.); ETTD on Masson Trichrome/H&E at unspecified magnification (Azevedo et al.); mm on H&E with digital software at 5×/10× (Romeo et al.); digital image analysis at 100× cross-validated against OCT (Gambino et al.). Magnitudes are broadly comparable in order of magnitude only, not directly poolable.
- Study designs differ sharply in what is held constant vs. varied: Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. varies power and wavelength but is not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway; Azevedo et al. varies power/setting but is ex vivo porcine tissue with no diagnosis rendered; Angiero et al., Romeo et al., and Gambino et al. are real human diagnostic-biopsy pathways but each uses one fixed power setting (or a narrow uncontrolled range, in Angiero et al.'s case) and does not vary power as an independent variable.
- Sample scale and design vary widely: Angiero et al. is a large single-institution retrospective series (n=608); Romeo et al. and Gambino et al. are small-to-moderate prospective series (n=17, n=30); Azevedo et al. is a small ex vivo bench comparison (diode n=20 of 120 total incisions).
- Romeo et al. has uneven diode/KTP distribution for two of six lesion types (mucocele, hyperkeratosis), requiring an in-vault recomputation (flagged INTERPRETATION in the source data note) to isolate diode-only figures.
- Capodiferro et al. 2008 — the vault's only identified candidate that scores a coagulation zone specifically — is now processed to an abstract-level data extraction ([[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data]]) but full text remains unavailable; its power values (2–7 W) appear to be set/nominal, and its claim about coagulation appearing "only with high power density output" still cannot be verified against full text. Capodiferro et al. 2009, a separate n=1 case report by the same author group, does not resolve this — it does not mention coagulation.
- Li et al. 2022 is a mouse wound-healing model (not human, not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway); no lesion excised, no diagnosis rendered, and wavelength of its diode device is not stated. Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 uses CO2 lasers, not diode, so it cannot contribute diode-specific evidence to this synthesis despite its strong measured-power methodology.
- Gundlapalle et al. 2022 reports no numeric diagnostic-yield percentage despite stating this as a study aim; no fiber diameter, tip type, or contact-mode is reported; no calibration protocol is reported; and no PDF is cached in this vault's Zotero library (full text was retrieved directly from PubMed Central instead). [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]]
- Pergolini et al. 2025 is ex vivo porcine tissue with no diagnosis rendered (not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway); uses a non-standard, looser significance threshold (p ≤ 0.10 rather than the conventional 0.05); and contains an internal inconsistency between its narrative-text p-value and its own Table 2 for the one comparison it reports as significant. Its total-energy value (579.8 J) is ambiguously scoped in the retrieved text (unclear whether per-sample or per-group). [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]

## Research gaps

- No source pairs a calibrated, independently measured dental-diode power value with a histologically assessed biopsy specimen outcome (thermal damage, coagulation, carbonization, diagnostic yield) in the same study. Confirmed across all six full-text-processed biopsy sources, including the two newly added this pass. [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]]
- No source reports incision speed as an independently varied (or even recorded) variable against specimen histologic quality.
- No source varies specimen size/margin and power independently in the same design — the ≥4–5 mm size/margin threshold and the power-related thermal-effect associations above have never been tested against each other while holding the other constant.
- No source scores or quantifies a coagulation zone as its own isolated variable for a dental diode laser with full-text verification; Capodiferro et al. 2008 is the only identified candidate and remains unprocessed at full-text level (now has an abstract-level data extraction only — see [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data]]). This gap is unchanged by the current update — neither Gundlapalle et al., Pergolini et al., nor the newly processed Capodiferro et al. 2009 (which does not mention coagulation) or Li et al. 2022 scores coagulation as its own variable.
- Suter et al. 2010 (diode vs. CO2 thermal-damage-zone comparison) remains an identified but unprocessed candidate. See [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]].
- NEW GAP: the CW-vs-PW thermal-damage direction conflict (Gundlapalle et al. + Romanos et al. showing PW<CW, vs. Pergolini et al. showing a non-significant PW>CW trend) is not resolved by any single source — no study varies species/tissue model while holding power and mode constant to test whether the conflict is a tissue-model artifact or a real device/wavelength-dependent effect.
- NEW GAP: no source reconciles the diverging recommended safety margins (≥5 mm: Angiero et al., Gambino et al., Gundlapalle et al.; ≥2 mm: Pergolini et al.) against a shared measurement protocol or tissue model — the ex vivo/in vivo distinction is a plausible but untested explanation.
- No standardized, shared calibration or measurement protocol is used across the biopsy-relevant sources, limiting direct pooling of magnitudes even where figures are broadly consistent.

## Provisional interpretation

- INTERPRETATION: Across currently processed evidence, specimen size/excision margin — not the power setting — is the variable most consistently and strongly associated with diagnostic outcome. Three independent sources, using different designs (retrospective size-stratified analysis, small prospective series, standardized-margin prospective series), converge on a ≥4–5 mm threshold. [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]]
- INTERPRETATION (revised): Power/intensity setting shows only a weak, inconsistently-supported association with thermal-effect magnitude. Raw values trend upward with power in Gutiérrez-Corrales et al., Azevedo et al., and now Pergolini et al., but the one source that formally tested power across three levels with a significance test (Pergolini et al.) found no statistically significant power effect on either epithelial or connective-tissue damage. This vault cannot currently state a general power → histologic-damage-severity curve, because (a) all values are set/console power, never independently measured; (b) the one within-device carbonization comparison available (Azevedo et al.) shows thermal-damage extent and carbonization score moving in opposite directions; and (c) the one rigorous multi-level power test available (Pergolini et al.) found no significant effect at all. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- INTERPRETATION (new): CW-vs-PW mode does not yet have a consistent direction of effect on thermal damage. Two sources (Gundlapalle et al., real human biopsy; Romanos et al., ex vivo bovine) found PW causes less damage than CW; one source (Pergolini et al., ex vivo porcine, pooled across three power levels) found a non-significant trend in the opposite direction. This vault cannot state whether mode choice reduces or increases thermal artifact in general — the effect, if real, may be species/tissue-model- or power-level-dependent, but no source tests this directly. [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]]
- INTERPRETATION: The chain **preset/set power → tissue effect → specimen quality** can currently be asserted only for its middle-to-end segment (tissue effect is measurable and, at fixed settings, does not necessarily impair diagnosis if specimen size/margin is adequate — most directly shown by Gambino et al.'s significant-thickness-yet-100%-diagnosis result). The first segment, **preset/set power → measured/delivered power**, is addressed elsewhere in this vault ([[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]], [[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]]) and shows device-dependent, non-uniform deviation; no processed biopsy source connects that measured-deviation evidence to its own tissue-effect or diagnostic-outcome data. This vault therefore **cannot yet connect preset power to delivered power to histologic damage in a single evidentiary chain** — each link exists in isolation, but no single study, or combination of studies with shared devices/settings, bridges all three.
- HYPOTHESIS: If a device's measured output deviates substantially from its set value (as documented for several devices in [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]]), the actual tissue effect and specimen quality delivered at a given panel setting could differ from what any of the set-power-only biopsy sources here would predict. This is untested by any source currently in this vault.
- This synthesis draws no clinical recommendation. All conclusions above are provisional and will be revised if Capodiferro et al., Suter et al., or other candidates are processed to full text.

## สรุปภาษาไทย

- บันทึกสังเคราะห์นี้เป็น **PROVISIONAL** (ผลชั่วคราว) อ้างอิงเฉพาะหลักฐานเรื่องคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อ biopsy ที่ประมวลผลแล้วในคลังนี้เท่านั้น
- **อัปเดตรอบนี้:** ประมวลผลบทความใหม่ 2 ฉบับ — Gundlapalle et al. 2022 (ชิ้นเนื้อ biopsy จริงในมนุษย์ เปรียบเทียบ CW กับ PW ที่กำลัง 3W เท่ากัน) และ Pergolini et al. 2025 (ex vivo ในลิ้นหมู ออกแบบไขว้ครบทุกคู่ระหว่างกำลัง 2/3/4W กับโหมด CW/PW)
- ความสัมพันธ์ที่ชัดเจนและสม่ำเสมอที่สุดยังคงเป็น **ขนาดชิ้นเนื้อ/ขอบตัด** ไม่ใช่ค่ากำลังไฟ — ปัจจุบันมีแหล่งข้อมูลอิสระ 3 รายการ (Angiero, Gambino, และ Gundlapalle ที่เพิ่มใหม่) สอดคล้องกันว่าขอบตัด ≥5 มม. เป็นค่าที่แนะนำ ขณะที่ Pergolini et al. (ex vivo ไม่มีการวินิจฉัยจริง) แนะนำเพียง ≥2 มม. ซึ่งเป็นข้อขัดแย้งใหม่ที่ยังไม่ได้แก้ไข
- ทิศทางของผล CW เทียบกับ PW ต่อความเสียหายจากความร้อนยังขัดแย้งกันข้ามแหล่งข้อมูล: Gundlapalle et al. และ Romanos et al. (มีอยู่แล้วในคลัง) พบว่า PW เสียหายน้อยกว่า CW แต่ Pergolini et al. พบแนวโน้มตรงกันข้าม (ไม่มีนัยสำคัญทางสถิติ) — ยังคงรักษาความขัดแย้งนี้ไว้ ไม่ปรับให้กลมกลืนกัน
- Pergolini et al. เป็นแหล่งข้อมูลแรกที่ทดสอบค่ากำลัง (2/3/4W) เป็นตัวแปรอิสระด้วยสถิติอย่างเป็นทางการ และ**ไม่พบผลอย่างมีนัยสำคัญทางสถิติ**ของค่ากำลังต่อความเสียหายทั้งชั้นเยื่อบุผิวและชั้นเนื้อเยื่อเกี่ยวพัน แม้ค่าดิบจะดูเพิ่มขึ้นตามกำลังไฟ
- หลักฐานเรื่อง coagulation zone **ยังไม่เพียงพอ** — ทั้งสองบทความใหม่ไม่ได้วัดหรือให้คะแนน coagulation zone แยกเป็นตัวแปรของตัวเอง เช่นเดียวกับ Capodiferro et al. 2008 ที่ยังไม่มีฉบับเต็มในคลังนี้
- ห่วงโซ่ **ค่าตั้งเครื่อง → กำลังที่ส่งจริง → ความเสียหายทางเนื้อเยื่อ** ยังเชื่อมโยงไม่ครบเช่นเดิม: ทั้งสองบทความใหม่รายงานเฉพาะค่ากำลังที่ตั้ง (set power) เท่านั้น ไม่มีการวัดกำลังขาออกจริงด้วยเพาเวอร์มิเตอร์
- บันทึกนี้ไม่มีข้อเสนอแนะทางคลินิกใด ๆ และรักษาข้อขัดแย้งภายในและระหว่างแหล่งข้อมูลไว้ตามเดิม ไม่ปรับให้กลมกลืนกัน
- **อัปเดตล่าสุด:** ประมวลผล Capodiferro et al. 2008 ถึงระดับบทคัดย่อ, Capodiferro et al. 2009 (ผู้ป่วย 1 ราย ไม่มีความเสียหายจากความร้อนที่ขอบตัดเลย แยกจาก 2008 โดยสิ้นเชิง), Li et al. 2022 (แบบจำลองในหนู) และ Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 (เลเซอร์ CO2 ไม่ใช่ไดโอด) — ไม่มีบทความใดปิดช่องว่างหลักของโครงการนี้
