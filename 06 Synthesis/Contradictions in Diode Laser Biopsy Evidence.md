# Contradictions in Diode Laser Biopsy Evidence

**Status: PROVISIONAL.** Built only from processed biopsy-related literature, evidence notes, and data extractions already in this vault: [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]], [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]], [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]], [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]], [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]], [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]], plus reused supporting/background sources [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth|Romanos et al. 2022]] and [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters|Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. 2020]], and the vault's existing evidence/synthesis notes: [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]], [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]], [[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]], [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]], [[06 Synthesis/Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Quality]], [[06 Synthesis/Current Evidence on Diode Laser Power Output]]. No new literature was searched or added to produce this note. This note draws no clinical recommendation.

## PW vs CW

### Sources reporting less thermal damage with PW

- FACT: [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] — real human oral mucosal biopsy pathway (n=30 diode: 15 CW, 15 PW), 980 nm Zolar Plus, matched 3 W set power — found PW produced a smaller mean necrotic-border/thermal-damage width (208.766 μm) than CW (279.84 μm), plus better pathologist-graded slide quality at both periphery and center for PW.
- FACT: [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth|Romanos et al. 2022]] — ex vivo bovine gingiva (periodontal-pocket irradiation model, not a biopsy specimen), SIROLaser Blue, 445 nm and 970 nm, 2 W set power (chopped-pulse mean 1 W) — found pulsed mode produced lower thermal/collagen-denaturation *penetration depth* than continuous-wave mode at the same nominal power setting, for both wavelengths and both initiated/non-initiated tip conditions. This is offered as supporting, not primary biopsy, evidence: it is not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway and its outcome (penetration depth) is a different measurement from thermal-damage-zone width.

### Sources reporting the opposite or no significant difference

- FACT: [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] — ex vivo porcine tongue (n=36, fully crossed 2/3/4 W × CW/PW), dual-wavelength 450/808 nm Wiser 3 (Doctor Smile) — found the pooled (across all three power levels) PW damage was numerically *greater* than CW for both epithelium (1.45 ± 0.64 mm vs. 1.22 ± 0.45 mm, p=0.234) and connective tissue (1.65 ± 0.73 mm vs. 1.49 ± 0.46 mm, p=0.444); neither difference reached the source's own stated significance threshold (p ≤ 0.10).

### Not usable as CW-vs-PW evidence (confounded or off-target)

- FACT: [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]] compares an 808 nm diode laser in CW mode (2 W) against a 532 nm KTP laser in PW mode (1.5 W) — device, wavelength, and mode all differ simultaneously, so any difference (or lack of one) in margin damage between its diode and KTP arms cannot be attributed to pulse mode specifically. This vault does not use Romeo et al. as CW-vs-PW evidence.
- FACT: [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]] tests only two pulsed settings of the same 980 nm diode ("3.5 W PW" and "3.5 W Boost PW") — no CW arm exists for the diode in this source. It shows that thermal-damage extent can differ substantially between two settings both labeled "PW" (456.15 μm vs. 626.82 μm), which is a caution against treating "PW" as a single homogeneous category, but it says nothing about PW vs. CW.

## Margin recommendations

### ≥5 mm cluster (in vivo / real diagnostic-biopsy pathway)

- FACT: [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]] — retrospective series of 608 real human specimens, 808 nm diode CW — empirically derived its ≥5 mm in vivo specimen-diameter recommendation from a size-graded diagnostic-failure gradient actually observed in real biopsies (63.63% unreadable at 2 mm, 37.71% at 3 mm, 0% unreadable at ≥4 mm), with the extra margin added to allow for post-excision tissue shrinkage.
- FACT: [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]] — prospective diagnostic series of 30 real human specimens, 980 nm diode CW — used a standardized 5 mm circumferential margin a priori and achieved 100% diagnostic yield.
- FACT: [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] — real human biopsy pathway, 980 nm diode — independently recommends a minimum 5 mm normal-tissue margin "if a diode laser with a power setting of 3W or lesser is used," based on its own measured necrotic-border width, though this source does not report a quantified diagnostic-yield percentage.

### ≥2 mm recommendation (ex vivo, no diagnosis rendered)

- FACT: [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] — ex vivo porcine tongue, no diagnosis rendered — recommends a smaller minimum margin of ≥2 mm in both CW and PW, "especially in excisional biopsies of lesions suspected of malignancy," based on 2 W CW showing the least thermal-damage extent among its six tested groups.

### Why these are not directly interchangeable

- FACT: The ≥5 mm sources (Angiero et al., Gambino et al., Gundlapalle et al.) are all real human biopsy pathways; two of three (Angiero et al., Gambino et al.) tie their recommendation to an actually-rendered histopathologic diagnosis outcome, not thermal-damage magnitude alone. The ≥2 mm source (Pergolini et al.) is ex vivo, renders no diagnosis at all, and derives its number purely from measured thermal-damage extent at the single setting (2 W CW) found least damaging in its own ex vivo model.
- INTERPRETATION: The two clusters are answering related but distinct questions — the ≥5 mm figures address "what margin, in real excised human tissue, is associated with reliable histologic diagnosis," while the ≥2 mm figure addresses "what margin covers the thermal-damage zone measured ex vivo at one power/mode combination," without any diagnostic-outcome check. This vault does not treat ≥2 mm as a lower, safely-substitutable version of ≥5 mm, nor treat ≥5 mm as simply confirming Pergolini et al. is too conservative — the two numbers were derived from different endpoints and different tissue conditions.
- INTERPRETATION (source's own, reproduced from [[06 Synthesis/Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Quality]], not independently tested here): Pergolini et al.'s own Discussion attributes possible underestimation of thermal effects in its ex vivo model to reduced vascularization in deceased tissue relative to living tissue — a plausible reason an ex vivo-derived margin would be smaller than an in vivo-derived one, but this explanation itself is untested by any vault source and is not treated as resolving the discrepancy.

## Set power vs delivered power

- FACT: All six full-text-processed dental-diode biopsy-relevant sources — [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]], [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]], [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]], [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]], [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]], [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] — plus the reused background source [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters|Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. 2020]], report set/console power only. None independently verifies delivered output with a calibrated power meter at the point of tissue contact. See [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]].
- FACT: The vault's separate power-output evidence base ([[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]], [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]], synthesized in [[06 Synthesis/Current Evidence on Diode Laser Power Output]]) establishes that measured/delivered output deviates from set/console power in a way that is nonzero for every tested device, and that both the magnitude and the *direction* (above vs. below the set value) of that deviation are device-dependent — Parker et al. 2022 and Zegaib et al. 2011 found output mainly below panel settings; Xue et al. 2022 and Kim et al. 2020 found deviations in both directions depending on device and mode, with Kim et al. reproducing the mixed-direction pattern within one study using one shared power meter and protocol.
- FACT: No device named in the biopsy-relevant sources overlaps with any device named in the vault's power-deviation sources. The biopsy devices are: DMT Milan 808 nm (Angiero et al.), DenMat SOL 808 nm / DEKA SmartLite KTP 532 nm (Romeo et al.), LITEMEDICS 980 nm (Azevedo et al.), Raffaello Laser DMT 980 nm (Gambino et al.), Zolar Plus 980 nm (Gundlapalle et al.), and Wiser 3 (Doctor Smile) dual 450/808 nm (Pergolini et al.). The power-deviation sources' named devices are SIROLaser Blue and Picasso (Xue et al. 2022) and K2 mobile, Epic 10 TM, and Saeshin (Kim et al. 2020); Zegaib et al. 2011 studied a single named Biolase 810 nm unit; Parker et al. 2022 tested 20 device types but does not individually name them in this vault's extraction, so overlap with Parker et al.'s device list specifically is unconfirmed rather than ruled out.
- INTERPRETATION: Because no biopsy-relevant device has an independently measured output value anywhere in this vault, none of the thermal-damage, margin, or diagnostic-yield findings above can be corrected for real delivered power — not even approximately — since the sign and magnitude of the set-vs-measured gap is itself device-dependent and unknown for each specific device used. A biopsy source reporting "2 W CW" should be read as "the effect observed at a 2 W console setting on this device," not as "the effect of 2 W of power actually incident on the tissue."
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (preserved from [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]] and [[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]]): even the vault's own measured-output evidence carries an unresolved internal inconsistency — Zegaib et al. 2011's own stated "mean PO loss of 54%" figure does not match the ~46% implied by its own Table 1 arithmetic. This is not corrected here, and it means the general "set ≠ measured, device-dependent" conclusion should itself be read with some caution, independent of the biopsy-specific device-overlap gap described above.

- INTERPRETATION: No two of the three genuine CW-vs-PW sources (Gundlapalle et al., Romanos et al., Pergolini et al.) share device, wavelength, and specimen type together — the closest pair (Gundlapalle et al. and Pergolini et al.) still differs in device, wavelength, and tissue model (real human mucosa vs. deceased porcine tongue). This makes it impossible, with currently processed evidence, to isolate whether the directional conflict is caused by species/tissue vascularization (Pergolini et al.'s own suggested factor), wavelength/device optical properties, or the specific power level tested (Gundlapalle et al. and Romanos et al. each used one fixed power; Pergolini et al. pooled across three).
- INTERPRETATION: The margin-recommendation split (≥5 mm vs. ≥2 mm) tracks cleanly with the ex vivo/no-diagnosis vs. in vivo/real-diagnosis split (Pergolini et al. is the only ex vivo, no-diagnosis source among the four margin-recommending sources), which is a candidate explanation already noted in [[06 Synthesis/Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Quality]] — but candidate, not confirmed, since only one ex vivo source exists to compare against three in vivo sources.
- FACT: Histological outcome definitions differ across every source — "diagnostic yield" (Angiero et al., Romeo et al., Gambino et al.) is a categorical readable/unreadable or certainty/no-certainty judgment by a pathologist; "ETTD" (Azevedo et al.) and "thermal damage" (Gambino et al., Gundlapalle et al., Pergolini et al.) are linear distance measurements with different magnifications and software; "penetration depth" (Romanos et al.) and "lost-tissue/necrosis area" (Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.) are yet other outcome definitions again. These are not pooled or converted into a common unit anywhere in this vault.

## What is genuinely contradictory

- CONFIRMED CONTRADICTION: The direction of the CW-vs-PW thermal-damage effect. [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] and [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth|Romanos et al. 2022]] both report PW causing less thermal effect than CW; [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] reports the opposite trend (not statistically significant). All three are comparing the same general mode variable (CW vs. PW) at broadly comparable power ranges, so this is a genuine, unresolved directional disagreement — not merely a difference in what was measured.
- CONFIRMED CONTRADICTION: The recommended safety margin. Three sources (Angiero et al., Gambino et al., Gundlapalle et al.) independently converge on ≥5 mm; one source (Pergolini et al.) recommends ≥2 mm. The numeric recommendations genuinely differ, even though (see below) part of the difference may trace to non-comparable study conditions.

## What is only non-comparable

- NOT A CONTRADICTION (confounded comparison): Romeo et al.'s diode-CW vs. KTP-PW comparison cannot be used as evidence for or against a CW-vs-PW effect, because wavelength and laser type differ simultaneously with mode.
- NOT A CONTRADICTION (no CW arm exists): Azevedo et al.'s PW vs. Boost PW comparison shows variability within the "PW" label but does not bear on the CW-vs-PW question at all.
- NOT A CONTRADICTION (different outcome measures): Romanos et al.'s "penetration depth" and Gundlapalle et al./Pergolini et al.'s "thermal-damage width"/ETTD are different measurement definitions in different tissue models (bovine periodontal gingiva vs. human oral mucosa vs. porcine tongue); their directional agreement or disagreement is suggestive but not a like-for-like numeric comparison.
- NOT A CONTRADICTION (no shared device): The biopsy sources' set-power-only reporting and the vault's measured-output evidence (Parker et al., Xue et al., Zegaib et al., Kim et al.) do not disagree with each other — they simply share no common device, so there is nothing to compare directly. This is an evidentiary gap, not a disagreement.
- PARTIALLY NON-COMPARABLE: The ≥5 mm vs. ≥2 mm margin figures, while numerically contradictory as stated recommendations (see above), are derived from different underlying endpoints (in vivo diagnostic reliability vs. ex vivo thermal-damage magnitude at one setting, with no diagnosis rendered) — so the disagreement is real as a stated number, but its interpretation as "the same question, two different answers" is only partly justified.

## Research questions generated by these contradictions

- Does the CW-vs-PW thermal-damage direction reverse (or hold) when species/tissue model, device, and wavelength are each held constant in turn — i.e., is the Gundlapalle/Romanos-vs-Pergolini conflict a tissue-model artifact, a power-level artifact, or a genuine device/wavelength-dependent effect?
- Would Pergolini et al.'s ≥2 mm ex vivo-derived margin still achieve 100% diagnostic yield if tested in an actual in vivo diagnostic-biopsy pathway, as Angiero et al., Gambino et al., and Gundlapalle et al.'s ≥5 mm figures were (in Angiero et al.'s case, empirically) associated with?
- Does "penetration depth" (Romanos et al.'s outcome) correlate with histologic "thermal-damage width"/ETTD (Gundlapalle et al./Pergolini et al.'s outcome) when both are measured in the same specimen and tissue type?
- What is the actual delivered power, independently measured, for any of the six specific devices used in the vault's biopsy-relevant sources (DMT Milan, DenMat SOL, LITEMEDICS, Raffaello Laser DMT, Zolar Plus, Wiser 3) — without this, no biopsy finding in this vault can be corrected for real tissue-incident power?
- Does the size/margin threshold for diagnostic yield (≥4–5 mm, per [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]]) interact with CW-vs-PW mode choice — i.e., does mode change the minimum margin needed for reliable diagnosis, or are the two variables independent? No source in this vault tests both together.

## สรุปภาษาไทย

- บันทึกนี้เป็น **PROVISIONAL** (ผลชั่วคราว) สร้างจากเฉพาะวรรณกรรม หลักฐาน และข้อมูลสกัดเรื่อง biopsy ที่ประมวลผลแล้วในคลังนี้เท่านั้น ไม่มีการค้นหาหรือเพิ่มวรรณกรรมใหม่ และไม่มีข้อเสนอแนะทางคลินิกใด ๆ ในบันทึกนี้
- **ข้อขัดแย้งที่ 1 (PW เทียบ CW):** Gundlapalle et al. (ชิ้นเนื้อ biopsy จริงในมนุษย์) และ Romanos et al. (ex vivo) พบว่า PW ทำให้เกิดความเสียหายจากความร้อนน้อยกว่า CW แต่ Pergolini et al. (ex vivo อีกเช่นกัน) พบแนวโน้มตรงกันข้าม (ไม่มีนัยสำคัญทางสถิติ) — ทั้งสามงานวิจัยแตกต่างกันแทบทุกด้าน (อุปกรณ์ ความยาวคลื่น ชนิดเนื้อเยื่อ) จึงยังไม่สามารถระบุสาเหตุของความขัดแย้งได้ ข้อมูลของ Romeo et al. และ Azevedo et al. **ใช้เป็นหลักฐาน CW-เทียบ-PW ไม่ได้** เนื่องจากมีตัวแปรอื่นปนอยู่
- **ข้อขัดแย้งที่ 2 (ขอบตัด):** Angiero, Gambino, และ Gundlapalle (ทั้งหมดเป็นการตรวจชิ้นเนื้อจริงในมนุษย์) แนะนำขอบตัด ≥5 มม. ขณะที่ Pergolini (ex vivo ไม่มีการวินิจฉัยจริง) แนะนำเพียง ≥2 มม. — ตัวเลขทั้งสองกลุ่มไม่ควรใช้แทนกันได้โดยตรง เพราะได้มาจากคนละหลักเกณฑ์ (ความน่าเชื่อถือในการวินิจฉัยจริง เทียบกับขนาดความเสียหายที่วัดได้ ex vivo)
- **ข้อขัดแย้งที่ 3 (กำลังที่ตั้ง เทียบ กำลังที่ส่งจริง):** งานวิจัยเรื่อง biopsy ทั้งหมดในคลังนี้รายงานเฉพาะค่ากำลังที่ตั้งเครื่อง (set power) เท่านั้น ไม่มีการวัดกำลังขาออกจริงด้วยเพาเวอร์มิเตอร์เลย และอุปกรณ์ที่ใช้ในงานวิจัย biopsy ก็ไม่ตรงกับอุปกรณ์ใด ๆ ที่มีข้อมูลกำลังขาออกจริงในคลังนี้ ดังนั้นจึงไม่สามารถแก้ไขหรือประมาณค่ากำลังที่ส่งจริงให้กับผลการศึกษา biopsy ใด ๆ ได้เลย
- ข้อขัดแย้งที่ชัดเจนที่สุดคือทิศทางของผล PW เทียบ CW ส่วนข้อขัดแย้งเรื่องขอบตัดส่วนหนึ่งอาจอธิบายได้ด้วยความแตกต่างระหว่าง ex vivo กับ in vivo (ยังเป็นเพียงคำอธิบายที่เป็นไปได้ ไม่ใช่ข้อสรุปที่ยืนยันแล้ว)
- ปัญหาการวัดผลลัพธ์ที่ต่างกัน (เช่น penetration depth เทียบกับ thermal-damage width) และการไม่มีอุปกรณ์ร่วมกันระหว่างหลักฐานเรื่อง biopsy กับหลักฐานเรื่องกำลังขาออกจริง ถูกจัดเป็น "ไม่สามารถเปรียบเทียบกันได้" ไม่ใช่ "ข้อขัดแย้ง"
