# Incision Speed and Movement in Diode Laser Tissue Interaction

**Status: ACTIVE.** This synthesis treats [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Incision Speed|incision speed (ความเร็วในการตัด)]] / movement as an independent exposure variable in the current RQ: operating parameters + independently measured delivered power + incision speed/movement → thermal tissue damage and biopsy-oriented histopathologic specimen quality. It is built from the structured extractions below and does not infer speed from procedure or excision time.

## Why speed/movement matters

FACT: Incision speed is the rate of fiber-tip advancement through tissue, distinct from power, exposure time, and procedure/excision time. [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Incision Speed]] [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Exposure Time]]

INTERPRETATION: Speed/movement must be recorded with delivered power and beam/fiber conditions because it changes the exposure conditions at a tissue location. This is a conceptual exposure relationship, not a back-calculated dose or a claim that speed alone determines tissue outcome. [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Power-to-Speed Relationship]]

## Control, measurement, and qualitative guidance are not interchangeable

- **Mechanized speed** is directly documented in Hanke, Strakas, and Goharkhay. It standardizes movement within each study but does not establish an effect of speed when speed is fixed.
- **Clinician-controlled movement** is reported by Isola as a visually evaluated 2.5 mm/s target. Al-Ani reports a fixed 0.75 mm/s performed by one experienced clinician, but the source does not state a control or verification mechanism; it remains `UNKNOWN`, not upgraded to mechanized or measured.
- **Measured speed** is documented by Isola as Sinc, calculated from field perimeter and incision time by a single observer. It is a study outcome, separate from the operator's 2.5 mm/s target; the paper does not reconcile the two values.
- **Descriptive/qualitative speed guidance** is not controlled speed. Romanos reports a qualitative speed–collateral-damage pattern from an illustrative speed sweep. Verified manufacturer documents summarized in [[06 Synthesis/Preset Reliance and Technical Misuse in Dental Laser Use]] advise regulating fiber-tip speed or warn against moving too slowly, but supply no numeric mm/s or dwell-time protocol.
- **Not reported / unclear** remains a finding. Prado reports manual cutting and excision time, not speed. No speed is inferred from length, exposure duration, or procedure time.

## What was varied versus held constant

FACT: Hanke, Strakas, Goharkhay, and Al-Ani hold a reported speed constant within their experimental designs. Their thermal and histologic outcomes cannot be attributed to speed differences.

FACT: Romanos is the only current vault source that reports multiple tested speeds. Its source-level conclusion is qualitative: decreasing speed increased cutting depth, collateral damage, and carbonization for standard fixed-power diode lasers. This does not provide quantified oral-biopsy evidence and is not pooled with the primary ex-vivo studies. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2013 - Diode Laser Soft-Tissue Surgery Review Data]]

FACT: Isola compares diode laser with cold blade and reports a measured incision-speed outcome, but does not experimentally vary speed within the diode arm. Its histology is qualitative and does not test speed against thermal damage, margin readability, architecture preservation, or specimen interpretability.

## Thermal damage and specimen-quality implications

FACT: At fixed speed, Hanke, Strakas, and Goharkhay link characterized laser operation to histometric tissue outcomes; Al-Ani links a reported fixed speed to quantified thermal/margin-adjacent histology. None establishes a speed-response relationship because their speed is fixed.

FACT: No current vault source varies diode-laser speed while measuring biopsy-oriented specimen quality: margin readability, tissue architecture preservation, or specimen interpretability.

INTERPRETATION: Speed/movement is therefore an exposure variable that the current RQ should control and report alongside independently measured delivered power. It is not yet an independently demonstrated determinant of biopsy-oriented specimen quality in the vault.

## Remaining gap and RQ support

The current evidence supports an ex vivo oral-soft-tissue design that independently measures delivered power and explicitly controls or measures speed/movement while separately assessing thermal damage and biopsy-oriented specimen quality. The unresolved question is whether variation in speed/movement changes either outcome, and whether any thermal change corresponds to margin readability, tissue architecture preservation, or specimen interpretability.

HYPOTHESIS: Under otherwise controlled conditions, speed/movement may modify tissue exposure and therefore thermal and specimen-quality outcomes. This requires testing; the current vault does not establish its direction or magnitude.

## สรุปภาษาไทย

- Incision speed (ความเร็วในการตัด) / movement เป็นตัวแปรการสัมผัสเนื้อเยื่อที่ต้องบันทึกร่วมกับกำลังขาออกที่วัดได้จริงและลักษณะการส่งผ่านลำแสง แต่ไม่ใช่ค่าที่อนุมานได้จาก procedure time หรือ excision time
- Hanke 2021, Strakas 2023 และ Goharkhay 1999 ควบคุมความเร็วด้วยกลไก; Al-Ani 2024 รายงานค่า 0.75 mm/s แต่กลไกการควบคุม/ตรวจสอบยัง `UNKNOWN`; Isola 2018 รายงานทั้งค่าเป้าหมายและค่าความเร็วที่วัดได้ซึ่งเป็นคนละค่า
- งานส่วนใหญ่ตรึงความเร็วไว้ จึงยังไม่พิสูจน์ผลของความเร็วต่อ thermal damage หรือ biopsy-oriented specimen quality โดยตรง
- ช่องว่างคือการทดสอบความเร็ว/การเคลื่อนไหวร่วมกับกำลังที่วัดได้จริง แล้วประเมิน thermal damage และคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแยกกันในแบบจำลอง ex vivo เนื้อเยื่ออ่อนในช่องปาก
