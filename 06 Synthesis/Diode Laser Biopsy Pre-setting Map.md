**No "optimal setting" is claimed anywhere in this note.** Per vault instruction, only "evidence-supported range" is used, and only where the evidence directly supports it — see [[#9. Whether any evidence-supported pre-setting range can currently be proposed]].

**Framing note (2026-09-13), following [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]'s reframing to an ex vivo experimental study:** the sources below fall into three distinct roles that this note does not pool into one number:

- **CLINICAL** — human in vivo diagnostic-biopsy sources (Angiero, Romeo, Gambino, Gundlapalle, Palaia, Suter, Shnawa, Capodiferro). Their set-power ranges describe what clinicians used in real patients, not experimentally measured delivered power.
- **EX VIVO EXPERIMENTAL** — non-diagnostic ex vivo/animal sources (Azevedo, Pergolini, Goharkhay, Li, Al-Ani 2023/2024, Merigo, Spille). These are the tissue-model precedents closest to this project's own design.
- **TECHNICAL/MEASURED-OUTPUT** — sources whose main contribution is a measurement-design precedent rather than a biopsy or lesion outcome (Wilder-Smith 1995, CO2; Hanke 2021 and Strakas 2023, diode — see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]], "Direct technical precedent: Hanke and Strakas").

A clinical set-power range (e.g. the 1.6–2.7 W CW band in §9 below) is **not experimentally equivalent to a measured-delivered-power range** — no clinical source in this table independently verifies output. Where an ex vivo measured-output study (Hanke 2021, Strakas 2023) exists, it may provide better methodological guidance for this project's experimental design than direct transfer from the clinical literature, even though its outcome framework is not yet biopsy-oriented. Clinical figures below remain useful as outcome context (what specimen quality mattered for diagnosis), not as this project's own experimental target range.

## 1. Preset ranges associated with acceptable biopsy quality

- FACT: The four sources with a quantified diagnostic-yield outcome at or near 100% — [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]] (100% at ≥4 mm), [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]] (100%, diode arm 2 W CW), [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]] (100%, 2 W CW), and — added this update — [[07 Data/Palaia et al 2021 - 445 nm Diode Laser In Vivo Biopsy Thermal Effect Data|Palaia et al. 2021]] (100%, n=42, 2.5 W CW) — all used **continuous-wave mode**, an 808, 980, or 445 nm diode, a fiber diameter of 320 μm (300 μm for Gambino et al.), and a set power within **1.6–2.7 W**. Palaia et al. 2021 is the largest of the three single-fixed-setting sources (n=42) and sits near the upper bound of this range. [[07 Data/Shnawa et al 2025 - Diode Laser vs Scalpel Oral Fibroma Excision Data|Shnawa et al. 2025]] (added 2026-09-13) used 2 W CW, 980 nm, 300 μm fiber, and a real human diode excisional-biopsy pathway (n=24), squarely inside this same range — but reports only a qualitative diagnostic-quality statement, not a quantified yield percentage, so it is consistent with, but does not itself add a fifth quantified-yield data point to, this range.
- INTERPRETATION: This vault's evidence is consistent with an evidence-supported CW set-power range of roughly **1.6–2.7 W** (445/808/980 nm, 300–320 μm fiber) being compatible with acceptable-to-100% diagnostic yield, *provided* specimen size/margin was adequate in the same studies (≥4–5 mm where reported; not numerically reported by Palaia et al. 2021). This is not evidence that the power range itself is the cause of acceptable quality — see [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]], which already concludes that specimen size/margin, not power, is the variable most consistently associated with diagnostic outcome.
- INTERPRETATION: among the three sources using a single, unvarying CW set power with a quantified ~100% yield (Romeo et al. 2 W, Gambino et al. 2 W, Palaia et al. 2.5 W), the values cluster more tightly in a **2.0–2.5 W** band, nested inside Angiero et al.'s wider 1.6–2.7 W series-wide range. See [[#9. Whether any evidence-supported pre-setting range can currently be proposed]] for the full caveats on this narrower band — it is descriptive, not causal or optimal.
- FACT: [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] used a higher set power (3 W, both CW and PW) and reports only a qualitative statement of correct diagnosis, not a quantified percentage; [[07 Data/Suter et al 2010 - CO2 vs Diode Laser Biopsy Thermal Damage Data (Abstract-Level)|Suter et al. 2010]]'s diode arm used a higher set power still (5.12 W, pulsed) and reports no diagnostic-yield outcome at all. Neither can extend the 1.6–2.7 W range above with the same evidentiary strength as the four quantified-yield sources.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: No source in this table varies power alone while holding specimen size/margin, wavelength, and fiber diameter constant, so the 1.6–2.7 W range above should be read as "the range in which acceptable outcomes were observed," not as a range validated as causally sufficient or necessary for acceptable quality.
- Ex vivo sources ([[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]], [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]]) render no diagnosis at all and therefore cannot contribute to a "diagnostic quality"-anchored range; their power values (3.5 W PW; 2–4 W CW/PW) are noted separately below as thermal-damage data only, not diagnostic-quality data.

## 2. Parameters associated with greater thermal artifact

- FACT: Within [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters|Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. 2020]] (non-biopsy background source), lost-tissue area, thermal-damage area, and necrosis area were all higher at 2.0 W set power than at 0.5 W, regardless of wavelength.
- FACT: Within [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]], the higher-intensity "Boost" PW setting produced greater ETTD (626.82 μm) than the standard PW setting at the same nominal 3.5 W label (456.15 μm) — a within-device gradient with increasing pulsed-setting intensity.
- FACT: Within [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]], raw thermal-damage minima/maxima increased with power (epithelium 0.62 mm at 2 W CW up to 3.12 mm at 4 W PW), but the source's own formal statistical test of power level alone (pooling CW/PW, 2/3/4 W) found **no significant difference** in either epithelial (p=0.765) or connective-tissue (p=0.652) damage.
- INTERPRETATION: The association between higher power/intensity setting and greater thermal-damage magnitude is weak and inconsistently supported — raw values trend upward with power in two sources, but the only source that formally tested power across three levels with a significance test found no significant effect. This vault cannot state a general power → thermal-damage-severity curve for biopsy-relevant diode settings.
- CONFIRMED within-source conflict (preserved, not resolved): in Azevedo et al., the setting with *more* ETTD (Boost, 626.82 μm) had *lower* carbonization (1.80) than the setting with *less* ETTD (standard PW, 456.15 μm, carbonization 2.50) — greater linear thermal-damage extent did not track with greater carbonization within this device.
- INTERPRETATION: Mode (CW vs. PW) cannot currently be listed as a parameter reliably "associated with greater thermal artifact" in either direction — see [[#4. CW vs PW patterns]] below for the unresolved directional conflict.
- FACT (new, this update, supporting/non-diagnostic source): [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data|Goharkhay et al. 1999]] tested 7 measured power levels from 0.5–4.5 W (810 nm, ex vivo pig mandible, ~200 incisions) and found collateral thermal-damage-zone width depended on **neither** average power, nor mode (CW vs. two pulsed settings), nor fiber-tip diameter — while incision *depth* correlated strongly and positively with power. This directly corroborates, in direction, [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]]'s non-significant pooled power-level finding, and is a second independent ex vivo source (different species-tissue preparation, different wavelength) finding no power-dependence of damage-zone width — further weakening the informal "higher power → more damage" pattern suggested by Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. and Azevedo et al.
- FACT (new, this update): [[07 Data/Suter et al 2010 - CO2 vs Diode Laser Biopsy Thermal Damage Data (Abstract-Level)|Suter et al. 2010]] found significantly greater thermal damage with its 5.12 W pulsed diode arm than with either of its CO2 arms — but this is a cross-laser-*type* comparison (diode vs. CO2), not a within-diode power or mode comparison, so it cannot be used to test whether higher diode power specifically increases thermal artifact.
- FACT (new, this update, supporting/non-diagnostic source): [[07 Data/Li et al 2022 - Diode vs Er-YAG vs Cold Scalpel Mice Oral Mucosa Data|Li et al. 2022]] (mouse oral-mucosa wound model, diode 3.0 W pulsed) found an "obvious carbonization layer" and deeper/more numerous apoptotic cells with the diode laser than with an Er:YAG comparator — qualitative corroboration, from an independent research group and an independent tissue model, that this vault's diode sources tend to show more thermal effect than non-diode comparators tested alongside them, but not a quantified power-to-damage relationship.
- FACT (new, this update, n=1 outlier): [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2009 - Diode Laser Chondroid Lipoma Case Report Data|Capodiferro et al. 2009]] found *no* measurable margin thermal alteration at 2.5 W CW (880 nm) — the only zero-damage data point in this vault's diode biopsy literature. As a single case, this does not shift the weak power → thermal-damage association discussed above, but it is preserved as a genuine finding, not smoothed away.
- INTERPRETATION, suggestively yes, not confirmed: wavelength may also modify thermal-damage magnitude and compartment pattern. FACT: [[07 Data/Palaia et al 2021 - 445 nm Diode Laser In Vivo Biopsy Thermal Effect Data|Palaia et al. 2021]] (445 nm) reported a mean epithelial thermal effect (507.07 μm) numerically above the entire diode-only margin-damage range reported by [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]] (808 nm, 149–473 μm) and above [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]]'s mean (808 nm, 282.8 μm); the source's own Discussion makes this same comparison and attributes it to wavelength. FACT: Palaia et al. 2021 (445 nm) found epithelium > connective-tissue damage, the *opposite* compartment pattern from [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]] (980 nm), which found connective tissue > epithelium. INTERPRETATION: both observations are consistent with wavelength-dependent chromophore absorption (shorter, hemoglobin-absorbed 445 nm depositing energy more superficially than longer 980 nm), but device, power, fluence, and lesion mix all differ between these studies too, so this is not a controlled wavelength comparison and remains NEEDS VERIFICATION.
- FACT (SUPPORTING, non-diagnostic context): [[07 Data/Merigo et al 2012 - Five-Wavelength Ex Vivo Thermal and Histologic Comparison Data|Merigo et al. 2012]]'s 5 W diode CW setting produced the single largest combined (superficial+deep) thermal rise (~40 °C) of nine tested wavelength/power combinations in ex vivo bovine tongue, and its 3 W diode setting was the single slowest excision (764 s) of the nine — directionally consistent with, but not quantitatively poolable with, this vault's existing μm-scale margin-damage figures (different outcome measure: temperature rise, not histologic margin width).

## 3. Parameters associated with better margin readability

- FACT: Specimen size/excision margin ≥4–5 mm is the parameter most consistently associated with margin/diagnostic readability across independent sources: [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]] (empirically derived, size-graded), [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]] (a priori 5 mm, 100% success), and [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] (independently recommends ≥5 mm at ≤3 W).
- FACT: [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] reports better pathologist-graded slide quality for PW than CW at matched 3 W, in a real human biopsy pathway — the only source in this table that directly scores "slide quality" (as distinct from diagnosis or thermal-damage width) by mode.
- FACT: Fiber diameter is 300–320 μm in every source that reports it (Angiero et al., Romeo et al., Gambino et al., Pergolini et al.); Azevedo et al. and Gundlapalle et al. do not report it. No source tests a different fiber diameter as a comparator, so fiber diameter's association with margin readability cannot be evaluated — it is invariant, not tested.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: Tip-initiation status is UNKNOWN in six of the nine core biopsy sources as of this update (previously seven of eight); [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] and — added 2026-09-13 — [[07 Data/Shnawa et al 2025 - Diode Laser vs Scalpel Oral Fibroma Excision Data|Shnawa et al. 2025]] both report "initiated," and neither has a non-initiated comparator within its own study. No biopsy source in this vault can currently support a claim linking tip-initiation status to margin readability.

## 4. CW vs PW patterns

- CONFIRMED CONTRADICTION (full detail in [[06 Synthesis/Contradictions in Diode Laser Biopsy Evidence]]): [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] (real human biopsy, matched 3 W) found PW causes less thermal damage than CW (208.766 μm vs. 279.84 μm) and better slide quality; [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] (ex vivo, pooled 2/3/4 W) found the opposite, non-significant trend (PW numerically greater than CW for both tissue compartments).
- FACT: [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]]'s diode-CW-vs-KTP-PW comparison and [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]]'s PW-vs-Boost-PW comparison are **not usable** as CW-vs-PW evidence — the former confounds mode with wavelength/laser type, the latter has no CW arm at all.
- INTERPRETATION: This vault cannot currently state a general direction of effect for CW vs. PW mode on thermal artifact or margin quality in dental-diode oral biopsy. The genuine within-mode comparisons now number three rather than two, and disagree in three different ways rather than two — see next point — and none reports a quantified diagnostic-yield percentage, so no mode can be linked to diagnostic outcome directly.
- FACT (new, this update): [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data|Goharkhay et al. 1999]] adds a **third** genuine within-device CW-vs-PW comparison (ex vivo pig mandible, 810 nm, 7 matched power levels 0.5–4.5 W) and finds **no consistent direction of difference** between CW and either of two pulsed settings at any power level — neither "PW < CW" (Gundlapalle et al.) nor "PW > CW, non-significant" (Pergolini et al.), but no detectable mode-dependence at all. [[07 Data/Suter et al 2010 - CO2 vs Diode Laser Biopsy Thermal Damage Data (Abstract-Level)|Suter et al. 2010]]'s diode arm has no CW comparator and [[07 Data/Palaia et al 2021 - 445 nm Diode Laser In Vivo Biopsy Thermal Effect Data|Palaia et al. 2021]] is CW-only, so neither adds CW-vs-PW information. This vault's CW-vs-PW evidence base is therefore now three-way discordant, not merely two-way conflicting.

## 5. Fiber/tip-related patterns

- FACT: Tip-initiation status is UNKNOWN in six of nine core biopsy sources as of this update (Angiero et al., Romeo et al., Azevedo et al., Gambino et al., Gundlapalle et al., Palaia et al., Suter et al. — seven listed, minus the two now reporting a value); [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] and [[07 Data/Shnawa et al 2025 - Diode Laser vs Scalpel Oral Fibroma Excision Data|Shnawa et al. 2025]] (added 2026-09-13) both report "initiated," neither with a non-initiated comparator in the same study.
- FACT: Contact mode is reported as "contact" in Azevedo et al., Gambino et al., and Pergolini et al.; UNKNOWN in Angiero et al., Romeo et al., Gundlapalle et al., Palaia et al., and Suter et al.
- FACT (supporting, non-biopsy source, reused for context only): [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth|Romanos et al. 2022]] directly tested initiated vs. non-initiated tips and found a within-source inconsistency — its Abstract states initiated tips showed *lower* penetration depth than non-initiated, while its own Table 1 and Discussion show the opposite wherever a significant difference was found. This is preserved, not resolved, and cannot be used to fill the tip-initiation gap in the biopsy literature itself.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: No processed oral soft-tissue biopsy source in this vault directly compares initiated vs. non-initiated tips against a biopsy-relevant outcome (thermal damage, margin readability, diagnostic yield). This is a confirmed reporting gap, not a resolved-but-unfavorable finding.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION, cross-source caveat: [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2013 - Diode Laser Soft-Tissue Surgery Review Data|Romanos et al. 2013]] independently characterizes the "articulating paper" initiation method used by [[07 Data/Shnawa et al 2025 - Diode Laser vs Scalpel Oral Fibroma Excision Data|Shnawa et al. 2025]] as one of two "non-standardized techniques" that manufacturers suggest but that "do not guarantee consistent initiation, nor does the initiation last longer than a few seconds while cutting tissue" — this is Romanos's own general caution about the technique class, not a claim that Shnawa et al.'s specific procedure failed, but it tempers how much confidence this vault places in "initiated" as a stable, verified device state for the full duration of Shnawa et al.'s excisions.
- INTERPRETATION: Fiber diameter (300–320 μm where reported) is uniform enough across sources that it cannot explain any of the disagreements identified in this note — it is not a candidate variable for the CW/PW or margin-recommendation conflicts, since it does not vary meaningfully between the disagreeing sources.

## 6. Specimen-size effects

- FACT: Diagnostic failure is sharply size-graded in [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]]: 63.63% unreadable at 2 mm, 37.71% at 3 mm, 0% unreadable at ≥4 mm (n=608).
- FACT: [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]] (5–10 mm lesions) and [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]] (standardized 5 mm margin) both report 100% diagnostic success, independently converging with Angiero et al.'s ≥4–5 mm threshold.
- INTERPRETATION: Specimen size/margin (≥4–5 mm) is the single strongest and most consistent parameter-to-outcome relationship anywhere in this table — stronger and more consistent than any power, mode, or fiber-related pattern identified above. This reproduces the standing conclusion of [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]] and [[06 Synthesis/Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Quality]].
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: No source varies specimen size and power/mode independently in the same design, so the relative contribution of size vs. power/mode to diagnostic outcome remains untested.

## 7. Where studies disagree

- CW-vs-PW thermal-damage direction: [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] (PW < CW) vs. [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] (PW > CW, not significant) — unresolved, full detail in [[06 Synthesis/Contradictions in Diode Laser Biopsy Evidence]].
- Recommended safety margin: ≥5 mm ([[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]], [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]], [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]]) vs. ≥2 mm ([[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]], ex vivo, no diagnosis rendered) — genuinely different recommendations from different evidentiary bases, not treated as interchangeable.
- Within-source conflict: [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]]'s ETTD and carbonization score move in opposite directions between its two pulsed settings.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (preserved from source data extraction): [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]]'s own narrative text ("t=1.94, p<0.01") and its own Table 2 (p=0.084) disagree for the Group A vs. Group B (2 W CW vs. 2 W PW) epithelial-damage comparison; this vault does not resolve the discrepancy.

## 8. Which parameters are too poorly reported to evaluate

- **Incision speed** — UNKNOWN in all core diagnostic-biopsy sources (still true after this update: Palaia et al. 2021, Suter et al. 2010, Capodiferro et al. 2008/2009, and — added 2026-09-13 — Shnawa et al. 2025 all do not report it; [[07 Data/Spille et al 2026 - Diode Laser Learning-Curve and Target-Based Accuracy Data|Spille et al. 2026]] reports operative time per incision, but per this task's explicit instruction this is not converted into a speed value). Three values now exist in the vault's *supporting/background*, non-diagnostic literature — [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data|Goharkhay et al. 1999]] reports 10 mm/second (ex vivo pig mandible), [[07 Data/Li et al 2022 - Diode vs Er-YAG vs Cold Scalpel Mice Oral Mucosa Data|Li et al. 2022]] reports 1 mm/second (in vivo mouse tongue), and — added 2026-09-13, non-diode and non-oral — [[07 Data/Lu et al 2026 - Dual-Wavelength 980-1470 nm Liver Cutting Parameters Data (Abstract-Level)|Lu et al. 2026]] reports 10 mm/second (in vivo rabbit liver, dual-wavelength 980/1470 nm surgical laser) — but all three come from non-lesional models with different devices, species, and tissue contexts, and none can be applied to any diagnostic-biopsy source or to each other. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2013 - Diode Laser Soft-Tissue Surgery Review Data|Romanos et al. 2013]] (added 2026-09-13, a CE review, not a primary study) adds this vault's most explicit qualitative statement that collateral damage depends on cutting speed for standard diode lasers, illustrated with a five-speed comparison (12.5–0.0 mm/s), but with no numeric damage values and no lesion or diagnosis — it strengthens the directional case for why incision speed matters without providing a quantified data point.
- **Tip-initiation status** — UNKNOWN in seven of eight core sources; only Pergolini et al. reports a value ("initiated"), and without a within-study comparator.
- **Pulse duration and duty cycle** — UNKNOWN in every core diagnostic-biopsy source that uses a pulsed setting (Azevedo et al., Gundlapalle et al., Pergolini et al., Suter et al.'s diode arm); only Pergolini et al. reports frequency (10 kHz). Goharkhay et al. (supporting, non-diagnostic) is the only source in this note reporting both pulse width and frequency for its pulsed settings, and its duty cycle is computed here (INTERPRETATION), not source-stated.
- **Coagulation zone** — not scored or measured as an isolated variable in any of the eight core sources; only qualitative, non-numeric descriptions exist (Angiero et al.: vascular/thrombosis descriptors; Gambino et al.: "hemocoagulative phenomena"; Gundlapalle et al.: physics-mechanism description only; Palaia et al.: photocoagulation mentioned only as a discussion point for vascularized lesions). Suter et al.'s abstract does not mention coagulation at all, nor does Capodiferro et al. 2009. Capodiferro et al. 2008 remains the only identified candidate that scores coagulation specifically, and it is now processed to an abstract-level data extraction ([[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data]]) but still lacks full text to quantify the claim.
- **Calibration method** — UNKNOWN in all nine core sources, including Shnawa et al. 2025 (added 2026-09-13); no core source describes a laser power-meter calibration protocol. Goharkhay et al. (supporting) mentions a wattmeter but not its calibration protocol. Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 (supporting, non-diode) does report a calibration step (beam characteristics conformed to manufacturer specification by a laser engineer immediately before the study) — the most complete calibration description in this vault's oral-mucosa laser literature, but for CO2, not diode, lasers. [[07 Data/Merigo et al 2012 - Five-Wavelength Ex Vivo Thermal and Histologic Comparison Data|Merigo et al. 2012]] (added 2026-09-13, supporting, ex vivo, non-diagnostic) reports a power-meter check (Nova II, Ophir) performed on every device including its diode arm before the experimental protocol, yielding a measured power-loss figure — but pooled across all five tested device types, not diode-isolated, and not paired with a diagnostic outcome.
- **Measured/delivered power** — still unavailable ("No") in every core diagnostic-biopsy source, confirmed in the mapping table above; see [[#Set power vs delivered power caveat]] below. Four supporting, non-diagnostic sources now report genuinely measured output — Goharkhay et al. 1999 (diode, wattmeter), Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 (CO2, calibrated power meter), Merigo et al. 2012 (five device types including one diode arm, power-meter-verified, 18–25% loss pooled across all five), and — added 2026-09-14 — [[07 Data/Prado et al 2022 - Micro vs Super Pulsed Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Prado et al. 2022]] (940 nm diode, power-meter-verified with a distinct numeric measured value reported per setting, ~17% under-delivery) — but none is transferable to any core diagnostic-biopsy source's device: Wilder-Smith et al.'s devices are not diode lasers at all, Merigo et al.'s diode-specific loss percentage cannot be isolated from its pooled figure, and Prado et al.'s device does not match any core diagnostic-biopsy source in this table.
- **Contact/non-contact mode** and **fiber diameter** — reported in roughly half the core sources each; not missing entirely, but too inconsistently reported across sources to support a cross-study comparison. Palaia et al. 2021 reports fiber diameter (320 μm) but not contact mode; Suter et al. 2010 reports neither.

## 9. Whether any evidence-supported pre-setting range can currently be proposed

- INTERPRETATION: A narrow, heavily caveated evidence-supported range can be stated for **CW mode only**: **1.6–2.7 W set power, 445, 808, or 980 nm diode, 300–320 μm fiber**, was the range used by the four sources achieving acceptable-to-100% quantified diagnostic yield in this vault ([[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield|Angiero et al. 2011]], [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data|Romeo et al. 2014]], [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data|Gambino et al. 2026]], and — added this update — [[07 Data/Palaia et al 2021 - 445 nm Diode Laser In Vivo Biopsy Thermal Effect Data|Palaia et al. 2021]]) — but this is contingent on specimen size/margin being adequate in the same studies, not isolated to power, and none of the four independently measured delivered power. This is an **evidence-supported range**, not an optimal setting. A narrower, still-more-caveated **2.0–2.5 W** band describes the three single-fixed-setting sources within it (Romeo et al., Gambino et al., Palaia et al.), confounded by specimen size/margin, wavelength (808/980/445 nm all included), and the complete absence of independently measured delivered power in all three sources — it is a tighter *descriptive* band across better-controlled (fixed-setting) sources, not a narrower causal or optimal range.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: This range should not be extended to PW mode. No PW-mode source in this vault reports a quantified diagnostic-yield percentage; the three PW-relevant sources now disagree in three different ways on the direction (or existence) of a CW-vs-PW effect — [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] (PW < CW), [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] (PW > CW, not significant), and [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data|Goharkhay et al. 1999]] (no consistent difference) — at three different power levels/ranges and three different wavelengths.
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: This range should not be extended to devices, wavelengths, or fiber diameters outside those directly tested (445/808/980 nm, 300–320 μm) — no source in this table tests a fiber diameter outside this narrow band, so its independent contribution is untested, not confirmed favorable. [[07 Data/Suter et al 2010 - CO2 vs Diode Laser Biopsy Thermal Damage Data (Abstract-Level)|Suter et al. 2010]]'s 5.12 W diode arm and [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data|Goharkhay et al. 1999]]'s tested band up to 4.5 W remain outside this range and are not linked to any diagnostic-yield outcome, so they cannot extend it upward.
- What prevents a broader or firmer range from being proposed: (1) specimen size/margin, not power, is the variable most consistently associated with diagnostic outcome, so the power range above is correlational, not isolated as causal; (2) no core diagnostic-biopsy source independently measures delivered power, so "1.6–2.7 W" refers only to console settings, which the vault's separate power-output evidence shows can deviate from actual tissue-incident power by a device-dependent and currently unknown amount for every device in this table (see [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]], [[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]]) — the one source with genuinely measured output (Goharkhay et al. 1999) uses a device found nowhere else in this vault, so it cannot close this gap for any other row; (3) tip-initiation status, incision speed, pulse duration, duty cycle, and calibration method are UNKNOWN across nearly all sources, so their interaction with the proposed range cannot be ruled in or out; (4) the CW-vs-PW conflict is now three-way, not two-way, and the margin-recommendation contradiction (see [[06 Synthesis/Contradictions in Diode Laser Biopsy Evidence]]) means no single range can currently cover both modes.
- This synthesis draws no clinical recommendation. The evidence-supported range stated above describes what has been observed in this vault's processed literature, not a recommended clinical setting.

## Set power vs delivered power caveat

- FACT: All values in the "Set/preset power" column above are console/panel values as reported by each source; none is independently verified by a calibrated power meter at the point of tissue contact. This distinction is preserved throughout this note per [[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]] and [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]], which show that measured/delivered output deviates from set power in a device-dependent, non-uniform way (both magnitude and direction vary by device) for every device independently tested in this vault — none of which overlaps with any device in the mapping table above. See [[06 Synthesis/Contradictions in Diode Laser Biopsy Evidence]] for the full argument.
- FACT (new, this update): [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data|Goharkhay et al. 1999]] is an exception to the pattern above — it reports genuinely wattmeter-measured output power, not a set/console value, and explicitly states the two differ. However, its device (Dentek LD 15, 810 nm) is not used in any other source in this vault, so this measured value cannot be used to infer, correct, or bound the set-vs-measured gap for any device in the core mapping table. Per vault rule, set power is still never treated as delivered power anywhere in this note, including for the sources where delivered power happens to be unknown for a different reason (Goharkhay et al.'s own *set* value is the one that is UNKNOWN here, inverting the usual gap).
- FACT (new, this update): [[07 Data/Wilder-Smith et al 1995 - CO2 Laser Incision and Thermal Damage Data|Wilder-Smith et al. 1995]] is a second exception, also outside the core diagnostic-biopsy table — it independently measured "actual power levels" (1, 4, 12 W) with a calibrated power meter (Gentec PRJ-M) directly before each incision, for three CO2 lasers. This is the clearest measured-power methodology found anywhere in this vault's oral-mucosa laser literature, but because the devices are CO2, not diode, it cannot be transferred to close the gap for any diode source in the core mapping table.
- FACT (new, this update): [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2009 - Diode Laser Chondroid Lipoma Case Report Data|Capodiferro et al. 2009]] (2.5 W CW, n=1) and [[07 Data/Li et al 2022 - Diode vs Er-YAG vs Cold Scalpel Mice Oral Mucosa Data|Li et al. 2022]] (3.0 W pulsed) both report set/console power only, with no independent measurement — reinforcing, not closing, this gap. [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data|Capodiferro et al. 2008]]'s 2–7 W range is also treated as set/nominal per its own abstract wording, though this cannot be fully confirmed without full text.
- FACT (added 2026-09-13): [[07 Data/Shnawa et al 2025 - Diode Laser vs Scalpel Oral Fibroma Excision Data|Shnawa et al. 2025]] (2 W CW, n=24 laser-treated) reports set/console power only, with no independent measurement, reinforcing the gap for the newest core diagnostic-biopsy source. [[07 Data/Merigo et al 2012 - Five-Wavelength Ex Vivo Thermal and Histologic Comparison Data|Merigo et al. 2012]] (supporting, ex vivo) is the closest partial exception found to date for a diode arm: it independently power-meter-verifies output for its GaAlAs 808 nm diode (3/5 W CW) alongside four other laser types, reporting 18–25% loss — but pooled across all five device types, not diode-isolated, and with no diagnosis rendered, so it narrows without closing this gap.
- FACT (added 2026-09-14): [[07 Data/Prado et al 2022 - Micro vs Super Pulsed Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Prado et al. 2022]] (supporting, ex vivo, not diagnostic) independently verifies a 940 nm diode's output with a power meter (PM600) and reports a diode-isolated numeric measured value (1.2/1.8 W display → 1.0/1.5 W measured, ~17% under-delivery) — a cleaner isolated diode measured-power figure than Merigo et al.'s pooled one, but still not from a diagnostic-biopsy pathway (standardized non-lesional excisions, no diagnosis rendered) and not transferable to any core table device.

## สรุปภาษาไทย

- บันทึกนี้เป็น **PROVISIONAL** (ผลชั่วคราว) สร้างจากวรรณกรรมเรื่อง biopsy เนื้อเยื่ออ่อนในช่องปากที่ประมวลผลแล้วในคลังนี้ **ไม่มีการอ้างว่ามี "ค่าตั้งที่เหมาะสมที่สุด (optimal setting)" ในบันทึกนี้** — ใช้คำว่า "evidence-supported range" แทนตลอดทั้งบันทึก
- **อัปเดตรอบนี้:** ประมวลผลบทความใหม่ 3 ฉบับ — Palaia et al. 2021 (การตรวจชิ้นเนื้อจริงในมนุษย์ 445 nm, n=42, วินิจฉัยได้ 100%, เพิ่มเป็นแถวหลักในตาราง), Suter et al. 2010 (บทคัดย่อเท่านั้น ไม่มีฉบับเต็มในคลังหรือใน PMC), และ Goharkhay et al. 1999 (การศึกษา ex vivo ที่ไม่ใช่ biopsy จริง ใช้เป็นหลักฐานสนับสนุนเท่านั้น)
- รูปแบบที่พบซ้ำมากที่สุดยังคงเป็น: การศึกษา 4 ฉบับที่วินิจฉัยได้เกือบหรือครบ 100% (Angiero, Romeo, Gambino, และ Palaia ที่เพิ่มใหม่) ล้วนใช้โหมดต่อเนื่อง (CW) กำลังที่ตั้ง 1.6–2.7 W — สามฉบับที่ใช้ค่ากำลังคงที่ค่าเดียว (Romeo, Gambino, Palaia) รวมตัวกันแคบลงที่ 2.0–2.5 W ซึ่งเป็นการจำกัดช่วงลงเล็กน้อยแบบมีเงื่อนไขระมัดระวังมาก ไม่ใช่ข้อสรุปเชิงเหตุ-ผล
- Goharkhay et al. 1999 เป็นบทความแรกในวรรณกรรมของโครงการนี้ที่รายงาน **ความเร็วในการตัดจริง (10 มม./วินาที)** และ **กำลังขาออกที่วัดได้จริงด้วยเพาเวอร์มิเตอร์** (ไม่ใช่ค่าตั้งเครื่อง) แต่ใช้อุปกรณ์ที่ไม่ตรงกับอุปกรณ์อื่นใดในคลังนี้ จึงนำมาใช้แก้ไขหรือประมาณค่าให้แหล่งข้อมูลอื่นไม่ได้
- เรื่อง CW เทียบ PW **ยังไม่คลี่คลาย และซับซ้อนขึ้นกว่าเดิม**: Goharkhay et al. พบว่ากำลังไฟและโหมด (CW กับ PW สองแบบ) ไม่ส่งผลต่อขนาดความเสียหายจากความร้อนเลยในช่วง 0.5–4.5 W ซึ่งเป็นรูปแบบที่สามที่ต่างจาก Gundlapalle et al. (PW น้อยกว่า CW) และ Pergolini et al. (PW มากกว่า CW แต่ไม่มีนัยสำคัญ) — ทำให้ตอนนี้มีความขัดแย้ง 3 ทิศทาง ไม่ใช่ 2 ทิศทางเหมือนเดิม
- ความยาวคลื่นอาจส่งผลต่อความเสียหายจากความร้อน (INTERPRETATION เท่านั้น): Palaia et al. (445 nm) พบความเสียหายที่ชั้นเยื่อบุผิวมากกว่าชั้นเนื้อเยื่อเกี่ยวพัน ซึ่งตรงข้ามกับ Gambino et al. (980 nm) ที่พบชั้นเนื้อเยื่อเกี่ยวพันเสียหายมากกว่า แต่ยังไม่มีการศึกษาใดแปรความยาวคลื่นเพียงตัวแปรเดียวโดยควบคุมปัจจัยอื่นให้คงที่
- ช่วงกำลังไฟ 1.6–2.7 W (CW) ยังคงเป็นเพียง **evidence-supported range** ไม่ใช่ค่าที่เหมาะสมที่สุด และไม่ควรขยายไปใช้กับโหมดพัลส์ (PW)
- บันทึกนี้ไม่มีข้อเสนอแนะทางคลินิกใด ๆ
- **อัปเดตล่าสุด:** ประมวลผลบทความใหม่ 4 ฉบับ — Li et al. 2022 (แบบจำลองในหนู รายงานความเร็วตัดไดโอด 1 มม./วินาที ค่าที่สองในคลังนี้), Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 (เลเซอร์ CO2 ไม่ใช่ไดโอด แต่วัดกำลังขาออกจริงด้วยเพาเวอร์มิเตอร์คู่กับผลทางเนื้อเยื่อได้สำเร็จ เป็นต้นแบบวิธีวิจัยเท่านั้น), Capodiferro et al. 2009 (รายงานผู้ป่วย 1 ราย พบว่าไม่มีความเสียหายจากความร้อนที่ขอบตัดเลย) และ Capodiferro et al. 2008 (ประมวลผลถึงระดับบทคัดย่อแล้ว แต่ยังไม่มีฉบับเต็ม) — ไม่มีบทความใดปิดช่องว่างหลักเรื่องกำลังขาออกที่วัดได้จริงคู่กับผลการวินิจฉัยของเลเซอร์ไดโอด
