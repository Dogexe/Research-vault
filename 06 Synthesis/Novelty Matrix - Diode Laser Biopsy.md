# Novelty Matrix — Diode Laser Biopsy

**Status: PROVISIONAL. Reframed 2026-09-13, Prado et al. 2022 added 2026-09-14.** This matrix cross-tabulates, at full-text level, the closest-prior-art candidates processed in this vault — [[07 Data/Hanke et al 2021 - Eight-Wavelength Diode Cut Efficiency Data|Hanke et al. 2021]], [[07 Data/Strakas et al 2023 - 940nm Diode Cutting Efficiency and Thermal Damage Data|Strakas et al. 2023]], [[07 Data/Isola et al 2018 - Diode Laser Pyogenic Granuloma Excision Speed Data|Isola et al. 2018]], [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2024 - Dual-Wavelength Diode vs Er,Cr YSGG Biopsy Margin Histology Data|Al-Ani et al. 2024]], and [[07 Data/Prado et al 2022 - Micro vs Super Pulsed Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Prado et al. 2022]] — against this vault's strongest existing sources, to test how close any of them comes to the current proposed design:

> independently measured delivered power + explicitly characterized incision speed/movement + biopsy-oriented histopathologic specimen-quality assessment, in an **ex vivo oral soft-tissue model**

`Diagnostic biopsy outcome` is retained as a column for context but is **no longer the decisive novelty column** — clinical diagnostic outcome is background/clinical-relevance information now, not this project's primary experimental endpoint. Per instruction: set power is never equated with delivered power; incision speed is never inferred from procedure time; companion-paper data are kept as separate rows; dental/oral diode evidence is kept separate from CO2/non-diode/background methodology; no "first ever" or absolute-novelty claim is made; `UNKNOWN` is used where data are missing.

## 1. Closest experimental/technical precedent

**[[07 Data/Hanke et al 2021 - Eight-Wavelength Diode Cut Efficiency Data|Hanke et al. 2021]] and [[07 Data/Strakas et al 2023 - 940nm Diode Cutting Efficiency and Thermal Damage Data|Strakas et al. 2023]] are direct prior art for this project's power-measurement and speed-control design**, not merely supporting evidence. Both independently verify delivered power with a calibrated power meter checked before every single incision (≥5% deviation triggering fiber-tip replacement), control incision speed mechanically at 2 mm/s, and quantify histologic thermal effect in oral (porcine gingival) tissue — the most rigorous measured-power protocol found anywhere in this vault's diode literature. Neither, however, uses a biopsy-oriented outcome framework: both quantify cutting efficiency (cut depth/width, total interaction zone, efficiency factor γz) for surgical-protocol optimization, not specimen-quality/margin-readability/architecture-preservation. See the full side-by-side in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy#Direct technical precedent: Hanke and Strakas]].

**[[07 Data/Prado et al 2022 - Micro vs Super Pulsed Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Prado et al. 2022]] (added 2026-09-14) reinforces the measured-power side of this precedent from a fourth independent source**, and is the only one of the four to tabulate a distinct numerical measured-power value (not just a QC pass/fail gate): 1.2/1.8 W display measured as 1.0/1.5 W (≈17% under-delivery), plus 3.2 W measured for a super-pulsed arm. It pairs this with histologic thermal-damage area/depth in ex vivo oral (pig tongue) tissue and an initiated tip — but reports **no incision speed of any kind** (cutting was manual; only excision time was recorded), so it does not extend the measured-power + controlled-speed + histology combination itself, and — like Hanke/Strakas — it excises no lesion and defines no biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome. Its net effect on this matrix is to add a third/fourth independent confirmation that measured power + histology has precedent, not to narrow or widen the remaining gap.

## 2. Closest ex vivo thermal-histology precedent

**[[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2024 - Dual-Wavelength Diode vs Er,Cr YSGG Biopsy Margin Histology Data|Al-Ani et al. 2024]]** is the closest ex vivo, biopsy-adjacent thermal-histology precedent in this batch: ordinal epithelial/connective-tissue damage scores (0–3, covering nuclear/cytoplasmic changes, carbonization, desiccation, vascular changes) plus lateral thermal-damage extent (μm), a reported fixed speed of 0.75 mm/s whose control/verification method is `UNKNOWN`, an initiated tip, and contact mode — all in oral (sheep tongue) tissue. These are thermal-damage and morphologic-artifact outcomes, not a formal assessment of margin readability, tissue-architecture preservation, or specimen interpretability. The study also lacks independently measured delivered power and does not excise a lesion or specimen in the sense a diagnostic biopsy would. **[[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]]** is a secondary partial match on the thermal-damage axis (crossed power×mode design, initiated tip, quantified compartment-level damage) but reports no speed and no biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome.

## 3. Clinical relevance context (not direct experimental matches)

[[07 Data/Palaia et al 2021 - 445 nm Diode Laser In Vivo Biopsy Thermal Effect Data|Palaia et al. 2021]], [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]], and [[07 Data/Isola et al 2018 - Diode Laser Pyogenic Granuloma Excision Speed Data|Isola et al. 2018]] are real human in vivo diagnostic-biopsy studies. They are retained as **clinical relevance / outcome context** — showing which histologic alterations matter clinically, when thermal artifact does or does not compromise interpretation, and (for Gundlapalle et al.) what a formal specimen-quality scoring instrument can look like — but they are not ex vivo and are not treated as direct experimental matches to the proposed design. **Gundlapalle et al. 2022's pathologist-graded slide-quality scale (good/average/poor/non-diagnostic) is this vault's strongest existing clinical analog for a specimen-quality outcome instrument**, and is retained specifically for that methodological reason.

**Checked against this matrix (2026-09-14) and found not to change the novelty conclusion**: [[07 Data/Monteiro et al 2019 - Multi-Instrument Oral Fibro-Epithelial Margin Histology Data|Monteiro et al. 2019]] and [[07 Data/Gobbo et al 2017 - Blue vs Infrared Diode Laser Biopsy Thermal Damage Data|Gobbo et al. 2017]] are real human diode-inclusive diagnostic-biopsy sources — same clinical-relevance-context role as Palaia/Gundlapalle/Isola above — but neither independently measures power nor reports incision speed, so neither competes for Table B/C-style "closest precedent" status on the measured-power/speed axis. [[07 Data/Vescovi et al 2010 - Nd YAG Laser vs Scalpel Specimen Size Histology Data|Vescovi et al. 2010]] and [[07 Data/Seoane et al 2013 - Er,Cr YSGG Leukoplakia Pseudodysplastic Artifact Data|Seoane et al. 2013]] are non-diode (Nd:YAG, Er,Cr:YSGG) and out of scope for this matrix's diode-laser comparison entirely. None of the four adds a diode-laser source that independently measures power and reports speed and uses a biopsy-oriented outcome set — the novelty conclusion in §5 below is unchanged.

## 4. What remains unlinked

- No ex vivo, oral, diode-laser source in this vault combines independently measured delivered power, explicit speed/movement characterization, and a biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome framework (margin readability, tissue architecture preservation, specimen interpretability) in one design. Hanke 2021/Strakas 2023 close the measured-power and controlled-speed links but use cutting-efficiency and thermal-interaction outcomes. Al-Ani 2024/Pergolini 2025 add thermal-damage histology but do not provide a biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome set; Al-Ani's speed-control method is also `UNKNOWN`.
- No source defines a formal margin-readability or tissue-architecture-preservation scoring instrument for an ex vivo, non-lesional oral specimen. Gundlapalle et al. 2022's slide-quality scale is the closest instrument found, but it is in vivo and set-power-only, so it cannot be directly imported without validation in an ex vivo setting.
- No primary experimental source varies incision speed as an independently manipulated variable against a biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcome. The closest primary studies hold one reported speed fixed; Romanos et al. 2013 presents a review-level speed sweep, but without quantified biopsy-oriented outcomes.

## 5. POTENTIAL NOVELTY — REQUIRES SYSTEMATIC VERIFICATION

> The combination of independently measured delivered power, controlled incision speed, and histologic thermal/tissue-effect assessment has precedent in prior diode-laser studies (Hanke et al. 2021; Strakas et al. 2023; Goharkhay et al. 1999). What remains to be established is whether characterized exposure has been combined in one ex vivo oral soft-tissue design with both thermal-damage assessment and biopsy-oriented specimen-quality endpoints: margin readability, tissue architecture preservation, and specimen interpretability.

This is narrower than a claim that measured power + speed + histology is novel, because Hanke 2021, Strakas 2023, and Goharkhay 1999 already demonstrate that combination for technical thermal/tissue-effect outcomes; they do **not** report diagnostic biopsy outcomes. The remaining distinction rests on biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcomes not yet being demonstrated together with measured power and explicit speed/movement characterization in one ex vivo oral design. Al-Ani 2024 contributes thermal-damage histology at a reported fixed speed but not a specimen-quality instrument; Gundlapalle 2022 contributes a clinical slide-quality grade but lacks measured power and speed. This conclusion rests on three independent verification passes on this vault ([[06 Synthesis/Novelty Check - Prior Systematic Review]], [[90 Agent/Search Reports/Novelty Verification - Measured Power and Incision Speed/Novelty Verification Report]], and this matrix), not silence alone, and is not a full systematic search — see the linked verification report for stated limitations. **No claim is made that no such study exists, or that this would be the first ever such study.**

## 6. สรุปภาษาไทย

- ตารางนี้ปรับกรอบใหม่ตามคำถามวิจัย ex vivo ของโครงการ — คอลัมน์ "ผลการวินิจฉัยทางคลินิก (diagnostic biopsy outcome)" **ไม่ใช่คอลัมน์ชี้ขาดความใหม่อีกต่อไป** เป็นเพียงข้อมูลบริบท
- **Hanke et al. 2021 และ Strakas et al. 2023 คือต้นแบบโดยตรง (direct prior art)** ของการออกแบบวัดกำลัง+ความเร็ว — มีกำลังที่วัดได้จริง (ตรวจสอบก่อนตัดทุกครั้ง) + ความเร็วควบคุม + ผลจุลพยาธิวิทยา ในเนื้อเยื่อช่องปาก (เหงือกหมู) แล้ว แต่ใช้กรอบผลลัพธ์แบบประสิทธิภาพการตัด ไม่ใช่กรอบคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแบบ biopsy
- **Al-Ani et al. 2024** รายงานความเร็วคงที่ 0.75 mm/s แต่ไม่ระบุวิธีควบคุม/ตรวจสอบ และประเมินความเสียหายจากความร้อน/การเปลี่ยนแปลงทางสัณฐานวิทยา ไม่ใช่เครื่องมือประเมินคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแบบ biopsy-oriented
- **Gundlapalle et al. 2022** (ในมนุษย์) มีเครื่องมือให้คะแนนคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อโดยพยาธิแพทย์ที่ใกล้เคียงที่สุดในคลังนี้ แต่ไม่ใช่ ex vivo
- ความใหม่ที่เหลืออยู่ (ระมัดระวัง): การผนวกกำลังที่วัดได้จริง + การระบุ/ควบคุมความเร็ว เข้ากับการประเมินความเสียหายจากความร้อน **และ** คุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแบบ biopsy-oriented (ความอ่านง่ายของขอบ การรักษาโครงสร้างเนื้อเยื่อ และความสามารถในการแปลผล) ในแบบจำลอง ex vivo เนื้อเยื่อช่องปากเดียวกัน — ยังไม่มีแหล่งข้อมูลใดทำครบ
- ไม่มีการอ้าง "ไม่มีการศึกษาใดเลย" หรือ "เป็นครั้งแรกในโลก" ในบันทึกนี้
