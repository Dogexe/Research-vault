# Biopsy Specimen Quality

**Status: PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** Three of this vault's processed sources now measure an actual diagnostic-yield outcome in real human diode-laser oral biopsy specimens, all mutually consistent. The central power-to-diagnosis link (below) remains NEEDS EVIDENCE.

## Claim

- Claim: Diode-laser-excised oral soft-tissue specimens can achieve full histopathologic diagnostic yield when specimen size or excision margin is adequate relative to the thermal-alteration zone; diagnostic failure is reported specifically in small specimens. No source ties this to a calibrated, independently measured power value — all three sources use set power only.
- Scope: Human oral soft-tissue biopsy specimens excised with a dental diode laser (808 nm in Angiero and Romeo; 980 nm in Gambino), evaluated for histopathologic diagnostic yield by a pathologist.

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Angiero et al. 2011 (n=608 cases, 808 nm diode, 1.6–2.7 W CW average 2.5 W, 320 μm fiber, DMT Milan, retrospective 2005–2010) found diagnostic failure was size-graded: 63.63% unreadable at 2 mm specimen size (29/46), 37.71% unreadable at 3 mm (31/84), and 0% unreadable (100% readable) at ≥4 mm. The authors recommend an in vivo specimen diameter of at least 5 mm, accounting for post-excision tissue shrinkage. Mean thermal-damage width was 282.8 μm (range 260.7–321.4 μm). [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- FACT: Romeo et al. 2014 (n=17 patients, 808 nm diode 2 W CW / 532 nm KTP 1.5 W PW, lesion size 0.5–1 cm i.e. 5–10 mm) achieved a certainty diagnosis in 17/17 (100%) specimens; mean margin thermal damage ranged 0.149–0.473 mm by lesion type, with more inflamed/cellular lesions (e.g., oral lichen planus) showing greater margin damage than less-inflamed ones (e.g., mucocele, melanotic macula). Per-patient data isolate diode-only figures for every lesion type. [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]
- FACT: Gambino et al. 2026 (n=30, 980 nm diode 2 W CW, 300 μm fiber, contact mode, standardized 5 mm circumferential margin, Raffaello Laser DMT) obtained a definitive diagnosis in all 30/30 (100%) specimens. Histologic thermal damage was directly quantified in the same specimens (288.9 μm epithelium, 430.3 μm connective tissue, cross-validated against OCT at 88.5% concordance), and laser-treated tissue was statistically significantly thicker than healthy control tissue (epithelium p=0.021, lamina propria p=0.034) — a real, measurable, significant alteration that nonetheless did not impair diagnosis in any case. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- FACT: None of the three sources reports an independently measured/calibrated power-meter output value — all report set/console power only, per this vault's rule against inferring measured output from a reported set value.
- FACT (ex vivo, not a diagnostic-biopsy outcome): Azevedo et al. 2016 (980 nm diode, 3.5 W PW / 3.5 W Boost PW, ex vivo porcine tongue) measured ETTD of 456.15 μm and 626.82 μm respectively, with diode carbonization scores of 2.50 and 1.80 (0–4 scale) — no diagnosis was rendered in this study, so it cannot directly confirm or contest the diagnostic-yield figures above, but its ETTD magnitudes are broadly consistent with Angiero et al.'s and Gambino et al.'s human in vivo/ex vivo margin-damage ranges. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]

## Conflicting findings

- Not a confirmed conflict: Angiero et al. reports diagnostic failure specifically for specimens at or below 3 mm, while Romeo et al. (5–10 mm lesions) and Gambino et al. (standardized 5 mm margin) both report 100% diagnostic success at larger sizes/margins — all three are consistent with, not contradicting, the hypothesis that specimen size/margin relative to thermal-alteration depth governs diagnostic outcome. No single study varies this as a controlled independent variable.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: Across all three processed diagnostic-yield sources, specimen size or excision margin — not power/setting, which differs between studies and is unverified in all three — is the variable most plausibly associated with diagnostic outcome. Angiero et al.'s large retrospective series shows a sharp, size-graded diagnostic-failure gradient below 4 mm; Romeo et al. (5–10 mm) and Gambino et al. (standardized 5 mm) both show no diagnostic failure at all, consistent with Angiero et al.'s own ≥4–5 mm threshold. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- HYPOTHESIS: A minimum specimen size/margin (≥4–5 mm, converging across all three processed sources) relative to the margin thermal-damage zone (which itself measures on the order of 0.15–0.6 mm / 150–630 μm across the processed sources) may be a more direct determinant of diagnostic yield than the power setting itself, provided the setting is compatible with clean tissue removal. Still untested as a controlled independent variable (power varied, size held constant, or vice versa) by any single vault source.
- Limitations: Angiero et al. is retrospective, single-institution. Romeo et al. is a small series (n=17) with uneven diode/KTP distribution for two of six lesion types. Gambino et al. is single-center, ex vivo OCT only, and fixed both power and margin (not varied). None of the three independently verifies delivered power.
- Uncertainty: NEEDS EVIDENCE for whether power/operating-parameter choice (as opposed to specimen size/margin) independently affects diagnostic yield — no source in this vault varies power while holding specimen size constant, or vice versa, to isolate the two variables.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable — no confirmed conflict between the three processed sources.
- Research gaps: No vault source varies dental-diode power/operating parameters as an independent variable while measuring diagnostic yield at a fixed specimen size (or vice versa); this remains the central unresolved question of [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]. See [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]] for the related power-verification gap.
