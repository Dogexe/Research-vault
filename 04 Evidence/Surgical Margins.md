# Surgical Margins

**Status: PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** Four processed sources (Angiero, Romeo, Azevedo, Gambino) each directly measure specimen-margin condition for a dental diode laser.

## Claim

- Claim: Specimen-margin thermal alteration in diode-laser-excised oral soft tissue is measurable (sub-millimeter to low-millimeter range across processed sources) and is associated with specimen size/margin width relative to that alteration zone; no source ties a calibrated, independently measured dental diode power value to margin histologic condition.
- Scope: The cut edge (periphery) of an excised oral soft-tissue specimen; see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Specimen Margin]].

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Angiero et al. 2011 (n=608, 808 nm diode, 1.6–2.7 W CW, 320 μm fiber) found margin thermal-damage width of 260.7–321.4 μm (mean 282.8 μm), and diagnostic reliability graded by specimen size: 63.63% unreadable at 2 mm, 37.71% at 3 mm, 0% at ≥4 mm; recommends ≥5 mm in vivo specimen diameter. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- FACT: Romeo et al. 2014 (n=17, 808 nm diode 2 W CW / 532 nm KTP 1.5 W PW) measured mean margin damage of 0.149–0.473 mm depending on lesion type (diode-only figures now isolated per lesion via per-patient data), and recommend enlarging laser biopsy specimens by approximately 0.5 mm to compensate for thermal alteration, especially in inflammatory lesions (oral lichen planus showed the highest margin damage, 0.473 mm). [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]
- FACT: Azevedo et al. 2016 (980 nm diode, ex vivo porcine tongue) measured margin thermal-damage extent (ETTD) of 456.15 μm (3.5 W PW) and 626.82 μm (3.5 W Boost PW), with 80% of diode incisions scored "irregular" at both settings — an order of magnitude consistent with, though not directly comparable to (different species, wavelength, power), Angiero et al.'s and Romeo et al.'s human margin-damage figures. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: Gambino et al. 2026 (n=30, 980 nm diode 2 W CW, standardized 5 mm circumferential margin) measured margin thermal damage of 288.9 μm (epithelium) and 430.3 μm (connective tissue), with laser-treated tissue significantly thicker than healthy control tissue (epithelium p=0.021, lamina propria p=0.034) — the vault's only source with a genuine untreated-tissue control group for margin comparison, and the only source using a standardized (rather than surgeon-judged) margin width. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. explicitly conclude that, in their in vitro porcine tissue model, an 810 nm diode laser at 0.5–2 W was the wavelength/power combination least likely to cause thermal damage to peri-incisional margins for oral soft-tissue biopsy. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]

## Conflicting findings

- Not a confirmed conflict: Azevedo et al.'s ETTD figures (456–627 μm), Angiero et al.'s width figures (261–321 μm), and Gambino et al.'s figures (289–430 μm) are all in the same broad order of magnitude despite different species (porcine vs. human), wavelength (980 vs. 808 nm), and power/mode — noted as a rough consistency check across four independent processed sources, not a validated pooled comparison, since methods and tissue differ.
- FACT: Gambino et al.'s standardized 5 mm margin matches Angiero et al.'s independently derived recommended minimum (≥5 mm) — an indirect but notable cross-study consistency point, arrived at by two different methods (Angiero et al.: empirical size-stratified failure analysis; Gambino et al.: a priori protocol design, not itself tested against smaller margins).

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: The four now-processed sources converge on margin thermal alteration being a real, measurable, sub-millimeter-to-low-millimeter phenomenon for dental diode lasers, and on specimen/margin size (relative to that alteration zone) — rather than power setting alone — being the variable most directly tied to whether the margin remains diagnostically readable. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN
- Limitations: Azevedo et al. is ex vivo porcine tissue, not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway. Romeo et al. is a small series (n=17) with uneven device distribution for two of six lesion types. Gambino et al. is single-center, ex vivo OCT only, one fixed margin (not varied). None of the four independently verifies delivered power with a calibrated meter — all report set/console power only.
- Uncertainty: None outstanding for these four sources (all now full-text-verified); NEEDS VERIFICATION remains only for the unprocessed candidates listed in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]].
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable — no confirmed conflict.
- Research gaps: A study directly relating calibrated, measured dental-diode power output to specimen-margin histologic condition and pathologist-assessed diagnostic yield, in human biopsy specimens, has not been identified in this vault. A study varying margin width as an independent variable (rather than fixing it, as Gambino et al. does) against diagnostic yield is also absent.
