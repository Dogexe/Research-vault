# Surgical Margins

**Status: PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** Upgraded from placeholder — Angiero et al. 2011, Romeo et al. 2014, and Azevedo et al. 2016 are now processed and each directly measures specimen-margin condition for a dental diode laser.

## Claim

- Claim: Specimen-margin thermal alteration in diode-laser-excised oral soft tissue is measurable (in the sub-millimeter to low-millimeter range across processed sources) and is associated with specimen size relative to that alteration zone; no source ties a calibrated, independently measured dental diode power value to margin histologic condition.
- Scope: The cut edge (periphery) of an excised oral soft-tissue specimen; see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Specimen Margin]].

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Angiero et al. (n=608, 808 nm diode, 1.6–2.7 W CW, 320 μm fiber) found specimens with a mean size below 3 mm had significantly more frequent epithelial/stromal margin change than specimens over 3 mm, and recommend an in vivo specimen diameter of at least 5 mm for reliable histologic reading. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- FACT: Romeo et al. (n=17, 808 nm diode 2 W CW / 532 nm KTP 1.5 W PW) measured mean margin damage of 0.149–0.473 mm depending on lesion type, and recommend enlarging laser biopsy specimens by approximately 0.5 mm to compensate for thermal alteration, especially in inflammatory lesions (oral lichen planus showed the highest margin damage, 0.473 mm). [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]
- FACT: Azevedo et al. (980 nm diode, ex vivo porcine tongue) measured margin thermal-damage extent (ETTD) of 456.15 μm (3.5 W PW) and 626.82 μm (3.5 W Boost PW), an order of magnitude consistent with, though not directly comparable to (different species/model, different wavelength/power), Romeo et al.'s human in vivo margin-damage range. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. explicitly conclude that, in their in vitro porcine tissue model, an 810 nm diode laser at 0.5–2 W was the wavelength/power combination least likely to cause thermal damage to peri-incisional margins for oral soft-tissue biopsy. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]

## Conflicting findings

- Not a confirmed conflict: Azevedo et al.'s ETTD figures (456–627 μm) and Romeo et al.'s margin-damage figures (149–473 μm) are in the same broad order of magnitude despite different species (porcine vs. human), wavelength (980 vs. 808 nm), and power (3.5 W PW vs. 2 W CW) — this is noted as a rough consistency check, not a validated cross-study comparison, since methods and tissue differ.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: The three now-processed sources converge on margin thermal alteration being a real, measurable, sub-millimeter-to-low-millimeter phenomenon for dental diode lasers, and on specimen/lesion size (relative to that alteration zone) — rather than power setting alone — being the variable most directly tied to whether the margin remains diagnostically readable. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN
- Limitations: Angiero et al. is abstract-only in this vault (no PMCID available). Azevedo et al. is ex vivo porcine tissue, not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway. Romeo et al. is a small series (n=17) with uneven device distribution across lesion types. None of the three independently verifies delivered power with a calibrated meter — all report set/console power only.
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION for Angiero et al. against full text if it becomes accessible.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable — no confirmed conflict.
- Research gaps: A study directly relating calibrated, measured dental-diode power output to specimen-margin histologic condition and pathologist-assessed diagnostic yield, in human biopsy specimens, has not been identified in this vault.
