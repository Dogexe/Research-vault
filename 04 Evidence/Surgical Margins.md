# Surgical Margins

**PLACEHOLDER — NEEDS EVIDENCE.** Created because relevant vault background evidence already exists ([[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]), but no source ties measured dental-diode power to margin histology in a biopsy-diagnosis context.

## Claim

- Claim: NEEDS EVIDENCE. No source in this vault ties a calibrated, independently measured dental diode power value to specimen-margin histologic condition in a biopsy-diagnosis context.
- Scope: The cut edge (periphery) of an excised oral soft-tissue specimen intended for histopathologic diagnosis; see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Specimen Margin]].

## Supporting findings

- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. explicitly conclude that, in their tissue model, an 810 nm diode laser at 0.5–2 W was the wavelength/power combination least likely to cause thermal damage to peri-incisional margins for oral soft-tissue biopsy. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (abstract-only, unprocessed candidates — see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]): Angiero et al. report diagnosis was "not achievable" in 46.15% of diode-laser-excised specimens smaller than 3 mm and recommend an in vivo specimen diameter of at least 5 mm (PMID 21387158, DOI 10.1007/s10103-011-0900-7). Romeo et al. measured epithelial/connective margin damage per lesion type (0.149–0.473 mm mean, depending on lesion) and suggest enlarging laser biopsies by ~0.5 mm to compensate (PMID 25405233, DOI 10.1155/2014/761704). Azevedo et al.'s title explicitly concerns "surgical margins made by different laser wavelengths," quantifying tissue damage extension for a diode laser among others (PMID 27703606, DOI 10.4317/jced.52830).

## Conflicting findings

- Not yet assessable — the only processed vault source (Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.) is not directly comparable in units or design to the unprocessed candidates (Angiero et al., Romeo et al., Azevedo et al.), which report margin damage in mm/μm per lesion or specimen-size class rather than as a function of set power alone.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: The one processed vault source that frames its conclusion in explicitly biopsy-margin terms (Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.) supports keeping power near the lower end of a tested CW range (0.5–2 W) and choosing 810 nm among the six wavelengths tested, but this is a single in vitro porcine study using set (not independently measured) power. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN
- Limitations: Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. is in vitro porcine tissue, not a human diagnostic biopsy specimen. The unprocessed candidates have not been verified against full text or extracted into vault Data notes.
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION for all margin-size/damage figures until the unprocessed candidates are read in full and cross-checked.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable.
- Research gaps: A study directly relating calibrated, measured dental-diode power output to specimen-margin histologic condition and pathologist-assessed diagnostic yield, in human biopsy specimens, has not been identified in this vault.
