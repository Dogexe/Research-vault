# Power Output and Tissue Effect

**PLACEHOLDER — NEEDS EVIDENCE.** This note is the vault's central bridge node between its existing power-output/power-loss evidence and the biopsy-specimen-quality question in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]. It is created because a real, partial evidentiary basis already exists in the vault (below), not as an empty stub.

**Role (2026-09-13 reframing): EXPERIMENTAL MECHANISM.** This note tracks the first link of the project's ex vivo chain (set power → measured delivered power) and where it does/does not connect to a tissue-effect outcome. Its clinical-source rows (Angiero, Romeo, Azevedo, Gambino) are secondary CLINICAL RELEVANCE context here, since none independently measures power.

## Claim

- Claim: NEEDS EVIDENCE. No source currently in this vault pairs a calibrated, independently measured dental diode laser power output with a histologically assessed tissue effect (thermal damage, coagulation, carbonization) or a biopsy diagnostic outcome, in the same study.
- Scope: Dental/oral soft-tissue laser surgery, where "power output" means the vault's existing measured/delivered-power sense (see [[03 Concepts/Power Output]]), not just the panel setting.

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. found that, for all three measured tissue-effect variables (lost tissue area, thermal damage area, necrosis area), values were higher at 2.0 W set power than at 0.5 W, regardless of wavelength, in an in vitro porcine periodontal-pocket/mucosa model. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]] — reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]].
- FACT: Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. conclude that, in their tissue model, an 810 nm diode laser at 0.5–2 W was the wavelength/power combination least likely to cause thermal damage to peri-incisional margins "for oral soft-tissue biopsy" — the only vault source whose own stated conclusion uses the word "biopsy." [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- FACT: Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.'s power values are set/panel values used to standardize the experiment; the source performed a fiber-tip power-meter check before each incision but did not publish the resulting measured-output value, so this is evidence about *set* power vs. tissue effect, not *measured/delivered* power vs. tissue effect. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (abstract-level extraction now on file; full text still unavailable): Capodiferro et al. 2008 report that, in diode-laser-excised oral specimens (2–7 W), "changes induced by laser such as coagulation of proteins were present only with high power density output" — directly on-topic for this claim, but power appears to be a set/nominal value, not independently power-meter-verified, per the abstract. [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data]]
- FACT (new, this update): Two additional sources reinforce rather than close this gap. Capodiferro et al. 2009 (n=1 case, 880 nm, 2.5 W CW) reports a set/console power value only, with no independent power-meter verification, and no measurable tissue effect was found at that setting. Li et al. 2022 (mouse oral mucosa, diode 3.0 W pulsed) likewise reports a set/console power value only, with no independent power-meter verification. Neither is diode-specific *and* a diagnostic-biopsy pathway with a measured-power value, so neither closes the central gap in this note's Claim. [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2009 - Diode Laser Chondroid Lipoma Case Report Data]] [[07 Data/Li et al 2022 - Diode vs Er-YAG vs Cold Scalpel Mice Oral Mucosa Data]]
- FACT, methodological only (new, this update): Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 independently measured delivered power with a calibrated power meter (Gentec PRJ-M) directly before each incision, paired with thermocouple and histologic thermal-damage measurement, in oral mucosa — exactly the measurement design this note's Claim identifies as absent for dental diode lasers. However, this source uses three CO2 lasers, not a diode laser, and its devices do not overlap with any device in this vault's diode evidence base, so it cannot close the gap — it is recorded only as a demonstration that the needed measurement design is achievable in oral mucosa. [[07 Data/Wilder-Smith et al 1995 - CO2 Laser Incision and Thermal Damage Data]]

- FACT: Four biopsy-specific sources processed since this note was created — Angiero et al. 2011 (1.6–2.7 W CW, average 2.5 W), Romeo et al. 2014 (2 W CW diode / 1.5 W PW KTP), Azevedo et al. 2016 (3.5 W PW / 3.5 W Boost PW diode), and Gambino et al. 2026 (2 W CW) — all report set/console power only, with no independent power-meter verification of delivered output, reinforcing rather than closing this gap. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]] [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- FACT: Gambino et al. 2026 found that, at one fixed set power (2 W CW), laser-treated tissue was statistically significantly thicker than untreated control tissue (p=0.021 epithelium, p=0.034 lamina propria) — the clearest statistically-tested tissue effect of a single set-power value in this vault's biopsy evidence, though it does not vary power and does not independently measure it. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- FACT: Azevedo et al. 2016 found that a diode laser's higher "Boost" power/intensity setting (3.5 W Boost PW) produced greater thermal-damage extent (626.82 μm) than its standard setting at the same nominal 3.5 W label (456.15 μm PW) — a within-device, set-power-level tissue-effect gradient, though "Boost" is not further defined in the retrieved text and is not a calibrated/measured value. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]

## Conflicting findings

- None identified — no vault source contradicts the above; the gap is absence of a study design that measures both delivered power and tissue effect together, not a disagreement between existing sources.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: The one vault source that both varies power and measures a tissue-effect outcome (Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.) uses set/panel power, not independently measured output — so this vault cannot yet say whether the *measured-output* deviations documented in [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]] (which differ in direction and magnitude by device) would change the power–tissue-effect relationship Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. report for set power. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]] [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]]
- HYPOTHESIS: If a device's measured output deviates substantially from its set value (as several devices do, per [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]]), the actual tissue effect delivered at a given panel setting could differ from what a set-power-only study like Gutiérrez-Corrales et al.'s would predict. This is untested by any source currently in this vault.
- Limitations: Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. is in vitro porcine tissue, not human oral biopsy, and reports set power only. Capodiferro et al. 2008 is abstract-level only (no full text available) and likely also reports set/nominal power; Capodiferro et al. 2009 is n=1; Li et al. 2022 is a mouse wound model, not a diagnostic biopsy; Wilder-Smith et al. 1995 is CO2, not diode.
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION for whether either source's power values were independently confirmed by a calibrated meter at the point of tissue contact (as opposed to a pre-experiment standardization check).
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable — no cross-source conflict, only an unfilled gap.
- Research gaps: A dental-diode study is needed that (1) independently verifies delivered power with a calibrated power meter, (2) varies that verified power across a range, and (3) histologically assesses thermal damage, coagulation, or carbonization — ideally in a specimen intended for diagnostic biopsy rather than a purely mechanistic tissue model.
