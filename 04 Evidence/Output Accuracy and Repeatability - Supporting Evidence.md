# Output Accuracy and Repeatability — Supporting Evidence

**SUPPORTING / BACKGROUND EVIDENCE.** This note is not part of the core dental set-power vs. measured-output evidence base ([[04 Evidence/Set Power vs Measured Output]], [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]]) and should not be merged with it. Neither source below is a dental diode device; both are adjacent laser-therapy domains included here as background on whether displayed/set power reliably predicts actual delivered power, and whether that delivered power is repeatable across firings or over device lifetime.

## Sources

- Davies CE, Riley MI, Dabbs EB, Whiteley MS. "Comparison of laser power output from the fiber tip during endovenous laser ablation against displayed power and the 'first treatment' effect." *J Vasc Surg Venous Lymphat Disord.* 2020;9(4):1051-1056. DOI: [10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018)
- Girasol CE, Braz GA, Bachmann L, Celli J, Guirro RRJ. "Laser light sources for photobiomodulation: The role of power and beam characterization in treatment accuracy and reliability." *PLoS One.* 2022;17(3):e0266193. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0266193](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0266193)

## Claim

- Claim: In non-dental laser-therapy domains, displayed/set output power is not a reliable proxy for actual delivered power, and delivered power is not necessarily stable across repeated firings or over a device's service life.
- Scope: Davies et al. — 1470 nm endovenous laser ablation fiber tips, bench model. Girasol et al. — 24 low-power (photobiomodulation) laser devices in routine clinical use in Brazil, multiple wavelengths. Neither is a dental diode surgical laser; applicability to dental diode devices is INTERPRETATION, not direct evidence.

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Davies et al. measured temperature rise from a 1470 nm endovenous laser fired at a displayed 5 W setting for 2 minutes into a thermally insulated water bath, using two thermocouples placed equidistant from the fiber tip as a proxy for delivered power; three separate fibers were tested, each fired three times in succession. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018]
- FACT: Davies et al. reported that actual power output from the fiber tip appeared lower than the value suggested by the console display. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018]
- FACT: Davies et al. found the first firing of a fiber in a sequence of three same-day firings had significantly lower power output than the second and third firings (P = .0004 and P < .0001, respectively) — described by the authors as a "first treatment" effect. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018]
- FACT: Davies et al. found that prewarming the laser console for 1 hour without firing the diode did not remove this first-firing effect (P = .293), which the authors interpret as evidence the effect is not due to a "cold" console alone but to the diode itself being less efficient on first use after power-on. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018]
- FACT: Davies et al. found no evidence of fiber degradation in any fiber tested up to the maximum manufacturer-recommended energy delivery per fiber (approximately 10,000 J). [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018]
- FACT (added on review of the fuller data extraction — see correction note below): Davies et al.'s measured water-heating effect was approximately 40% lower than the value calorimetrically expected from the displayed 5 W setting, and the first-firing effect was described by the source as approximately a 15% power decrease relative to subsequent firings of the same fiber. [[07 Data/Davies et al 2020 - EVLA Fiber Tip Power Output and Degradation]]
- FACT: Davies et al. cite, as background (not as their own finding), that IEC 60601-2-22 permits laser consoles an inherent power-display inaccuracy of up to ±20% — the source's own citation of a regulatory standard, not independently verified by this vault. [[07 Data/Davies et al 2020 - EVLA Fiber Tip Power Output and Degradation]]
- FACT: Girasol et al. measured power output and light-emitting beam diameter on 24 therapeutic (photobiomodulation) laser devices in clinical use, with a mean device age of 11 ± 5 years and mean weekly use under 30 minutes. [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0266193]
- FACT: Girasol et al. found measured power ranged from 2% to 134% of the value declared by the manufacturer across the 24 devices. [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0266193]
- FACT: Girasol et al. found beam diameter differed from the manufacturer's nominal value by 38% to 543%, including differences between units of the same brand and model. [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0266193]
- FACT: Girasol et al. found a statistically significant difference in power output measured after 1 minute vs. 3 minutes of sequential emission for the 830 nm and 904 nm devices tested (p < 0.05), but no significant wavelength-by-time interaction across the full sample. [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0266193]
- FACT: Girasol et al. found no significant correlation between device age (time since purchase) and measured power (Spearman's rs = -0.051, P = .813) or beam diameter (rs = -0.249, P = .277), and likewise no significant correlation between time since last maintenance and measured power (rs = -0.107, P = .619) or beam diameter (rs = -0.282, P = .216), despite the wide 2%–134% accuracy spread. [[07 Data/Girasol et al 2022 - Photobiomodulation Device Power and Beam Accuracy]]

## Conflicting findings

- NEEDS VERIFICATION (corrected from an earlier version of this note, which stated no percentage was reported): Davies et al.'s calorimetric method gives an approximate ~40% below-expected figure and a ~15% first-firing decrease, but both are derived from an indirect water-bath calorimetry proxy, not a fiber-tip optical power meter — so this remains not directly numerically comparable to the calibrated-power-meter percentages reported for dental diode devices in [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]]; the measurement methods differ, not only the devices. [[07 Data/Davies et al 2020 - EVLA Fiber Tip Power Output and Degradation]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: Girasol et al.'s 2%–134%-of-declared range spans both under- and over-reporting devices without a stated signed mean or direction breakdown, so it is not directly comparable to the dental-diode deviation directions either. [[07 Data/Girasol et al 2022 - Photobiomodulation Device Power and Beam Accuracy]]
- The two sources do not conflict with each other or with the core dental-diode evidence in this vault; they measure different devices, domains, and outcome variables, so any resemblance to the dental-diode pattern is an analogy, not a replication.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: Across two non-dental laser-therapy domains, set/displayed power is not a reliable proxy for actual delivered power, and delivered power can change within a session (Girasol et al.) or across successive firings of the same device (Davies et al.) — a pattern structurally similar to the set-power vs. measured-output inconsistency reported for dental diode devices elsewhere in this vault, though this is a cross-domain analogy, not evidence from dental devices. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018] [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0266193]
- HYPOTHESIS: A "first-firing" or diode warm-up effect similar to Davies et al.'s could, if present in dental diode lasers, partly explain device-level output variability reported in [[04 Evidence/Power Loss and Deviation Across Dental Diode Devices]] — this is untested in any dental-diode source currently in this vault and remains a hypothesis requiring direct testing.
- Limitations: Davies et al. used one bench setup (thermocouple-based indirect proxy for power, not a calibrated power meter) for a single wavelength (1470 nm) and modality (endovenous ablation) across three fibers. Girasol et al. surveyed low-power photobiomodulation devices in one country's clinical service context, not dental diode surgical lasers; the 2%–134% figure is the full observed range across 24 devices, not a mean, and its direction (over- vs. under-reporting) is not broken out in the data extracted here. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jvsv.2020.10.018] [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0266193]
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION — whether the mechanisms reported here (diode warm-up/first-firing effect; device-aging drift) generalize to dental diode lasers is untested by these sources.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable — no direct conflict exists between these two sources or between them and the core dental-diode evidence; they are adjacent-domain background rather than measurements of the same devices.
- Research gaps: NEEDS VERIFICATION — no source currently in this vault tests a "first-firing"/warm-up effect specifically in a dental diode laser using the same displayed-vs.-measured protocol applied to the core dental-diode evidence set.
