# Thermal Artifact

**Status: PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** Upgraded from placeholder — Azevedo et al. 2016 is now processed and directly measures dental-diode thermal artifact extent. The link from thermal artifact to actual diagnostic outcome is addressed separately in [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]] and remains only indirectly connected here.

## Claim

- Claim: A dental diode laser (980 nm, ex vivo) produces a measurable thermal-artifact zone at the incision margin whose extent correlates with macroscopic carbonization; this is now directly measured by one processed vault source, but that source does not assess diagnostic impact (it is not a biopsy-diagnosis pathway).
- Scope: Thermally induced histologic artifact (thermal damage zone, coagulation, carbonization, tissue splitting) at the margin of a laser-incised oral soft-tissue specimen.

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Azevedo et al. (980 nm diode, ex vivo porcine tongue) measured Extent of Thermal Tissue Damage (ETTD) of 456.15 μm at 3.5 W PW and 626.82 μm at 3.5 W Boost PW — the second-highest ETTD of eight instrument/setting groups tested (after Nd:YAG, 670.68 μm). [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: Azevedo et al. found a significant positive correlation between ETTD and macroscopic carbonization degree across all instruments/settings pooled (r = 0.789, p = 0.01), and a significant negative correlation between ETTD and incision regularity (r = −0.299, p = 0.01). [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: Azevedo et al. found significant differences in ETTD according to histologic tissue-change scores (nuclear, cytoplasmic, connective-tissue changes; each p < 0.001) — greater tissue-change scores were associated with higher ETTD. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: The diode's own numeric carbonization-degree score was not reproduced in the retrieved full text (only pooled correlation statistics and qualitative statements were available); this is recorded as UNKNOWN rather than inferred. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. found statistically significant differences between the 1470 nm and 810 nm groups for thermal damage and necrosis in periodontal-pocket tissue, and between the 1064 nm group and others for lost-tissue area in oral-mucosa tissue, across six wavelengths (445–1470 nm) and 0.5–2.0 W. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Romanos et al. found the 445 nm wavelength produced significantly higher histologic penetration depth of thermal effect than 970 nm, and that pulsed mode produced less thermal penetration depth than continuous-wave mode at the same 2 W setting, in ex vivo bovine gingiva. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Al-Ani et al. found the highest mean temperature rise with a 2.5 W diode laser setting and the lowest with a 2.5 W Er,Cr:YSGG setting, across 50 incisions in ex vivo sheep tongue. [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (abstract-only, unprocessed candidate — see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]): Suter et al. found the diode laser's thermal damage zone was significantly larger than either CO2 laser setting tested, in a pilot RCT of excisional oral mucosal biopsies (PMID 21038754).

## Conflicting findings

- None identified between processed vault sources — Azevedo et al., Gutiérrez-Corrales et al., Romanos et al., and Al-Ani et al. each vary a different independent variable (instrument/power-mode, wavelength/power, tip status, laser type/power) and do not directly contradict one another; each documents that thermal effect changes with the variable it manipulates.
- Not yet assessable: whether Suter et al. (unprocessed) agrees or disagrees with Azevedo et al.'s diode-vs-comparator magnitude ordering, since Suter et al. has not been extracted into a vault Data note.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: Across the processed vault sources, thermal tissue effect at a diode-laser incision margin is consistently reported to vary with power/mode setting (Azevedo et al.: higher "Boost" setting produced greater ETTD than standard PW) and to correlate with carbonization severity — but only Azevedo et al. measures this directly for a dental diode laser with a quantified μm figure; none of these sources reports whether the observed thermal effect actually impaired histopathologic diagnosis (that link is addressed only indirectly, via specimen size, in [[04 Evidence/Biopsy Specimen Quality]]). [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]] [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]] [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]] [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]]
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN
- Limitations: Azevedo et al., Gutiérrez-Corrales et al., and Romanos et al. all use ex vivo/in vitro animal tissue, not human oral biopsy specimens submitted for diagnosis; none reports pathologist-assessed diagnostic yield. Azevedo et al.'s diode-specific carbonization score and incision-regularity score are UNKNOWN (not reproduced in the retrieved text).
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION — Suter et al. (and other candidates listed in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]) have not been confirmed against full text.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable.
- Research gaps: A dental-diode biopsy study is needed that reports both a thermal-artifact measurement (zone width, carbonization score) and a pathologist-assessed diagnostic outcome in the same human specimen set — no source currently in this vault does both. A dental-diode study independently verifying delivered power (rather than reporting set power only, as all sources here do) alongside a thermal-artifact measurement is also still absent — see [[04 Evidence/Power Output and Tissue Effect]].
