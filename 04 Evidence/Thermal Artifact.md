# Thermal Artifact

**PLACEHOLDER — NEEDS EVIDENCE.** Created because relevant vault background evidence already exists ([[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]), but no source specifically measures thermal artifact in a dental diode *biopsy* specimen against a calibrated/verified power value.

## Claim

- Claim: NEEDS EVIDENCE. Whether — and how much — diode laser thermal artifact compromises a dental oral soft-tissue *biopsy* specimen specifically (as opposed to a non-diagnostic ex vivo/in vitro tissue block) is not established by any source currently in this vault.
- Scope: Thermally induced histologic artifact (coagulation, carbonization, tissue splitting) at the margin of a laser-excised oral soft-tissue specimen.

## Supporting findings

- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. found statistically significant differences between the 1470 nm and 810 nm groups for thermal damage and necrosis in periodontal-pocket tissue, and between the 1064 nm group and others for lost-tissue area in oral-mucosa tissue, across six wavelengths (445–1470 nm) and 0.5–2.0 W. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Romanos et al. found the 445 nm wavelength produced significantly higher histologic penetration depth of thermal effect than 970 nm, and that pulsed mode produced less thermal penetration depth than continuous-wave mode at the same 2 W setting, in ex vivo bovine gingiva. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Al-Ani et al. found the highest mean temperature rise with a 2.5 W diode laser setting and the lowest with a 2.5 W Er,Cr:YSGG setting, across 50 incisions in ex vivo sheep tongue. [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (abstract-only, unprocessed candidates — see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]] for full list): Azevedo et al. quantified "Tissue Damage Extension" and found a significant correlation with "Degree of Carbonization" (r = 0.789, p = 0.01) across laser types including a diode laser, in ex vivo porcine tongue (PMID 27703606, DOI 10.4317/jced.52830). Suter et al. found the diode laser's thermal damage zone was significantly larger than either CO2 laser setting tested, in a pilot RCT of excisional oral mucosal biopsies (PMID 21038754).

## Conflicting findings

- None identified between processed vault sources — Gutiérrez-Corrales et al., Romanos et al., and Al-Ani et al. each vary a different independent variable (wavelength/power, tip status, laser type/power) and do not directly contradict one another; each documents that thermal effect changes with the variable it manipulates.
- Not yet assessable: whether the unprocessed candidates above (Azevedo et al., Suter et al.) agree or disagree with the processed vault sources' magnitudes, since neither has been extracted into a vault Data note.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: Across the processed vault sources, thermal tissue effect in ex vivo/in vitro oral or oral-adjacent models is consistently reported to vary with wavelength, power/mode, and tip condition — but none of these sources is a diagnostic biopsy specimen, and none reports whether the observed thermal effect actually impaired or would impair histopathologic diagnosis. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]] [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]] [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]]
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN
- Limitations: All three processed sources use ex vivo/in vitro animal tissue, not human oral biopsy specimens submitted for diagnosis; none reports pathologist-assessed diagnostic yield.
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION — the unprocessed candidates (Azevedo et al., Suter et al., and others listed in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]) have not been confirmed against full text.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable.
- Research gaps: A dental-diode biopsy study is needed that reports both a thermal-artifact measurement (zone width, carbonization score) and a pathologist-assessed diagnostic outcome in the same human specimen set.
