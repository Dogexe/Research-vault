# Thermal Artifact

**Status: PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** Multiple processed vault sources measure dental-diode thermal damage or thermal artifact; Gambino et al. additionally pairs quantified thermal alteration with a diagnostic outcome in the same specimens.

**Role (updated 2026-09-16): THERMAL-DAMAGE OUTCOME.** Thermal artifact is a primary outcome parallel to, and distinct from, biopsy-oriented specimen quality (see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy#5. Primary outcomes]]). It may affect margin readability, tissue architecture, or specimen interpretability, but it is not equivalent to any of them or to diagnostic failure. Angiero/Gambino's diagnostic-outcome pairing is CLINICAL RELEVANCE context, not this project's own experimental design.

## Claim

- Claim: Dental diode lasers (808–980 nm) produce a measurable thermal-artifact zone at the incision margin. In the Angiero, Azevedo, and Gambino datasets summarized here, reported dimensions are on the order of 150–630 μm; this range is not generalized to all processed sources. Extent does not correlate simply with carbonization severity within one device's own settings even though it does across instrument types pooled. One processed source (Gambino et al.) shows this artifact can be statistically significant relative to untreated tissue while still not impairing diagnosis.
- Scope: Thermally induced histologic artifact (thermal damage zone, coagulation, carbonization, tissue splitting) at the margin of a laser-incised oral soft-tissue specimen.

## Supporting findings

- FACT: Angiero et al. 2011 (n=608, 808 nm diode, 1.6–2.7 W CW, DMT Milan) measured thermal-damage width of 260.7–321.4 μm, mean 282.8 μm, on H&E sections at 100× magnification. Connective-tissue descriptors included carbonization and desiccation; vascular descriptors included intraluminal clotted erythrocytes, vascular stasis, and thrombosed/collapsed vessels. [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]
- FACT: Azevedo et al. 2016 (980 nm diode, ex vivo porcine tongue) measured ETTD of 456.15 μm at 3.5 W PW and 626.82 μm at 3.5 W Boost PW. The diode's own numeric carbonization score was *lower* at the higher-ETTD Boost setting (1.80 vs. 2.50, 0–4 scale) — the pooled cross-instrument ETTD–carbonization correlation (r=0.789, p=0.01) does not hold as a simple within-device pattern for the diode's two settings. Diode incisions were irregular in 80% of cases at both settings. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]
- FACT: Gambino et al. 2026 (n=30, 980 nm diode 2 W CW, standardized 5 mm margin) measured mean thermal damage of 288.9 μm (epithelium) and 430.3 μm (connective tissue) histologically, cross-validated against ex vivo OCT (88.5% concordance, ICC 0.88). Laser-treated tissue was statistically significantly thicker than healthy scalpel-control tissue for both epithelium (p=0.021) and lamina propria (p=0.034) — a real, measurable, significant thermal artifact — yet diagnosis was unimpaired in all 30/30 specimens. This is the vault's first source pairing a quantified thermal-artifact measurement with a diagnostic outcome and a true untreated control group, in the same specimen set. [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- FACT (reused from [[04 Evidence/Tip Initiation and Fiber Condition - Supporting Evidence]]): Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. found statistically significant differences between the 1470 nm and 810 nm groups for thermal damage and necrosis in periodontal-pocket tissue, and between the 1064 nm group and others for lost-tissue area in oral-mucosa tissue, across six wavelengths (445–1470 nm) and 0.5–2.0 W. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]]
- FACT (reused): Romanos et al. found the 445 nm wavelength produced significantly higher histologic penetration depth of thermal effect than 970 nm, and that pulsed mode produced less thermal penetration depth than continuous-wave mode at the same 2 W setting, in ex vivo bovine gingiva. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]]
- FACT (reused): Al-Ani et al. found the highest mean temperature rise with a 2.5 W diode laser setting and the lowest with a 2.5 W Er,Cr:YSGG setting, across 50 incisions in ex vivo sheep tongue. [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]]
- FACT (technical precedent, not biopsy-oriented specimen quality): Prado et al. independently measured delivered power numerically and paired it with histologic thermal-damage area/depth in ex vivo pig tongue, but reported no incision speed. Hanke et al. and Strakas et al. independently verified delivered power before each incision, held speed mechanized at 2 mm/s, and quantified histologic thermal interaction in porcine gingiva. Goharkhay et al. reported measured output at the fiber outflow, mechanized speed at 10 mm/s, and histologic collateral thermal-damage dimensions in ex vivo oral tissue. Together these studies establish measured-power + thermal-histology precedent without providing biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcomes. [[07 Data/Prado et al 2022 - Micro vs Super Pulsed Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data]] [[07 Data/Hanke et al 2021 - Eight-Wavelength Diode Cut Efficiency Data]] [[07 Data/Strakas et al 2023 - 940nm Diode Cutting Efficiency and Thermal Damage Data]] [[07 Data/Goharkhay et al 1999 - Diode Laser Incision Depth and Collateral Damage Data]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (abstract-level extraction; full text unavailable — see [[07 Data/Suter et al 2010 - CO2 vs Diode Laser Biopsy Thermal Damage Data (Abstract-Level)]]): Suter et al. found the diode laser's thermal damage zone was significantly larger than either CO2 laser setting tested, in a pilot RCT of excisional oral mucosal biopsies (PMID 21038754).
- SUPPORTING (animal model, not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway): Li et al. 2022 (diode 3.0 W pulsed, 300 μm fiber, mouse tongue dorsum wound model) found the diode laser produced an "obvious carbonization layer" and deeper, more numerous TUNEL-positive apoptotic cells than an Er:YAG comparator, with significantly slower wound healing (P<0.05) — consistent in direction with Al-Ani et al.'s finding (above) that a diode setting produced more thermal effect than a non-diode comparator. [[07 Data/Li et al 2022 - Diode vs Er-YAG vs Cold Scalpel Mice Oral Mucosa Data]]
- SUPPORTING (n=1 case report, genuine outlier, not smoothed into the CORE-tier range below): Capodiferro et al. 2009 (880 nm diode, 2.5 W CW, 300 μm fiber, real human excisional biopsy) found *no* measurable thermal alteration at the cut margin on either conventional H&E or confocal autofluorescence microscopy — the only source in this vault's biopsy literature reporting a complete absence of margin thermal artifact. Single case; cannot be generalized. [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2009 - Diode Laser Chondroid Lipoma Case Report Data]]
- NEEDS VERIFICATION (abstract-only; now has a dedicated abstract-level data extraction — see [[07 Data/Capodiferro et al 2008 - Oral Laser Surgical Pathology Abstract-Level Data]]): Capodiferro et al. 2008 (n=25, diode 2–7 W) reports protein coagulation "present only with high power density output" — still the vault's only identified coagulation-zone candidate, still unresolved at abstract level, and not answered by the separate, non-overlapping Capodiferro et al. 2009 case report above.

## Conflicting findings

- None identified between processed vault sources — Angiero et al., Azevedo et al., Gambino et al., Gutiérrez-Corrales et al., Romanos et al., and Al-Ani et al. each vary a different independent variable and do not directly contradict one another.
- Noted internal nuance, not a cross-source conflict: within Azevedo et al.'s own diode arm, higher ETTD (Boost setting) paired with *lower* carbonization score — this is a within-source finding, preserved as-is rather than smoothed into the source's own pooled, cross-instrument correlation.

## Assessment

- INTERPRETATION: Across the processed vault sources, thermal tissue effect at a diode-laser incision margin is real and measurable. In the Angiero, Azevedo, and Gambino datasets summarized here it is sub-millimeter (approximately 150–630 μm), and Gambino et al. shows statistically significant thermal alteration relative to untreated tissue without diagnostic failure in that series. This does not establish a general size threshold or make thermal-damage magnitude a surrogate for specimen quality. Carbonization degree tracks thermal-damage extent across instrument types in general but not cleanly within one device's own power/mode settings (Azevedo et al.). [[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]] [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]] [[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]
- HYPOTHESIS: UNKNOWN
- Limitations: Azevedo et al. is ex vivo porcine tissue, not a diagnostic-biopsy pathway. Angiero et al. and Gambino et al. are real human diagnostic-biopsy pathways but neither varies power as an independent variable (Angiero et al. reports a range across the series without per-case attribution; Gambino et al. used one fixed setting).
- Uncertainty: NEEDS VERIFICATION — Suter et al. (and other candidates listed in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]) have not been confirmed against full text.
- Possible explanations for disagreement: Not applicable.
- Research gaps: Measured-power + thermal-histology precedent exists in Prado, Hanke, Strakas, and Goharkhay. The narrower unresolved integration gap is an ex vivo oral diode-laser design combining independently measured delivered power, explicit speed/movement characterization, and biopsy-oriented specimen-quality outcomes (margin readability, tissue architecture preservation, specimen interpretability) while assessing thermal damage separately. No current source establishes that reducing thermal damage necessarily improves any of those specimen-quality outcomes. ## สรุปภาษาไทย

- บันทึกนี้เป็น **หลักฐานบางส่วน (PARTIAL EVIDENCE)** สำหรับผลลัพธ์ความเสียหายจากความร้อน โดย Angiero, Azevedo และ Gambino วัดขนาดรอยความร้อนโดยตรง และมี Prado, Hanke, Strakas และ Goharkhay เป็นหลักฐานเชิงเทคนิคเรื่องกำลังที่วัด/ตรวจสอบได้ร่วมกับผลจุลพยาธิวิทยาจากความร้อน
- Angiero et al. วัดความกว้างความเสียหาย 260.7–321.4 ไมโครเมตร (เฉลี่ย 282.8)
- Azevedo et al. พบว่าค่า ETTD สูงขึ้นที่การตั้งค่า Boost (626.82 μm) แต่คะแนนการไหม้เป็นถ่านกลับต่ำกว่า (1.80) เมื่อเทียบกับค่าตั้งมาตรฐาน (456.15 μm, คะแนน 2.50) — แสดงว่าความสัมพันธ์ระหว่างค่าทั้งสองไม่เป็นเส้นตรงภายในอุปกรณ์เดียวกัน
- Gambino et al. พบว่าเนื้อเยื่อที่ผ่านเลเซอร์หนากว่ากลุ่มควบคุมอย่างมีนัยสำคัญทางสถิติ แต่ไม่กระทบต่อการวินิจฉัย — เป็นแหล่งข้อมูลแรกในคลังนี้ที่มีทั้งการวัดสิ่งแปลกปลอมจากความร้อนและผลการวินิจฉัยในชิ้นเนื้อชุดเดียวกัน พร้อมกลุ่มควบคุมที่แท้จริง
- Prado, Hanke, Strakas และ Goharkhay เป็นหลักฐานเดิมของการวัด/ตรวจสอบกำลังขาออกจริงร่วมกับผลทางจุลพยาธิวิทยาจากความร้อน แต่ยังไม่มีกรอบผลลัพธ์คุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแบบ biopsy-oriented ครบถ้วน
- ช่องว่างที่ยังเหลือคือการรวมกำลังขาออกที่วัดได้จริง การระบุ/ควบคุมความเร็ว การประเมินความเสียหายจากความร้อน และคุณภาพชิ้นเนื้อแบบ biopsy-oriented โดยแยกผลลัพธ์ทั้งสองออกจากกันในงาน ex vivo ช่องปากเดียวกัน
- เพิ่มเติม (อัปเดตนี้): Li et al. 2022 (แบบจำลองในหนู ไม่ใช่ biopsy จริง) พบว่าเลเซอร์ไดโอดทำให้เกิดชั้นไหม้เป็นถ่านที่ชัดเจนและเซลล์ตายลึกกว่าเลเซอร์ Er:YAG; Capodiferro et al. 2009 (รายงานผู้ป่วย 1 ราย) พบว่าไม่มีร่องรอยความเสียหายจากความร้อนที่ขอบตัดเลยทั้งด้วยกล้องจุลทรรศน์ธรรมดาและ confocal — เป็นข้อมูลเฉพาะกรณีเดียว ไม่สามารถสรุปเป็นภาพรวมได้ Capodiferro et al. 2008 (บทคัดย่อเท่านั้น) ยังคงเป็นแหล่งเดียวที่กล่าวถึง coagulation zone แต่ยังไม่มีฉบับเต็ม
