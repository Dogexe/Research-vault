# Diagnostic Quality

## Definition

Whether an excised specimen retains enough architecturally intact tissue for a pathologist to render a confident histopathological diagnosis, as opposed to producing an indeterminate or non-diagnostic result due to thermal or processing artifact. According to PubMed, Angiero et al. report diagnosis was "not achievable" in 46.15% of diode-laser-excised specimens smaller than 3 mm, versus reliable diagnosis in larger specimens, directly tying specimen size and thermal damage to diagnostic yield (Angiero F, Parma L, Crippa R, Benedicenti S. *Lasers Med Sci.* 2011;27(2):383-8. DOI: [10.1007/s10103-011-0900-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-011-0900-7), PMID: 21387158). Gambino et al. report that, despite measurable laser-induced thermal alteration (mean 288.9 μm epithelial, 430.3 μm connective tissue) at 980 nm diode-laser excision margins, "laser-induced thermal effects did not impair definitive histopathological diagnosis in any specimen" in their series (Gambino A, Magliano A, Haddad GE, Bezzi M, Cafaro A, Karimi D, Broccoletti R, Arduino PG. "Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) Evaluation of Thermal Tissue Alterations After Diode Laser Excision of Oral Leukoplakia (OL)." *Dent J (Basel).* 2026;14(3):168. DOI: [10.3390/dj14030168](https://doi.org/10.3390/dj14030168), PMID: 41892776).

## นิยามภาษาไทย

Diagnostic quality (คุณภาพในการวินิจฉัย) คือความสามารถของชิ้นเนื้อที่ตัดออกมาในการคงเนื้อเยื่อที่มีโครงสร้างสมบูรณ์เพียงพอให้พยาธิแพทย์วินิจฉัยได้อย่างมั่นใจ ตรงข้ามกับผลที่ไม่สามารถวินิจฉัยได้ (indeterminate/non-diagnostic) เนื่องจากสิ่งแปลกปลอมจากความร้อนหรือกระบวนการเตรียมชิ้นเนื้อ

## In this vault

Three sources are now processed with a real diagnostic-yield outcome. Angiero et al. 2011, now full-text-processed ([[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]), gives a size-graded picture rather than a single threshold: 63.63% of 2 mm specimens were unreadable, 37.71% of 3 mm specimens were unreadable, and specimens ≥4 mm were reliably diagnosable in 100% of cases (n=608 total). Romeo et al. 2014 ([[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]): a certainty diagnosis was achieved in 17/17 (100%) diode/KTP-excised specimens (lesion size 0.5–1 cm, i.e., 5–10 mm — well above Angiero et al.'s failure-prone range). Gambino et al. 2026, now processed ([[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]): definitive diagnosis was obtained in all 30/30 (100%) specimens excised with a standardized 5 mm margin, despite statistically significant epithelial/lamina propria thickening relative to healthy control tissue (p=0.021/0.034) — i.e., a real, measurable, statistically significant thermal alteration was present but did not impair diagnosis. All three processed sources are mutually consistent with specimen size (or margin) relative to thermal-alteration depth being the operative variable, not a contradiction between them. NEEDS EVIDENCE remains for linking a calibrated/measured (as opposed to set) dental diode power value directly to diagnostic-yield outcome.

## Common confusion

Diagnostic quality is an outcome (can the pathologist diagnose it), distinct from [[Thermal Damage]] or [[Histological Artifact]], which are mechanisms/findings that may or may not be severe enough to compromise diagnostic quality in a given specimen.

**Diagnostic quality vs. specimen adequacy.** This vault does not treat "diagnostic quality" (whether artifact/thermal alteration compromises interpretability) and "specimen adequacy" (whether enough tissue was excised at all) as the same question, even though the same source can address both. Angiero et al. 2011's central finding is size-graded adequacy: 63.63% of 2 mm specimens were unreadable, 37.71% of 3 mm, and specimens ≥4 mm were 100% diagnosable — a finding about sufficient tissue *amount*, not about artifact severity at a fixed size. This vault does not create a separate "Specimen Adequacy" canonical note for this distinction, since no source currently scores adequacy independently of diagnosability itself, but the two questions should not be collapsed when reading a source's stated recommendation (see [[Specimen Margin]] for the size thresholds themselves).
