# Fluence

## Definition

Radiant exposure — the total optical energy delivered per unit area over the full exposure/treatment time, also called energy density in much of the clinical laser literature. Fluence is a time-integrated, energy-based quantity (J/cm²), distinct from irradiance, which is an instantaneous, power-based quantity. Jenkins PA, Carroll JD ("How to report low-level laser therapy (LLLT)/photomedicine dose and beam parameters in clinical and laboratory studies." *Photomed Laser Surg.* 2011;29(12):785-7. DOI: [10.1089/pho.2011.9895](https://doi.org/10.1089/pho.2011.9895)) identify "energy density" as one of the three commonly used dose parameters (alongside time and energy) and stress it must be reported alongside beam area for reproducibility.

## Formula

Fluence (H) = Energy (Q) / Area (A) = Irradiance (E) × Exposure Time (t), for constant irradiance over the exposure.

## Unit

J/cm² (also kJ/cm² for higher-dose dental procedures).

## Common confusion

Do not confuse fluence/energy density (J/cm², total dose) with irradiance/power density (W/cm², instantaneous rate) — the two are related by exposure time (see Formula), but are not interchangeable, and a source reporting only one cannot be assumed to imply a specific value for the other without also knowing exposure duration. This vault deliberately keeps them as separate concepts, even where a single source (e.g., Kim et al.) reports both under source-specific labels ("power density," "energy density").

## Source basis

**Classification:** STANDARD TERM

The underlying radiometric quantity is standardized as "radiant exposure" (J/cm²) in ANSI Z136.1 (American National Standard for Safe Use of Lasers) and ISO 11145. "Fluence" and "energy density" are the equivalent terms used for the same quantity in clinical/PBM dosimetry literature (Jenkins & Carroll 2011, cited above, lists "energy density" among the essential dose parameters to report).
