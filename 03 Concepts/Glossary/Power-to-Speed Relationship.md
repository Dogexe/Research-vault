# Power-to-Speed Relationship

## Definition
The conceptual pairing of delivered power and incision speed as two variables that must be interpreted jointly, not independently, when comparing or predicting a laser's tissue effect — since the energy deposited per unit tissue length depends on both together (see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Energy per Unit Length]]).

## Unit / equation
Not applicable directly; see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Energy per Unit Length]] for the associated relationship E/L ≈ P/v.

## In this project
This vault treats both delivered power and incision speed as independently tracked, first-class experimental variables (per [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]] "Key variables") rather than assuming one can substitute for interpretation of the other. No source in this vault varies incision speed as an independently manipulated experimental variable against a specimen-quality outcome — every numeric speed value found is a single fixed protocol value (10, 2, 1, 0.75 mm/s across different sources), not a tested variable — this is documented as a stated research gap in [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]] "Main research gaps."

## Common pitfalls
Do not assume that comparing power alone (or speed alone) across studies is meaningful without checking the other — two studies at the same power but very different speeds represent very different local energy exposures, and vice versa.
