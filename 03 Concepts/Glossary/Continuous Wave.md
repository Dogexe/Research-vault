# Continuous Wave

## Definition

An operating mode in which the laser emits a constant, uninterrupted beam of light for the duration of activation (commonly abbreviated CW), as opposed to a pulsed/chopped emission pattern. In CW mode, average power, peak power, and instantaneous power are all equal, since there is no on/off cycling.

## นิยามภาษาไทย

โหมดต่อเนื่อง (Continuous Wave, CW) คือโหมดการทำงานที่เลเซอร์ปล่อยแสงออกมาอย่างต่อเนื่องไม่มีการขาดช่วงตลอดเวลาที่เปิดใช้งาน ตรงข้ามกับโหมดพัลส์ ในโหมด CW กำลังเฉลี่ย กำลังสูงสุด และกำลังขณะนั้นมีค่าเท่ากันทั้งหมด เนื่องจากไม่มีรอบเปิด-ปิด

## In this vault

Most of the vault's core dental sources use CW mode for at least part of their protocol: [[07 Data/Zegaib et al 2011 - Fiber Tip Power Loss During Periodontal Curettage]] (810 nm, fully CW), [[07 Data/Kim et al 2020 - Set vs Actual Power Across Three Dental Diode Lasers]] (all three devices, CW only), [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters]] (all six wavelengths, CW only), and the diode arm of [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]] (1.5/2.5 W, CW). [[07 Data/Xue et al 2022 - Two Semiconductor Lasers Power Output]] and [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2022 - Initiated vs Non-Initiated Diode Laser Penetration Depth]] test both CW and pulsed modes on the same devices, allowing within-study CW-vs-pulsed comparison.

## Common confusion

Do not assume "power" reported for a CW source is directly comparable in magnitude to "power" reported for a pulsed source without checking whether the pulsed value is peak, average, or per-pulse — see [[Average Power]] and [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Peak Power]].

## Source basis

**Classification:** STANDARD TERM

Continuous wave (CW) is defined in ANSI Z136.1 (American National Standard for Safe Use of Lasers) as the output of a laser operated in a continuous rather than a pulsed mode, and is likewise a defined laser operating-mode term in ISO 11145 (*Optics and photonics — Lasers and laser-related equipment — Vocabulary and symbols*).
