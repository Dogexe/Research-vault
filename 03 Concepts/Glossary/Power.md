# Power

## Definition
Power is the rate of energy transfer or delivery, in watts (1 W = 1 J/s). It is a rate quantity — it does not by itself state how much total energy was delivered without also knowing the exposure time.

## Unit / equation
W; P = E / t.

## In this project
This project's experimental chain begins with power (set, then independently measured) rather than energy, because the device/clinical literature almost universally reports console values in watts, not joules. Distinguishing power (a rate) from energy (a total) matters because a given power can deliver very different total energy depending on incision speed/exposure time — see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Energy]], [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Energy per Unit Length]], [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Incision Speed]].

## Common pitfalls
Power (W) is not energy (J). A higher power for a shorter exposure can deliver less total energy than a lower power for longer. Do not equate "more power" with "more total energy delivered" without also knowing time.
