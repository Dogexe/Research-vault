# Chromophore

## Definition
A molecule or tissue component that preferentially absorbs light at specific wavelengths — in oral soft tissue, the principal chromophores relevant to diode-laser wavelengths are hemoglobin, melanin, and water.

## Unit / equation
Not applicable.

## In this project
Chromophore content explains why wavelength selection matters (wavelengths near hemoglobin/melanin absorption peaks couple more efficiently into non-initiated tissue) and why an initiated (pre-charred) fiber tip is sometimes used specifically to bypass reliance on native tissue chromophore absorption (see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip]]).

## Common pitfalls
Do not assume all oral soft tissue has uniform chromophore content — pigmentation, vascularity, and hydration vary by site and specimen, which this vault's sources do not control for unless explicitly stated.
