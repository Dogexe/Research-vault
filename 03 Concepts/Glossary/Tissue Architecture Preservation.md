# Tissue Architecture Preservation

## Definition
The degree to which a specimen's normal histologic structure (epithelial layering, connective-tissue organization, cellular detail) remains intact and interpretable after laser excision, as opposed to being obscured or distorted by thermal or mechanical alteration.

## Unit / equation
Not applicable — the schema `tissue_architecture` field is free text.

## In this project
Tissue architecture preservation is one of three components (alongside thermal artifact and margin readability) this project treats as composing overall histopathologic specimen quality — see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Biopsy Specimen Quality]] and [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]] "Primary experimental outcomes." No source in this vault currently pairs an independently measured delivered power with a formal tissue-architecture-preservation scoring instrument in a biopsy-oriented ex vivo design — this is part of the project's stated remaining gap.

## Common pitfalls
Do not assume tissue architecture preservation is simply the inverse of thermal damage width — architecture can be regionally distorted (e.g. splitting, compression) by mechanical rather than thermal effects, which a pure thermal-damage-width measurement would not capture.
