# Hot-tip Effect

## Definition
The mechanism by which a standard (non-initiated or intermittently initiated) diode fiber tip carbonizes tissue protein at the point of contact during use, and that carbonized deposit — not the laser light itself — becomes the actual absorber that heats the tip and drives thermo-mechanical cutting. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2013 - Diode Laser Soft-Tissue Surgery Review Data|Romanos et al. 2013]] states this explicitly: standard dental diode lasers (typically 2–7 W) cut far below the ≈25 W the source calculates would be needed for direct light-absorption cutting with a 400 μm tip, and instead rely on this tip-carbonization mechanism, which is continuously scraped away and re-forms during cutting — producing fluctuating cutting/coagulation power. This differs from deliberate [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip|tip initiation]] only in that initiation pre-forms this same carbonized layer intentionally, before use, rather than relying on it forming unpredictably during the incision itself.

## Unit / equation
Not applicable — a mechanistic description, not a measured quantity.

## In this project
[[07 Data/Prado et al 2022 - Micro vs Super Pulsed Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Prado et al. 2022]] separately invokes this same mechanism to explain carbonized build-up affecting penetration depth, without scoring it numerically. [[07 Data/Romanos et al 2013 - Diode Laser Soft-Tissue Surgery Review Data|Romanos et al. 2013]] links this mechanism directly to incision speed: at low cutting speed, the source states collateral damage "can exceed 1 mm" due to "a snag or drag" — its own terms for the tip catching/dragging as the carbonized layer builds and is scraped away. This mechanism helps explain why an uninitiated tip's cutting behavior can be inconsistent, and why some protocols deliberately pre-initiate the tip instead (see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip]]).

## Common pitfalls
Do not confuse this fiber-tip mechanism with [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Carbonization]] at the tissue specimen margin — the hot-tip effect describes carbonization occurring at and driving cutting from the fiber tip itself, not the (separate, unwanted) carbonization sometimes found in the excised specimen. Do not confuse the hot-tip effect (an uncontrolled, use-dependent carbonization process) with [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip|tip initiation]] (a deliberate, pre-use preparation step) — the two produce a similar carbonized layer by different routes.
