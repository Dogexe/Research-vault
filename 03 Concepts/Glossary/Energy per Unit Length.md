# Energy per Unit Length

## Definition
A conceptual quantity relating delivered power and incision speed along a moving incision: E/L ≈ P / v, where P is delivered power and v is incision speed. It approximates the energy deposited per unit length of tissue cut, not the total energy delivered to the whole specimen.

## Unit / equation
J/mm (or J/cm). E/L ≈ P / v — this assumes approximately constant delivered power and advancement speed over the incision; it is a conceptual relationship, not a general law.

## In this project
This relationship is a conceptual aid for reasoning about why power and incision speed must be considered jointly, not separately, when comparing studies' tissue effects — a high power at high speed can deposit similar energy-per-length as a low power at low speed. It is explicitly NOT a value this vault calculates for any actual study: doing so would require both an independently measured power and a source-reported speed for the same incision, and per this vault's rules this must not be retroactively calculated for a study unless the source itself reports the necessary values and the project specifically decides to perform such a derivation.

## Common pitfalls
Energy per unit length is not the same as total delivered energy (which also depends on total incision length) — it is a local, per-unit-length rate. Never retroactively calculate this value for a vault study from separately reported power and speed figures without an explicit, documented decision to do so.
