# Absorption

## Definition
The process by which tissue converts incident optical energy into another form (primarily heat), determined by the tissue's chromophore content and the laser's wavelength. Absorption is the dominant mechanism by which laser energy produces a thermal tissue effect.

## Unit / equation
Not applicable — described by a wavelength-dependent absorption coefficient (μ_a) in tissue optics; not computed in this vault.

## In this project
Absorption is why wavelength does not determine tissue effect by itself — the same wavelength can be strongly or weakly absorbed depending on tissue chromophore content (blood, melanin, water), and effect also depends on delivered power, exposure time, and geometry (see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Beam Delivery Geometry]]). This vault does not model or calculate absorption coefficients for any study.

## Common pitfalls
Do not assume higher absorption always means "more cutting effect" — strong absorption produces effect at a shallow depth (surface heating), while weak absorption allows deeper penetration before energy is deposited; both can be clinically relevant depending on intent.
