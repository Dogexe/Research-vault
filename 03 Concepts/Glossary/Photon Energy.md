# Photon Energy

## Definition
The energy carried by a single photon, E_photon = hν = hc/λ, where h is Planck's constant. Shorter wavelengths carry higher photon energy per photon.

## Unit / equation
J (or eV); E_photon = hν = hc/λ.

## In this project
Photon energy underlies why different diode wavelengths interact differently with the same tissue chromophore (see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Absorption]], [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Chromophore]]) — but this vault does not calculate photon energy for any study; it is stated here as background physics to help interpret why wavelength-dependent absorption differences are physically expected, not to justify any numeric derivation.

## Common pitfalls
A higher photon energy does not mean higher total delivered energy — total energy also depends on the number of photons, i.e. on power and exposure time (see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Power]], [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Energy]]).
