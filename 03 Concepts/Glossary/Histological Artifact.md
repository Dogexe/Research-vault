# Histological Artifact

## Definition

An unintended structural or staining alteration introduced into a tissue specimen during collection, fixation, processing, or sectioning — rather than a feature of the tissue's actual in vivo biology — that can obscure or mimic diagnostic features under microscopic examination. In laser-excised specimens, thermally induced coagulation, carbonization, and tissue splitting are recognized artifact categories that can be introduced by the excision method itself, in addition to any standard lab-processing artifact. According to PubMed, Romeo et al. note that, alongside thermal signs, "many artifacts were also associated to histologic procedures" in diode/KTP laser oral biopsies (Romeo U, Russo C, Palaia G, Lo Giudice R, Del Vecchio A, Visca P, Migliau G, De Biase A. "Biopsy of different oral soft tissues lesions by KTP and diode laser: histological evaluation." *TheScientificWorldJournal.* 2014;2014:761704. DOI: [10.1155/2014/761704](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/761704), PMID: 25405233).

## นิยามภาษาไทย

Histological artifact (สิ่งแปลกปลอมทางจุลพยาธิวิทยา) คือการเปลี่ยนแปลงโครงสร้างหรือการติดสีของชิ้นเนื้อที่เกิดขึ้นโดยไม่ได้ตั้งใจระหว่างการเก็บ ตรึง เตรียม หรือตัดชิ้นเนื้อ ไม่ใช่ลักษณะทางชีววิทยาจริงของเนื้อเยื่อ ซึ่งอาจบดบังหรือเลียนแบบลักษณะที่ใช้วินิจฉัยได้ภายใต้กล้องจุลทรรศน์ ในชิ้นเนื้อที่ตัดด้วยเลเซอร์ การแข็งตัวของโปรตีนจากความร้อน (coagulation) การไหม้เป็นถ่าน (carbonization) และการฉีกขาดของเนื้อเยื่อ ถือเป็นสิ่งแปลกปลอมที่เกิดจากวิธีการตัดเอง

## In this vault

Romeo et al. 2014 ([[07 Data/Romeo et al 2014 - Diode and KTP Laser Biopsy Margin Data]]): states that many artifacts observed on its 17 diode/KTP-excised specimens were attributable to standard tissue fixation/sectioning/staining procedures rather than to the laser itself — a direct, if qualitative, source-level statement distinguishing processing artifact from laser-induced thermal artifact. Angiero et al. 2011, now full-text-processed ([[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]), independently frames the same distinction in its Introduction, citing prior literature on "laser artifacts and diagnostic biopsy" as an established controversy, and separately reports a genuine controlled comparator: Gambino et al. 2026 ([[07 Data/Gambino et al 2026 - Diode Laser OCT and Histologic Thermal Damage Data]]) is this vault's first source with an actual scalpel-excised control group processed identically to the laser group (15 vs. 15 specimens, same fixation/staining/imaging protocol), against which laser-specific thermal alteration was shown to be statistically significant (not merely qualitative) yet non-diagnosis-impairing. No vault source yet quantifies laser-induced vs. processing-induced artifact as two separately scored variables within one specimen; this remains NEEDS EVIDENCE.

## Common artifact patterns named in this vault's sources

Rather than creating a separate canonical note per named pattern, this vault links specific artifact descriptors back to this one umbrella concept when a source lists them as histologic findings (not as its primary quantified outcome — see the necrosis caveat below). [[07 Data/Pergolini et al 2025 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Laser Ex Vivo Thermal Damage Data|Pergolini et al. 2025]] reports "epithelial detachment, loss of epithelial/subepithelial cellular adhesion, epithelial erosion, epithelial spongiosis, carbonization, vacuolization" as its histologic artifacts. [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] separately lists "tissue necrosis, fibrous degeneration, hyperchromatism, nuclear elongation, intercellular spacing, loss of cellular morphology, intercellular edema, hyalinization, shedding of keratin, vacuolation, epithelium separation from connective tissue." These are preserved as each source's own descriptive vocabulary, not merged into a single controlled list or promoted to their own glossary notes, per this vault's anti-inflation policy.

**Necrosis caveat — a genuine terminology ambiguity, not silently resolved:** [[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]] lists "tissue necrosis" as one item among many qualitative artifact descriptors. [[07 Data/Gutierrez-Corrales et al 2020 - Diode Wavelength Operating Parameters|Gutiérrez-Corrales et al. 2020]], by contrast, reports "necrosis area" as its own separately quantified outcome alongside (not folded into) "thermal damage area" — treating necrosis as a real, distinctly measured biological effect rather than an incidental processing artifact. This vault does not force these two usages into one meaning: whether "necrosis" denotes a scored artifact-list item or an independently quantified tissue-effect variable depends on the specific source, and is preserved as read rather than normalized.

## Common confusion

Distinguish "artifact" (an alteration not present in vivo, introduced by the excision or lab technique) from "thermal damage" (a real biological tissue-effect zone at the margin, also visible histologically) — the two overlap in laser specimens but are not synonymous; a specimen can show real thermal damage without necessarily obscuring diagnosis (see [[Diagnostic Quality]]).
