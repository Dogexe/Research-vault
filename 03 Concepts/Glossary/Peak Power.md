# Peak Power

## Definition

The maximum instantaneous optical power reached during a single pulse, in a pulsed operating mode. For CW operation, peak power equals average/instantaneous power (there is no pulsing). Peak power is typically much greater than average power in pulsed or "super pulsed" modes, since it is not time-averaged over the pulse's off-time.

## นิยามภาษาไทย

กำลังสูงสุด (Peak Power) คือกำลังแสงสูงสุดในขณะหนึ่งระหว่างพัลส์เดียว ในการทำงานแบบพัลส์ สำหรับการทำงานแบบต่อเนื่อง (CW) กำลังสูงสุดเท่ากับกำลังเฉลี่ย/กำลังขณะนั้น กำลังสูงสุดมักสูงกว่ากำลังเฉลี่ยมากในโหมดพัลส์หรือ "super pulsed" เพราะไม่ได้ถูกเฉลี่ยตามเวลาปิดพัลส์

## Formula

P_peak = P_avg / Duty Cycle (ideal periodic rectangular pulse train); also P_peak = Pulse Energy / Pulse Duration.

## Unit

W.

## In this vault

[[07 Data/Mazzoni et al 2026 - Neonatal Frenulum Laser Parameters]] explicitly reports a peak power of 100 W for its "super pulsed" mode, alongside a much lower average/"final" power of 1.1 W — the largest peak-vs-average gap recorded in this vault, illustrating why the two must never be reported or compared interchangeably. [[07 Data/Al-Ani et al 2023 - Dual-Wavelength Diode Operating Parameters]]'s Er,Cr:YSGG comparator (not a diode laser, but reported in the same extraction) also separately reports peak power (71.43 W and 100 W) distinct from its average/set power (2.5 W and 3.5 W) for its pulsed-wave mode.

## Common confusion

Do not read a reported "peak power" as the clinically relevant average dose, or vice versa — Jenkins & Carroll 2011 (DOI: [10.1089/pho.2011.9895](https://doi.org/10.1089/pho.2011.9895)) stress reporting pulse parameters (including peak power) separately from average power for reproducibility.

## Source basis

**Classification:** STANDARD TERM

Peak power is a standard pulsed-laser radiometric quantity defined in ISO 11145 (*Optics and photonics — Lasers and laser-related equipment — Vocabulary and symbols*), and is used in ANSI Z136.1 pulsed-laser hazard classification. Clinical/PBM dosimetry reporting guidance (Jenkins & Carroll 2011, cited above) also treats peak power as an essential, separately-reportable beam parameter.
