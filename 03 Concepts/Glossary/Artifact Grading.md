# Artifact Grading

## Definition
An ordinal or categorical scoring system a study applies to rate specimen/slide quality or histologic artifact severity, as opposed to reporting only a continuous quantified measurement (e.g. a μm thermal-damage width) or a purely qualitative narrative description. Grading systems let a study classify specimens into ranked tiers (e.g. good/average/poor/non-diagnostic) rather than, or in addition to, reporting a single continuous number.

## Unit / equation
Not applicable — an ordinal scale, study-specific and not standardized across sources.

## In this project
[[07 Data/Gundlapalle et al 2022 - Diode Laser CW vs PW Biopsy Margin Data|Gundlapalle et al. 2022]]'s pathologist-graded slide-quality scale (1 = good, 2 = average, 3 = poor, 5 = non-diagnostic — the source's own scale skips a "4" grade, preserved as reported) is this vault's strongest existing example, and is already flagged in [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Margin Quality]] as the closest analog to a formal margin-readability instrument. [[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data|Azevedo et al. 2016]]'s 0–4 carbonization score is a second example, applied to a mechanistic (non-biopsy) outcome. No vault source yet applies a grading system to margin readability or tissue-architecture preservation specifically, in an ex vivo, measured-power design — this project's own stated central remaining gap (see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]).

## Common pitfalls
Do not assume different sources' grading scales are comparable just because both use ordinal categories — Gundlapalle et al. 2022's 4-tier slide-quality scale and Azevedo et al. 2016's 0–4 carbonization score measure different things on different scales, and neither is a validated, cross-study instrument. Do not treat the mere presence of a grading system as evidence of formal validation (inter-rater reliability, published criteria) unless the source itself states this.
