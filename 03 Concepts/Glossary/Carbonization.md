# Carbonization

## Definition

Visible charring (blackening) of tissue at or near a laser incision, produced when local temperature rise is severe enough to pyrolyze organic tissue rather than merely coagulate it; treated in the laser-surgery literature as a more severe thermal effect than coagulation alone, and associated with greater peripheral tissue damage. According to PubMed, Azevedo et al. found a statistically significant association between "Tissue Damage Extension" and "Degree of Carbonization" (r = 0.789, p = 0.01) across several laser types including a diode laser tested on porcine tongue mucosa (Azevedo AS, Monteiro LS, Ferreira F, Delgado ML, Garcês F, Carreira S, Martins M, Suarez-Quintanilla J. "Histological evaluation of the surgical margins made by different laser wavelengths in tongue tissues." *J Clin Exp Dent.* 2016;8(4):e388-e396. DOI: [10.4317/jced.52830](https://doi.org/10.4317/jced.52830), PMID: 27703606).

## นิยามภาษาไทย

Carbonization (การไหม้เป็นถ่าน) คือการไหม้ดำที่มองเห็นได้ของเนื้อเยื่อบริเวณรอยตัดเลเซอร์ เกิดขึ้นเมื่ออุณหภูมิเฉพาะที่สูงพอจะสลายเนื้อเยื่ออินทรีย์ (pyrolysis) แทนที่จะเพียงแค่ทำให้โปรตีนแข็งตัว ถือเป็นผลกระทบจากความร้อนที่รุนแรงกว่าการแข็งตัวของโปรตีนเพียงอย่างเดียว

## In this vault

Azevedo et al. 2016 is now processed with full text, including its actual carbonization-score table ([[07 Data/Azevedo et al 2016 - Diode Laser Thermal Damage and Carbonization Data]]): across all instruments tested (including a 980 nm diode), the study found a significant correlation between thermal-damage extent (ETTD) and macroscopic carbonization degree (r = 0.789, p = 0.01). For the diode arm specifically, the numeric carbonization score was 2.50 (0–4 scale) at 3.5 W PW and 1.80 at 3.5 W Boost PW — notably *lower* at the setting that produced *more* ETTD (626.82 μm vs. 456.15 μm), showing the pooled cross-instrument correlation does not hold as a simple within-device pattern across the diode's own two settings. Angiero et al. 2011 also reports carbonization qualitatively, listing it among the connective-tissue thermal-change descriptors observed (alongside desiccation), without a numeric score ([[07 Data/Angiero et al 2011 - Diode Laser Biopsy Specimen Size and Diagnostic Yield]]). NEEDS EVIDENCE remains for a dental-diode source pairing measured/delivered (calibrated) power with a carbonization score at the specimen margin. A related but distinct vault concept is fiber-tip [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip]] preparation, which deliberately produces a carbonized layer at the *fiber tip itself* (not the specimen) to increase absorption — the two uses of "carbonization" (tip preparation vs. specimen artifact) should not be conflated.

## Common confusion

Do not confuse tip carbonization (a deliberate pre-treatment step applied to the fiber, see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip]]) with specimen/margin carbonization (an unwanted histologic finding in the excised tissue) — this vault treats them as distinct phenomena occurring at different locations (fiber tip vs. tissue margin).
