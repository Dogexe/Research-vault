# Carbonization

## Definition

Visible charring (blackening) of tissue at or near a laser incision, produced when local temperature rise is severe enough to pyrolyze organic tissue rather than merely coagulate it; treated in the laser-surgery literature as a more severe thermal effect than coagulation alone, and associated with greater peripheral tissue damage. According to PubMed, Azevedo et al. found a statistically significant association between "Tissue Damage Extension" and "Degree of Carbonization" (r = 0.789, p = 0.01) across several laser types including a diode laser tested on porcine tongue mucosa (Azevedo AS, Monteiro LS, Ferreira F, Delgado ML, Garcês F, Carreira S, Martins M, Suarez-Quintanilla J. "Histological evaluation of the surgical margins made by different laser wavelengths in tongue tissues." *J Clin Exp Dent.* 2016;8(4):e388-e396. DOI: [10.4317/jced.52830](https://doi.org/10.4317/jced.52830), PMID: 27703606).

## นิยามภาษาไทย

Carbonization (การไหม้เป็นถ่าน) คือการไหม้ดำที่มองเห็นได้ของเนื้อเยื่อบริเวณรอยตัดเลเซอร์ เกิดขึ้นเมื่ออุณหภูมิเฉพาะที่สูงพอจะสลายเนื้อเยื่ออินทรีย์ (pyrolysis) แทนที่จะเพียงแค่ทำให้โปรตีนแข็งตัว ถือเป็นผลกระทบจากความร้อนที่รุนแรงกว่าการแข็งตัวของโปรตีนเพียงอย่างเดียว

## In this vault

NEEDS EVIDENCE for a dental-diode source pairing measured/delivered power with a carbonization score at the specimen margin; Azevedo et al. above is an identified, unprocessed candidate (see [[01 Projects/Diode Laser Biopsy]]). A related but distinct vault concept is fiber-tip [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip]] preparation, which deliberately produces a carbonized layer at the *fiber tip itself* (not the specimen) to increase absorption — the two uses of "carbonization" (tip preparation vs. specimen artifact) should not be conflated.

## Common confusion

Do not confuse tip carbonization (a deliberate pre-treatment step applied to the fiber, see [[03 Concepts/Glossary/Initiated Tip]]) with specimen/margin carbonization (an unwanted histologic finding in the excised tissue) — this vault treats them as distinct phenomena occurring at different locations (fiber tip vs. tissue margin).
